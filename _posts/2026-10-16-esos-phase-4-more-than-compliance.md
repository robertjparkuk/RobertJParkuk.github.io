The Energy Savings Opportunity Scheme, ESOS, has a reputation as a four-yearly chore. An assessor visits, a report gets written, it goes in a drawer. Phase 4 is a good moment to break that habit, because the rules are moving towards accountability for what you actually do with the findings.

## Who's in

ESOS applies across the UK, including Northern Ireland, where the regulator is the Northern Ireland Environment Agency. You're a "large undertaking" if your UK business employs 250 or more people, or has turnover above £44 million and a balance sheet above £38 million. If one UK company in a group qualifies, the whole UK group is in.

## The dates that matter

- **5 December 2026:** Phase 3 participants must submit an annual progress update on their action plan, signed off by a board-level director.
- **31 December 2026:** the qualification date for Phase 4. If you meet the thresholds on that day, you're in.
- **5 December 2027:** the Phase 4 compliance deadline. Notification goes through the government's online system, MESOS.

## What's changed

Phase 4 tightens things in a few ways that matter for manufacturers:

- **Action plans now carry weight.** Your assessment has to report progress against previous commitments, and explain where you fell short.
- **Fewer shortcuts.** Display Energy Certificates and Green Deal assessments no longer count as alternative routes to compliance.
- **A net zero option.** Participants can choose to use PAS 51215 standards to frame a decarbonisation assessment alongside the energy audit.

The board-level sign-off on progress updates is the real shift. ESOS is no longer something you can hand to an assessor and forget.

## How I deliver it

I'm not an ESOS Lead Assessor myself, and I'm upfront about that. The final assessment has to be signed off by an assessor from an approved register, so I work with several experienced Lead Assessors depending on timing and workload.

What I add is the wrapping around it. I think ESOS works best delivered alongside two other pieces of work:

- **A carbon inventory.** ESOS covers energy. A carbon inventory covers your wider emissions. Gathering the data once for both saves time and money.
- **A review of your energy contracts.** The audit tells you where energy is used. The contracts tell you what you're paying for it and how exposed you are to price swings. Together they make the savings case far easier to put to a board.

## Start early

Twelve months sounds like plenty of time. It isn't, once you factor in site visits, data gathering across a group and board sign-off. If you think you'll qualify on 31 December, now is a sensible time to start pulling the data together.

Done well, ESOS gives you a costed list of savings, a clear energy picture and evidence that your board is on top of it. That's worth having whether or not the regulator asks.
