*Part of the [Policy series]({{ '/policy-series/' | relative_url }}): where the rules stand in Northern Ireland, who decides them, and what to do about it. Updated as the rules change.*

## The argument in brief

On 15 May 2026, Defra said it would not legislate in 2026 to change the Great Britain HFC phase-down step due on 1 January 2027. It had received 137 consultation responses, said further work was needed, and promised a report on next steps later in the year. As at 28 October 2026 no report had appeared on Defra's consultation page. Northern Ireland follows a different rulebook. Under the Windsor Framework it applies EU Regulation (EU) 2024/573, which bans many new heat pumps using refrigerants with a global warming potential (GWP) of 150 or more from 1 January 2027.

The result is a two-speed market inside one country. GB keeps the 2014 regime and its quota cut. Northern Ireland faces EU product bans, an EU route to zero HFCs by 2050 and, possibly, an EU restriction on PFAS. At the same time NI has the UK's heaviest reliance on heating oil and no heat pump grant. The Budget of 28 October 2026 tilted the sums further towards oil. It removed the 4% kerosene tariff, and from 1 April 2027 VAT on installing heat pumps in homes rises from 0% to 5%. Refrigerant policy and heat policy are being made in separate rooms, and NI manufacturers and households carry the cost of the gap. The pillars compete too: low-GWP fluorinated refrigerants can be PFAS, the natural alternatives are flammable or toxic, and heat pumps cut carbon but can raise costs for the households least able to pay.

*This piece sets out the arguments on each side; the recommendations are the author's. Facts checked to 30 October 2026.*

## The lifecycle at a glance

GB and NI rules part company where refrigerant and equipment are placed on the market. After that, the gas leaks at installation, in use and at scrapping, so recovery and reclamation decide how much ever reaches the air.

- **Subsidy is the real dividing line.** Labour and the Lib Dems would expand grants. Reform would abolish the BUS. The Conservatives question the timetable.

- **F-gas reform is a quiet issue.** No party has campaigned on the 2027 GB step; criticism of Defra's delay came from industry.

## Who decides what

Westminster sets the GB refrigerant rules. Brussels sets the NI refrigerant rules. Stormont controls NI heat subsidies.

## Where the pillars compete

- **E versus S: who pays for the switch.** A grant that covers only part of the cost helps owners who can fund the balance first. NEA NI asks for full grants for low-income homes. The return of 5% VAT on home installs from 1 April 2027 adds to the balance owners must fund.

- **E versus S: efficiency before kit.** Belfast City Council warned in Dec 2024 that heat pumps in poorly insulated homes could use more electricity and deepen fuel poverty.

- **E versus E: low-GWP fluorinated refrigerants can be PFAS.** HFOs such as R1234yf (CF₃CF=CH₂) meet the GWP 150 test. Their fully fluorinated CF₃ group makes them PFAS under the OECD-based definition in the EU proposal. R134a (CF₃CH₂F) is PFAS too. R32 (CH₂F₂) has no fully fluorinated carbon, so it is not, but its GWP of 675 fails the 150 test. A firm that redesigns once for F-gas rules may have to redesign again.

- **E versus E: natural refrigerants still need fluoropolymers.** Compressor makers argue that natural refrigerants raise the need for fluoropolymer seals. ECHA's RAC supports only time-limited exemptions, such as for spare parts.

- **E versus safety.** Propane (R290) is highly flammable, and units need clearance and rated parts in some designs. Without propane in split systems, GB relies on R32, which uses up quota.

## What it means for Northern Ireland firms and councils

- **Two specifications for one product line.** Equipment made in NI for the NI or EU market must meet the 2027 GWP 150 bans; the same unit can still be sold in GB.

- **An opportunity for the compressor cluster.** Copeland's Cookstown plant developed the company's first CO₂ and R290 compressors and won a £19m expansion in 2024 to supply Europe.

- **Separate quota pools.** NI contractors buy HFCs under EU quota; GB contractors face the 24% step. Prices and availability may differ across the Irish Sea.

- **SF6 bans.** Since 1 January 2026, new NI switchgear up to 24 kV may not rely on F-gases such as SF6; 24–52 kV follows in 2030. Council and NI Water capital plans should reflect this now.

- **Councils are the first contact for households.** Without a grant before 2027 at the earliest, councils will field questions on oil, heat pumps and safety, and building control will meet flammable-refrigerant installs.

- **Heat pump installs cost more from April 2027.** VAT on installs in homes and charity buildings rises from 0% to 5% on 1 April 2027. Heat batteries join the relief (Budget, 28 Oct 2026). With no NI grant before 2027 at the earliest, households carry that cost.

- **Installer skills are the bottleneck.** NI needs certified installers ready before any grant opens.

## Practical examples

- **Cookstown builds for the EU rules.** Copeland's Cookstown plant developed the company's first transcritical CO₂ and R290 propane compressors. Its £19m expansion was aimed at Europe's heat pump demand ([Cooling Post](https://www.coolingpost.com/uk-news/copeland-cookstown-expansion-nears-completion), 14 May 2024). DESNZ later added £4.6m from its Heat Pump Investment Accelerator for new lines and test labs ([Cooling Post](https://www.coolingpost.com/uk-news/4-6m-grant-to-boost-copeland-compressor-production), 30 Apr 2025).

- **Housing Executive homes go fabric first.** The Housing Executive and Phoenix Energy retrofitted 15 Belfast homes with insulation, new windows and doors, then hybrid heat pump and gas boiler systems. Phoenix is monitoring ten of them, including two gas-only comparators ([Phoenix Energy](https://phoenixenergyni.com/news/phoenix-energy-joins-with-northern-ireland-housing-executive-to-launch-hybrid-heating-programme-in-local-homes), Oct 2026).

- **A 2021 install that the 2027 rules would bar.** The RULET project, run by Ulster University, the Housing Executive and Grant, fitted a 6 kW R32 air-to-water unit in rural NI ([Passive House Plus](https://passivehouseplus.ie/news/marketplace/grant-heat-pumps-at-centre-of-ni-energy-transition-project), 30 Aug 2021). R32 has a GWP of 675. From 1 January 2027 such units up to 12 kW cannot be placed on the NI market unless safety rules require it.

- **A housing association trial.** Alpha Housing fitted hybrid heat pumps to two bungalows at Carnvue Court, Newtownabbey, as a fully monitored trial with Phoenix Energy ([Business Eye](https://www.businesseye.co.uk/news/first-ni-housing-association-instals-hybrid-heat-pumps/), 12 Jun 2024).

- **The GB comparison.** From 21 July 2026, oil-heated homes in England and Wales can get £9,000 off a heat pump, with leaflets sent to 200,000 eligible homes ([DESNZ](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/thousands-of-homes-will-be-eligible-for-9000-off-a-heat-pump), 26 Jun 2026). NI's oil-heated homes got a £100 heating oil card instead ([DfC](https://www.communities-ni.gov.uk/news/lyons-announces-agreement-home-heating-oil-support), 16 Apr 2026).

## Recommendations

1.  **DfE: build a low-income tier into the NI grant and legislate in 2027.** A part-grant open to all favours owners who can fund the balance. The return of 5% VAT on 1 April 2027 widens that balance.

2.  **DfE and DfC: fund insulation first for oil homes.** Belfast's warning on fuel poverty applies across NI's oil-heated homes.

3.  **Defra: publish the promised next-steps report before 1 January 2027.** Industry needs to know whether GB will adopt product bans or SF6 rules.

4.  **UK Government: publish an NI F-gas divergence assessment,** and the Windsor Framework scrutiny committee should review it again.

5.  **Invest NI and DfE: back natural-refrigerant skills and exports.** The Cookstown compressor base is an advantage worth building on.

6.  **NI manufacturers: design to the stricter EU rules by default.** Avoid HFO lock-in until the PFAS restriction is settled.

7.  **Councils and public bodies: audit SF6 and HFC assets now,** and specify low-GWP, PFAS-free options from 2027 where safe.
