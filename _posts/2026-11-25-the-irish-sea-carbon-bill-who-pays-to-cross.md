## The argument in brief

On 10 March 2026 the Northern Ireland Assembly voted 49 to 30 to approve the order putting domestic shipping into the UK Emissions Trading Scheme (UK ETS). DAERA Minister Andrew Muir moved the motion. Two days later the Lords rejected a fatal motion by 134 votes to 26. In that debate Lord Empey (UUP) said NI parties had been misled, and Lord Dodds (DUP) asked why Scottish island ferries are exempt and Northern Ireland is not. The order came into force on 1 July 2026 as SI 2026/392.

Freight carbon costs fall hardest on a region that depends on sea links. Northern Ireland moves almost everything by ferry and HGV, and it sits across two carbon regimes. The 50% discount on GB–NI voyages is temporary by design. The UK ETS Authority says it lasts only while Irish Sea carbon pricing differs, and GB–NI voyages move to full surrender if international voyages join the UK ETS (Nov 2025). The UK and EU aim to agree ETS linking at a summit on 20 November 2026.

The real choice for firms is whether to treat today's small surcharge as the start of a rising cost line. The author argues they should, and that the money raised should come back to NI ports and fleets.

*This piece sets out the arguments on each side; the recommendations are the author's. Facts checked to 30 October 2026.*

## The lifecycle at a glance

<figure class="page-photo"><img src="{{ '/assets/policy-series/freight-lifecycle.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Diagram: GB to NI freight lifecycle: 8 stages, 3 leaks" loading="lazy"><figcaption>GB to NI freight lifecycle · 8 stages, 3 leaks</figcaption></figure>

A trailer from GB to Larne pays full carbon price while its ferry sits at either berth, and half price at sea. The cost reaches the shipper as a monthly surcharge, while the revenue goes to the Treasury rather than to ports.

- **Equal treatment.** Unionists compare NI with Scottish islands. The government compares NI with Ireland–GB routes, which pay EU ETS at 50%.

- **Permanence.** Labour and the Lib Dems accept a path to full pricing. The Conservatives and Reform would scrap the whole scheme.

- **Revenue.** No proceeds are earmarked, and nobody has committed money to NI ports.

- **CBAM leverage.** Supporters say approval keeps NI exports clear of EU CBAM. Lord Empey says that risk was overstated.

The UK ETS is run jointly by four governments. EU rules reach NI through the Windsor Framework.

## Where the pillars compete

- **Decarbonisation against cost of living (E against S).** The government puts the freight cost effect below 1%; Baroness Hoey cited about 6% (12 Mar 2026). A small percentage on every pallet from GB weighs more on a peripheral region.

- **Equal treatment against lifeline exemption (S against S).** Scottish island ferries are exempt to protect essential services. NI's two million residents depend on sea links too.

- **LNG against diesel (E against E).** The ICCT's FUMES project measured average methane slip of 6.4% from common LNG engines, against 3.1% assumed by the EU and 3.5% by the IMO. The UK ETS prices methane, so the assumed rate sets the bill (Nov 2025). A low-CO₂ ferry can warm the climate more than its paperwork shows.

- **Port growth against local air (E against S).** Belfast Harbour is completing a £90m cruise terminal (19 May 2026). Berth time is charged in full under the UK ETS, so shore power helps both the carbon bill and local air.

- **Cross-border fuel prices (S against E).** ETS2 will raise road fuel costs in Ireland from 2028 unless Dublin's exemption holds. NI is outside ETS2, which may shift where fleets refuel.

## What it means for Northern Ireland firms and councils

- **Surcharges are already on invoices.** Stena's monthly notices for its Belfast routes set a per-metre UK ETS charge: £1.08 in July and £1.13 in September 2026. It tracks the carbon price and would rise if the 50% deduction ends.

- **Larne–Cairnryan is a domestic route.** It gets the deduction at sea, but berth time in Larne and Cairnryan is charged in full. Turnaround time and shore power matter.

- **Customers will ask for Scope 3 freight data.** GLEC Framework v3 aligns with ISO 14083:2023.

- **Update contract clauses.** Check carriage contracts for carbon pass-through, fuel indexation and who carries ETS costs after 2028.

- **Fuel duty steps up in July 2027.** The 5p cut now ends on 30 June 2027. Rates then return to pre-March 2022 levels and rise with RPI (Budget, 28 Oct 2026). Hauliers pricing 2027 contracts should build in the step.

- **Councils have three roles.** Mid and East Antrim (Larne), Belfast and Newry, Mourne and Down host ports; they manage air quality and plan HGV routes and depots.

- **Fleet renewal is live.** NI appears to follow EU HGV CO₂ rules (45% cut by 2030). GB policy depends on the next election.

## Practical examples

- **Stena's ETS line on Belfast freight.** Stena Line Freight set its UK ETS charge at £1.08 a metre for July 2026 and £1.13 for September, on its Belfast routes to Cairnryan, Heysham and Liverpool. On a 13.6-metre trailer that is about £15 a crossing (author's arithmetic). [Stena Line Freight, September 2026 notice](https://stenalinefreight.com/app/uploads/2026/08/uk-ets-september-2026-9.pdf)

- **Larne berth time counts in full.** The UK ETS covers every in-port emission at a UK port, whatever the voyage. On Larne–Cairnryan, time alongside at both ends is charged in full, while the sea leg gets the 50% deduction (Nov 2025). [UK ETS Authority response](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6925919c33d088f6d5da2ccd/uk-ets-domestic-maritime-main-authority-response.pdf)

- **A hybrid freight ferry on the Irish Sea.** Stena Futura entered service on Belfast–Heysham on 22 September 2025. It is methanol-ready and can run on batteries and shore power "when available". [Stena Line release, 18 Sep 2025](https://news.cision.com/stena-line-ukie/r/stena-line-welcomes-stena-futura-onto-belfast-heysham-route,c4237009)

- **Belfast plans shore power; Larne has no plan found.** Belfast Harbour's £1.3bn 25-year plan, set out on 19 May 2026, includes its first shore power for docked ships. We found no published shore power plan for Larne (28 Oct 2026). [RTÉ, 19 May 2026](https://www.rte.ie/news/business/2026/0519/1574088-belfast-harbours-13-billion-investment-plan/)

- **A £1 road tax year for hauliers.** Most HGVs renewing vehicle excise duty between 1 July 2026 and 30 June 2027 pay £1. VED is a UK-wide tax, so NI fleets renewing in that window benefit too. The Budget of 28 October 2026 kept the £1 rate to 30 June 2027 and raises HGV VED and the HGV levy by RPI from 1 April 2027. [GOV.UK, 17 Jun 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/12-month-vehicle-excise-duty-ved-holiday-for-heavy-goods-vehicles-hgvs/temporary-vehicle-excise-duty-rates-for-heavy-goods-vehicles)

- **Methane slip measured at twice the assumption.** The ICCT's FUMES project measured average slip of 6.4% from common four-stroke LNG engines on 18 ships. The EU assumes 3.1% and the IMO 3.5% (Jan 2024). [ICCT FUMES report](https://theicct.org/publication/fumes-characterizing-methane-emissions-from-lng-fueled-ships-using-drones-helicopters-and-on-board-measurements-jan24/)

## Recommendations

1.  **UK ETS Authority: ring-fence a share of maritime proceeds for NI port shore power.** None is earmarked, and berth emissions are charged in full.

2.  **DESNZ and NIO: publish route-level cost estimates for GB–NI freight before the 2028 review.** The 1% and 6% figures cannot both be right.

3.  **UK Government: set out how and when the NI deduction ends under any EU link.** Firms need a known date.

4.  **UK ETS Authority: tighten default methane-slip values for LNG ferries.** Measured slip runs about twice the assumed rate.

5.  **DfI: publish an NI HGV charging and depot plan tied to the 2030 EU CO₂ targets.** Grid capacity near Larne, Belfast and Warrenpoint is the binding constraint.

6.  **Firms: ask carriers for ISO 14083 data and an ETS line on invoices.**

7.  **Firms: add carbon pass-through and review clauses to freight contracts now.** A cost that is small today is designed to rise.

8.  **Councils: map port air quality and HGV routes, and back shore power in local plans.**
