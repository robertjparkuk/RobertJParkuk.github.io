*Part of the [Policy series]({{ '/policy-series/' | relative_url }}): where the rules stand in Northern Ireland, who decides them, and what to do about it. Updated as the rules change.*

## The argument in brief

On 2 June 2026, SDLP MLA Colin McGrath warned of a "costly catastrophe" in how Northern Ireland handles its rubbish. DAERA Minister Andrew Muir had confirmed in a written answer that only two landfill sites still take household waste: Cottonmount and Craigmore. The Irish News reported that Northern Ireland has about 3% of the UK's population but sends 16% of the UK's residual waste exports.

Waste rules in Northern Ireland are moving faster than the infrastructure and money behind them. Producers now pay councils through packaging EPR, with £52.2 million going to NI councils in 2026–27. A deposit return scheme starts in October 2027. Parts of the EU Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR) apply here from 12 August 2026. Westminster has delayed carbon pricing for incineration in Great Britain. NI energy-from-waste plants stay under the EU carbon market, which plans to phase incineration in from 2031. Each rule is defensible on its own. Together they move costs onto households, small shops and council budgets, while the biggest environmental harm, illegal dumping, remains underfunded.

*This piece sets out the arguments on each side; the recommendations are the author's. Facts checked to 30 October 2026.*

- **Who pays.** Labour keeps EPR producer-funded. Retailers say costs pass through to shoppers. The DUP frames council charges as a tax.

- **Incineration.** Arc21 councils still back Hightown. Sinn Féin and campaigners have opposed it. The SDLP questions the cost of exporting waste.

- **Glass.** Wales keeps glass in its DRS. England and NI leave it out. Welsh Conservatives want it removed.

- **Carbon pricing.** Westminster has paused carbon pricing for incineration in GB. NI plants stay in the EU scheme, so policy now splits within the UK.

## Where the pillars compete

- **Producer pays, shopper pays.** EPR is meant to shift costs from ratepayers to producers. Retailers estimate up to £56 a year per household passes through to prices (their own figure, May 2026). A flat cost on food packaging hits low-income households hardest.

- **Recyclability versus food protection (E versus E).** Red fees apply to paper and board with more than 5% non-fibre content. The Foodservice Packaging Association warns this could discourage barrier coatings that cut food waste.

- **Incineration versus recycling.** Campaigners estimate Hightown would cost about £1.5bn over 25 years and say higher recycling could halve the waste it needs. Exporting to Scandinavia carries shipping emissions and cost risk.

- **Carbon price versus council budgets.** Delaying ETS pricing protects council finances but removes a signal to take fossil plastics out of residual waste.

- **Waste crime versus public spending.** The NI Audit Office found about £1m in fines since 2019, against an estimated £17m in avoided disposal costs (3 Jul 2025). Mobuoy still has no committed funding. The UK Budget funded tax liabilities for clearing six illegal waste sites (28 Oct 2026). It did not say any are in NI.

## What it means for Northern Ireland firms and councils

- **Manufacturers:** red-rated packaging gets dearer each year, from 1.2× to 1.6× and then 2×. Your recyclability ratings now affect costs directly. Plastic packaging tax rises by CPI from 1 Apr 2027, and recycled-content certification becomes mandatory from 1 Apr 2030 (Budget, 28 Oct 2026).

- **Exporters to Ireland and the EU:** you now need a PPWR declaration of conformity for heavy metals and PFAS. Harmonised labels follow later. The EU Council agreed on 21 Oct 2026 to ease obligations for small producers; talks with Parliament have not started.

- **Councils:** EPR income helps, but stagnant recycling rates put it at risk. The provisional recycling rate for January–March 2026 was 47.2%, against a 70% target. Landfill tax on residual waste rises again from 1 Apr 2027 (Budget, 28 Oct 2026).

- **Councils with energy-from-waste contracts:** a future carbon cost could pass through gate fees, with no support mechanism announced.

- **Small shops:** register with Exchange for Change and decide whether to seek an exemption. The manual handling fee is 3p per container.

## Practical examples

- **Illegal waste site, Belfast.** On 25 June 2026 Laganside Crown Court jailed an operator for nine months over about 367 tonnes of waste at two Crumlin Road sites. NIEA cleared both sites at public expense ([DAERA](https://www.daera-ni.gov.uk/news/man-given-prison-sentence-367-tonnes-unauthorised-waste-offence)).

- **Mobuoy, near Derry.** The site holds over a million tonnes of waste. DAERA's summary of consultation responses found no fundamental change was needed to the remediation methods ([Derry Now, 26 Mar 2026](https://www.derrynow.com/news/local-news/2036786/daera-publishes-responses-to-mobuoy-consultation.html)). Costs were still being worked out in May ([DAERA, 22 May 2026](https://www.daera-ni.gov.uk/news/daera-minister-updates-political-leaders-mobuoy-remediation)).

- **Hightown, Mallusk.** arc21's incinerator application is still undecided. The Department for Infrastructure asked for more information on 6 March 2026, with three months to reply ([UKWIN, a campaign group, updated 20 Apr 2026](https://ukwin.org.uk/incinerators/library/County+Antrim/96)).

- **Fuel exports.** NI councils exported 281,660 tonnes of waste as refuse-derived fuel in figures reported in November 2024. Most went to Scandinavia, and over two-thirds left through Warrenpoint ([Irish News, 6 Nov 2024](https://irishnews.com/news/northern-ireland/minister-expected-to-give-fresh-consideration-to-hightown-incinerator-proposal-as-waste-exports-soar-3BA74K2YMFDUTEWDCLDT5VLMAU)).

- **Less packaging, Moy Park.** Northern Ireland's largest private firm cut its packaging by 10% in a year. It targets 5% less packaging and 5% more recyclable packaging each year ([Packaging Gateway, 3 Jan 2024](https://www.packaging-gateway.com/newsletters/moy-park-reduces-packaging)).

- **PFAS in food packaging.** Food-contact packaging placed on the NI market from 12 August 2026 must meet PPWR PFAS limits. Older stock can sell through, and the Food Standards Agency enforces ([DAERA FAQ, Aug 2026](https://www.daera-ni.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2026-08/Frequently%20Asked%20Questions%20-%20Packaging%20and%20Packaging%20Waste%20Regulation.PDF)).

## Recommendations

1.  **DAERA: publish the final strategy with a costed plan for residual waste.** Capacity decisions should follow the recycling plan.

2.  **DAERA and councils: report each year how the £52m of EPR income is spent.** Households should see the offset.

3.  **DAERA, with DESNZ: set out how NI would meet any future carbon price on incineration.** The Commission's proposed 2031 start leaves little lead time.

4.  **Executive and Department of Justice: fund Mobuoy and act on the NIAO waste crime review.** Penalties must exceed the profits. Ask HM Treasury whether its new funding for clearing illegal waste sites (28 Oct 2026) can extend to NI.

5.  **DAERA: decide collection consistency with an equality assessment.** Charities have raised concerns for older and disabled householders.

6.  **Firms: run recyclability assessments now and redesign red-rated lines first.** The penalty doubles by Year 4. Build evidence of recycled content too: certification becomes mandatory from 1 Apr 2030.

7.  **Firms selling into NI or the EU: prepare PPWR declarations now** and plan label changes once the EU implementing act is published.

8.  **Small retailers: price the handling fee against staff time before applying for an exemption.**
