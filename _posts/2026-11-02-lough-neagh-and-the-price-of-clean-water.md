## The argument in brief

On 22 October 2026, Sinn Féin president Mary Lou McDonald asked the Irish government to intervene in the Lough Neagh crisis, and said any delay on wastewater reform was not coming from her party. In the same report, DAERA Minister Andrew Muir's plan to end NI Water's protection from environmental penalties had been before the Executive for months. Six weeks earlier, the Office for Environmental Protection (OEP) warned that his new farm nutrient rules may not go far enough or fast enough.

The lough has had blue-green algae every summer since 2023, and in 2026 signs appeared as early as February. Everyone agrees on the diagnosis: too much phosphorus from farms, sewage and septic tanks, made worse by warmer water and zebra mussels. No one agrees on who should pay. Every remedy costs a defined group: farmers, households, developers or other public services. Stormont has so far made each choice only part of the way. The Assembly rises on 16 March 2027, and the next five months will show which choices are settled and which are left to the next mandate.

*This piece sets out the arguments on each side; the recommendations are the author's. Facts checked to 30 October 2026.*

- **Who pays first.** Unionist parties and the UFU say NI Water must face the same rules as farmers. Alliance agrees but says the DUP is delaying the SORPI change.

- **Water charges.** Sinn Féin rules them out. Business and builders back a rates-based levy of about £1.25 a week (NI Chamber, 20 Jan 2026).

- **Independent oversight.** The DUP prefers a stronger NIEA inside DAERA. Alliance, the SDLP and the RSPB want an arm's-length agency.

- **All-island framing.** Sinn Féin looks to Dublin and the Shared Island Fund. Unionists treat it as a Stormont matter.

Most levers sit at Stormont. Cross-cutting or controversial decisions need the whole Executive, which in practice gives any large party a veto.

## Where the pillars compete

- **Farm incomes against phosphorus cuts.** The NAP proposals aim to cut the national phosphorus surplus by 30% from a 2024 baseline over four years. Low-emission slurry kit would be mandatory on farms over 100 livestock units from February 2028, reaching 50 units by 2030. Tiered rules put most duties on the most intensive farms, which could spare small ones.

- **Housing against pollution.** Builders say the funding gap blocks about 6,150 homes over three years. DAERA says over 20 million tonnes of untreated sewage reach waterways each year. Connecting new homes to overloaded works adds load.

- **No water charges against investment.** Ruling out charges protects households now. It also leaves a gap of about £2bn for 2028–2033 (an industry estimate), competing with health and schools in the same budget. The Budget of 28 Oct 2026 added £270m to that budget and offered £1.5bn more, but none of it is earmarked for water.

- **E against E.** The OEP warns that loosening the 170 kg N limit could let many farms reach 250 kg N/ha, raising ammonia and nitrous oxide. Manure processing fixes water but may shift pollution to air.

- **Sand against ecology.** Lough sand supplies construction. A Queen's University Belfast and Newcastle University study found dredging has lowered parts of the bed by up to 17 metres, with plumes across much of the lake (15 Apr 2026). The 2025 eel season was suspended.

## What it means for Northern Ireland firms and councils

- **Trade effluent costs will rise.** The trade effluent tariff basket rises 3.4% in real terms in 2027/28 (Utility Regulator, Aug 2026). Expect tighter consent limits as NI Water faces enforcement.

- **Discharge consents are under review.** DAERA plans new standards across NI (3 Mar 2026). Firms with direct discharges should check headroom now.

- **Connections may block expansion.** Over 100 towns have restricted wastewater capacity, so check sewerage early for any new site or extra shift.

- **Agri-food supply chains are exposed.** New dairy excretion bands change farm nutrient budgets and may squeeze milk supply.

- **Councils carry planning risk.** NI Water's consultee responses can stop otherwise sound applications; local development plans need to reflect real capacity.

- **Environmental health workload is growing.** The inter-agency blue-green algae protocol was updated in January 2026, and bathing-water warnings now reach the north coast.

## Practical examples

- **A dairy processor fined £115,000.** LacPatrick Dairies (NI) Ltd, a Lakeland subsidiary at Artigarvan, admitted seven PPC permit and nine Water Order offences at Strabane Magistrates' Court on 13 August 2026. Milk and effluent reached the Glenmornan River in 2025. Lakeland says it has since upgraded wastewater treatment and monitoring ([Irish Farmers Journal, 14 Aug 2026](https://www.farmersjournal.ie/news/news/lakeland-fined-115-000-for-artigarvan-pollution-928215)).

- **An eel fishery cut back.** For 2026, DAERA allowed a 25-tonne catch of silver eels only and closed the yellow eel fishery for three years ([DAERA, 4 Jun 2026](https://www.daera-ni.gov.uk/news/minister-confirms-2026-eel-fishery-measures-lough-neagh)). A food business that depends on the lough is shrinking while the remedies are argued over.

- **A fisherman takes DAERA to court.** A Lough Neagh eel fisherman sought judicial review of DAERA's pollution controls, with a permission hearing in Belfast on 24 March 2026. River Action and Friends of the Earth NI applied to intervene ([Irish Legal News, 24 Mar 2026](https://www.irishlegal.com/articles/fisherman-seeks-to-bring-judicial-review-over-lough-neagh-pollution)).

- **Capacity bought with public money.** An extra £30m of capital has unlocked wastewater capacity for 5,300 more homes, above the 4,300 NI Water had planned by the end of PC28. A voluntary developer scheme comes next ([NI Assembly, 19 Mar 2026](https://www.niassembly.gov.uk/assembly-business/official-report/written-ministerial-statements/department-for-infrastructure-developer-contributions-for-wastewater-infrastructure)).

- **Farm payments tied to soil data.** About £240m in Farm Sustainability Payments had been paid by 1 September 2026. Claimants must join the Soil Nutrient Health Scheme, which links public farm support to soil nutrient testing ([DAERA, 1 Sep 2026](https://www.daera-ni.gov.uk/news/minister-welcomes-first-year-farm-sustainability-payments)).

## Recommendations

1.  **Executive: approve the SORPI withdrawal before Christmas 2026.** Equal enforcement is the price of farmer consent to the NAP.

2.  **DAERA: adopt the OEP's advice to fix an early phosphorus baseline.** Without one, reported cuts may not be real.

3.  **DAERA: aim support at the highest-surplus farms.** It is cheaper and fairer than blanket rules.

4.  **Executive and DfI: publish a multi-year NI Water capital settlement before PC28.** Business plans due in November 2026 need certainty. The £270m and £1.5bn from the 28 Oct 2026 Budget give the Executive room to commit.

5.  **DfI: set a review date for the voluntary developer-contribution scheme.** That gives a clear route to a compulsory levy if uptake is low.

6.  **Next Executive: legislate for an independent environmental protection agency early in the 2027 mandate.** Trust in regulation needs distance from ministers.

7.  **Manufacturers: audit trade effluent and water use in 2026–27.** Lower loads cut costs and ease connection risk.

8.  **Councils: map wastewater constraints into local development plans.** That steers growth to places with capacity.
