*Status as at 21 November 2026. General information, not legal advice.*

Much of the law that governs land, water, carbon and the streets we share does not do what its text says. Some was never switched on. Some has been quietly replaced. Some has been rewritten by the courts. Eight of the fifteen provisions in this paper's register are Northern Ireland law.

## Five things this paper argues

- **The statute book no longer tells you the law.** The register finds fifteen provisions that are uncommenced, not working, bypassed or overtaken by the courts.
- **Commence, explain or repeal.** Ministers should have a duty to bring law into force within five years, or explain to the legislature every year why not. Nothing should lapse by accident; a sunset applies only where an Act expressly says so.
- **One code of engagement.** One named decision-maker, one front door, plain notice, reasons and optional internal review, whatever statute the decision is taken under.
- **Northern Ireland: central delivery, local voice.** Keep delivery with the Department for Infrastructure, the Housing Executive, Translink and NI Water. Give councils a statutory voice on listed decisions and a right to reasons.
- **Consolidate in instalments.** Wales is already rebuilding its statute book topic by topic. A UK framework, with each nation's own Code inside it, can do the same for environment and infrastructure.

## Law that sleeps

Three examples show the pattern.

**Contaminated land.** [Part III of the Waste and Contaminated Land (Northern Ireland) Order 1997](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/nisi/1997/2778/part/III) would give Northern Ireland the regime Great Britain has had for decades. It has never been commenced. Councils fall back on planning conditions and nuisance law.

**Water charges.** The [Water and Sewerage Services (NI) Order 2006](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/nisi/2006/3336/contents) was built around household charges from April 2007. Instead, the Department pays NI Water grants under [Article 213](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/nisi/2006/3336/article/213) equal to the discounts on those charges, currently for the period ending 31 March 2027. The statute describes one funding model; the country runs another.

**A climate watchdog with no one in post.** The [Climate Change Act (NI) 2022](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/nia/2022/31/section/50) provided for a Northern Ireland Climate Commissioner. The regulations came into operation on 9 April 2025, late. No appointment had been announced in the latest Assembly answer I found (March 2026).

## Why case law matters

The common law is law made by judges rather than by a legislature. When a court decides a case, the reasoning needed for the decision binds lower courts in later cases on the same point. It fills gaps the statute book leaves, decides what statutes mean, and sets the tests for judicial review. In Northern Ireland, with no statutory contaminated land regime, judge-made rules such as nuisance are much of what a neighbour of contaminated land can rely on.

Four judgments in the register change what the text means in practice, including [*Re Buick* [2018] NICA 26](https://www.judiciaryni.uk/files/judiciaryni/decisions/Buick%27s%20%28Colin%29%20Application%20as%20Chair%20Person%20of%20NOARC%2021.pdf) on decisions with no minister in office and [*Finch* [2024] UKSC 20](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/uksc/2024/20) on downstream emissions. The draft Bill does not codify the common law. It asks the department to respond within 12 months of a final ruling that changes what an Act means: amend it, confirm it, or annotate it.

## Northern Ireland: central delivery, local voice

Northern Ireland's infrastructure is the most centralised in the UK, and deliberately so. Councils have no roads, public transport or social housing functions. This paper does not reopen that settlement. It builds on the existing community planning duty in [Part 10 of the Local Government Act (NI) 2014](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/nia/2014/8/part/10), moving it from "have regard" to "give reasons": central bodies publish how their programmes respond to each council's plans, and explain any departure.

## What should happen now

1. Count it: a systematic sweep of legislation.gov.uk for every uncommenced provision in this field.
2. Start the clock: legislate the duty to commence, explain or repeal.
3. Revive the Northern Ireland Law Commission, or agree a joint reference with England and Wales.
4. Strengthen community planning in Northern Ireland from "have regard" to "give reasons".
5. Adopt the code of engagement, starting with the register of responsibility.
6. Before the May 2027 Assembly election, ask each party where it stands on items 2 to 4.
