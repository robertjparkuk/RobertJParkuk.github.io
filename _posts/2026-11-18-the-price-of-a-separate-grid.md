## The argument in brief

On 21 July 2026, the day after he took office, Prime Minister Andy Burnham announced a zero VAT rate on household electricity in Great Britain for six months from 1 October. Northern Ireland was left out, because EU VAT rules still apply to electricity here under the Windsor Framework. Communities Minister Gordon Lyons (DUP) blamed post-Brexit rules. Economy Minister Caoimhe Archibald (Sinn Féin) said it continued a pattern of London overlooking Northern Ireland. In September the Treasury funded a one-off credit for NI households instead. The Budget of 28 October 2026 extended the GB zero rate to 30 June 2027; HMRC guidance of 8 September kept NI at 5%.

The VAT row is the household version of a bigger gap for industry. Since 2023, Westminster has built a set of tools to cut electricity costs for GB manufacturers: 90% network charge relief, the British Industrial Competitiveness Scheme (BICS) and the planned end of Carbon Price Support. None of them reaches Northern Ireland, because NI sits in the all-island Single Electricity Market and energy is devolved. The Budget of 28 October 2026 changed that for industry. It funds the NI Executive to set up its own version of BICS for heavy electricity users in manufacturing. No amount was stated, and design and timing are for the Executive.

Northern Ireland cannot simply copy GB relief. GB funds part of it by moving costs elsewhere, and in a small market those costs would land on households, many of them fuel poor and dependent on heating oil. The lasting fix is cheaper NI generation and a stronger grid: renewables auctions and the North–South interconnector. Those take years, and business needs help sooner. The funded NI version of BICS could bridge the gap, and Exchequer money keeps its cost off household bills. How soon depends on the Executive.

*This piece sets out the arguments on each side; the recommendations are the author's. Facts checked to 30 October 2026.*

- **Who pays.** Reform and the Conservatives would remove carbon costs and levies. Labour and the Lib Dems would move costs onto the Exchequer or restructured contracts. Under every option, someone other than the bill payer still pays.

- **Carbon pricing.** Scrapping the UK ETS would not touch NI generators, which sit in the EU ETS.

- **Constitution versus money.** The DUP presents the VAT gap as a Windsor Framework problem; Sinn Féin presents it as London underfunding Stormont. Both want more money for NI.

- **Speed.** GB relief starts in 2027. NI's structural fixes, renewables auctions and the interconnector, arrive from 2028 to 2031. The funded NI version of BICS has no start date yet (28 Oct 2026).

## Where the pillars compete

- **Industrial relief against fuel poverty.** A senior DfE official told the Economy Committee that GB-style relief is funded through household bills, which makes it hard to copy in NI without hurting fuel-poor homes (reported early 2026). Exchequer funding for an NI version of BICS, announced on 28 Oct 2026, eases this if the scheme stays off household bills.

- **Cheaper gas power now against carbon signals.** Ending Carbon Price Support in 2028 lowers the cost of GB gas generation. That cuts bills but weakens the reason to switch away from gas.

- **Two-way contracts.** Under the NI price guarantee, consumers pay in when wholesale prices are low and get money back when they are high. That protects against gas spikes but can add to bills in calm years.

- **Grid against land.** The interconnector and about £1.3bn of SONI grid projects are needed to cut wasted wind (22% of renewable output was dispatched down in 2025). Landowners oppose the routes.

- **Electricity relief misses oil-heated homes.** Half of NI households rely on oil alone for central heating (Census 2021), so help with electricity bills does not reach their heating. The Budget removed the 4% kerosene tariff (28 Oct 2026); HM Treasury says 61% of households rely on heating oil.

## What it means for Northern Ireland firms and councils

- **GB competitors get lower costs from 2027.** Benchmark against DESNZ figures: GB manufacturing power averaged 17.4p/kWh excluding Climate Change Levy in Q2 2026.

- **Heavy electricity users should engage early on the NI version of BICS.** The Budget (28 Oct 2026) funded the Executive to set it up but gave no amount, criteria or start date.

- **Climate Change Levy payers get a frozen electricity rate.** From 1 Apr 2028 the electricity main rate stays at the 2027–28 level while gas rises by RPI. That helps the case for electrifying heat.

- **Regulated tariffs are rising.** Power NI went up 6.2% from 1 July 2026. Firmus gas rose 15.7% in July, then a further 9% (Utility Regulator notice, 4 Sep 2026).

- **Groups with GB sites** should check BICS eligibility for those plants and flag the Windsor Framework Article 10 question.

- **Councils** can offer land, rooftops and planning certainty for renewables projects ahead of the first REPG auction, and look at generation for their own estate.

- **Large users** should respond to the Utility Regulator's four-year review of network tariff structures (consultation opened 23 Sep 2026) using their own load profiles.

## Practical examples

- **NI manufacturers named in Parliament.** Jim Shannon (DUP) said Magellan Aerospace, T.G. Eakin and Mash Direct may not qualify for BICS. Peter Kyle replied that the Government was working with the Assembly so NI manufacturers would benefit ([Commons, 16 Apr 2026](https://parallelparliament.co.uk/debate/2026-04-16/commons/commons-chamber/british-industrial-competitiveness-scheme)).

- **A council buying local wind power.** Belfast City Council became the first customer for power from 3T Power's Ballyutoag wind farm. The supply covers City Hall and over 200 other council buildings ([Belfast City Council, 9 Dec 2025](https://www.belfastcity.gov.uk/news/council-venues-to-make-the-switch-to-locally-gener)).

- **The credit that replaced the VAT cut.** About 860,000 NI homes get a one-off £63 discount from 6 October 2026, costing an estimated £53m ([ITV News, 17 Sep 2026](https://www.itv.com/news/utv/2026-09-17/households-to-get-63-off-electricity-bills)). It combines NIRO relief with a VAT equivalent ([DfE, 25 Aug 2026](https://www.economy-ni.gov.uk/news/minister-provides-update-domestic-electricity-discount-scheme)).

- **Small firms under strain.** A Utility Regulator study of 42 NI small businesses found upfront cost was the main barrier to efficiency work. Awareness of available support was low ([Utility Regulator, 14 Oct 2026](https://www.uregni.gov.uk/news-centre/small-business-consumer-experience-energy-stress-lived-experience-report-published)).

- **Wind the grid could not take.** In 2025, 22% of NI renewable output was dispatched down, against 29.6% in 2024. SONI's draft plan to 2034 lists over £1.3bn of grid projects ([Farmers Journal, 8 Apr 2026](https://www.farmersjournal.ie/amp/more/northern-ireland/renewables-driving-investment-in-grid-910871)).

## Recommendations

1.  **NI Executive and HM Treasury: set the amount, rules and start date for the funded NI version of BICS.** The Budget (28 Oct 2026) gave funding but no figure. Exchequer funding keeps the cost off household bills.

2.  **HM Treasury and DfE: extend the NI credit to match the GB zero VAT rate, now extended to 30 June 2027.** Otherwise NI households fall behind again.

3.  **DfE and the Assembly: pass the Renewable Electricity Generation Bill and set a firm date for the first auction.** On NISRA's latest figures, 80% needs about 2,700 GWh more renewable output a year at today's demand.

4.  **Utility Regulator: publish a regular NI and GB non-domestic price comparison by size band.** The last full analysis dates from 2016.

5.  **DfE and DfC: target heating oil households separately.** Electricity relief misses most rural homes. The 4% kerosene tariff removal (28 Oct 2026) helps, and the extra £270m of Barnett money gives room for more.

6.  **Firms: model costs under both GB and NI regimes,** including EU ETS pass-through and BICS for any GB sites and the NI version once designed, before deciding where to invest.

7.  **Councils: prepare sites and planning guidance for renewables and grid upgrades.** Faster consents bring down future prices.
