The Department for the Economy is building a business case for a scheme to subsidise biomethane in industrial heat from 2028 to 2035, and has asked producers and users what they would supply and buy. My response answers the user questions as an adviser to manufacturers, and makes one argument: demand will follow recognition, not only price. A firm will sign a long purchase agreement for certified gas only if that gas counts where the firm reports.

- **Request for information:** [Biomethane Business Case - Request for Information](https://consultations.nidirect.gov.uk/dfe/biomethane-business-case-request-for-information/)

- **Sent:** 19 November 2026

This is my own response as an individual practitioner. No client is involved or represented. I am not a biomethane producer or gas buyer.

## Where certified grid biomethane counts today

| Where the firm reports | Does it count? |
|---|---|
| UK statutory energy and carbon reporting (SECR) | Partly, as a second, market-based figure beside the standard grid-gas figure |
| UK ETS, where NI manufacturers sit | No. Only biomethane supplied directly to the installation is zero-rated |
| EU ETS (in NI, electricity generators only) | Yes, with sustainability evidence, no double counting, and buyer and producer on the same grid |
| Customers' Scope 3 data requests | Unsettled |

Position as at 19 November 2026; sources are in the PDF.

## What I told the Department

- **Price.** Most firms will pay no more than the natural gas they would otherwise buy. Support should aim at price parity, and never exceed the gap. That is the lesson of the Renewable Heat Incentive.
- **Contracts.** A seven-year commitment is hard for a small or mid-sized firm. Let the supplier hold the long contract and the firm buy certified gas on normal terms.
- **Scale.** At about £13 million a year, support of 3p per kWh covers roughly 433 GWh and 5p roughly 260 GWh (my arithmetic). The cap will bind, so the allocation rules need to be clear from the start.
- **2035.** The funding ends in 2035. If supported firms then go back to fossil gas, the saving ends. If they leave the network, its fixed costs fall on the households and businesses that remain. The business case should say what happens in 2036.
- **Off-grid firms.** Some manufacturers are not on the gas network. A scheme limited to grid-injected gas will not reach them.

## Six design points

1. One certificate per supported kWh, cancelled once, interoperable with Gas Networks Ireland's scheme and the Green Gas Certification Scheme, with an EU-recognised Proof of Sustainability where the buyer needs it.
2. A UK ETS decision on grid-injected biomethane. DAERA sits on the UK ETS Authority.
3. Northern Ireland injection data supplied to the greenhouse gas inventory, so that NI production counts against NI's carbon budgets. At present it does not.
4. Measured methane leakage at plants and in the networks. I have found no published NI figure.
5. Digestate treated as a co-product, with plants and spreading kept off peat soils.
6. A short buyer's guide on reporting supported biomethane.

## Update, 21 November 2026

Two things have moved since I sent the response, and I have prepared a short addition for the Department.

**A correction.** The response says I found no UK ETS Authority consultation or decision on grid biomethane. That was too broad. The Authority has raised the question, most recently in DESNZ's [Common Biomass Sustainability Framework consultation](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/common-biomass-sustainability-framework) (2 December 2025 to 27 February 2026, section 1.3), which says it is considering whether the scheme could account for biomethane injected into the grid. I have found no published decision as at 21 November 2026. The substance stands: today only directly supplied biomethane is zero-rated.

**The UK–EU summit.** On 20 November the two sides agreed to link the UK and EU emissions trading schemes, intended from 2028. The Government's explainer says grid-injected biomethane will not be recognised until there is a system to account for it in line with EU legislation. The explainer is a guide, not law. The support scheme and the linked system would start in the same year, so the certificate design now decides whether supported gas has any value in the scheme. I set this out in [The carbon deal: what changes for Northern Ireland and Great Britain]({{ '/go/deal/' | relative_url }}).

## Why it matters to clients

If you run steam, ovens, kilns, dryers or furnaces on gas, this scheme is the main public money on offer for that heat between 2028 and 2035. Before you answer DfE, or sign anything later, ask one question of any biomethane offer: in which of my reports will this gas show as lower carbon, and who will accept the certificate?
