Since 1 January 2021, Northern Ireland has been the only part of the UK able to sell seed potatoes into the EU. On 20 November 2026 the UK and EU concluded negotiations on a sanitary and phytosanitary (SPS) agreement that will change that. If it applies from its target date of 1 March 2028, GB seed goes back into EU markets.

The potato is a small crop here: 3,187 hectares in June 2025, up 3% on the year before, out of about a million hectares farmed ([DAERA](https://datavis.nisra.gov.uk/daera/ni-agricultural-census-2025.html)). But it carries a valuable seed trade, a protected name in Comber Earlies, and a bigger environmental story than its size suggests.

## What the SPS deal changes

The government's guidance says [seed potato exports will resume](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/sps-agreement-preparing-your-business/horticulture-industry-preparing-your-business), and that GB-listed varieties will be added to the EU Common Catalogue. The text is not yet published, and the deal still needs signature, ratification and legislation.

For NI seed growers that cuts both ways:

- **New competition.** GB seed will compete with NI seed in EU markets that NI has served, against Dutch and other EU growers, since 2021.
- **An easier supply route.** Many NI growers buy Scottish basic seed. Since 1 October 2023 certified GB seed can come into NI under the [NI Plant Health Label scheme](https://www.daera-ni.gov.uk/articles/moving-gb-seed-potatoes-northern-ireland-under-niphl-scheme), for planting here only. A simpler route would help them.

If the March 2028 date holds, NI has about 15 months to lock in buyers on health status and established varieties.

## Low carbon, high soil cost

Per kilo, the potato is one of the lowest-carbon staples: about 0.09 kg CO2e at the farm gate in Scottish data ([FAS, 2024](https://www.fas.scot/downloads/ktif-final-report-data-driven-decisions-in-potatoes-phase-2)). Fertiliser is about two-thirds of that.

The bigger costs are in the ground:

- **Compaction.** Heavy harvesters on wet soils leave layers that roots can barely get through.
- **Soil carried off.** Across the EU about 14.7 million tonnes of soil a year leaves farms on harvested root crops, roughly 35% of it on potatoes ([JRC](https://esdac.jrc.ec.europa.eu/themes/harvest-erosion)).
- **Nitrate.** Bare ridges after harvest leak nitrate over winter.

Conacre is part of the picture. It gives growers the clean, rotated ground potatoes need, but 11-month lets reward no one for protecting soil structure.

## The most wasted food

Potatoes are the single most wasted food in UK homes, much of it as peel. The skin holds fibre and potassium and is worth eating, as long as the potato is sound and not green or sprouting. The FSA has said since 2023 that raw potatoes can be kept in the home fridge, which helps them last.

Two things people often get wrong: potatoes contain almost no vitamin A (orange sweet potato is the vitamin A root), and the NHS does not count them as one of your 5 A Day.

People on a low-potassium diet for kidney disease should follow their kidney dietitian, whose advice is usually the opposite: peel, cut small and boil in plenty of water.

## What I'd ask for

- **DAERA:** an NI seed potato market assessment before GB seed returns, and an NI potato carbon footprint, commissioned from AFBI.
- **Processors:** report peel and wash-water volumes so they can be matched to feed and anaerobic digestion.
- **Councils:** a simple food-waste message: store potatoes in the fridge, eat the skin unless it is green.
