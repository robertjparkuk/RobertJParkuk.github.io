*Part of the [Policy series]({{ '/policy-series/' | relative_url }}): where the rules stand in Northern Ireland, who decides them, and what to do about it. Updated as the rules change.*

## The argument in brief

On 30 September 2026 the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) published its final rules for listed companies' sustainability reporting (PS26/19). It had consulted in January on making climate reporting under UK SRS S2 mandatory, then settled on "comply or explain" for all of it, citing the burden on smaller issuers. Three weeks earlier, on 7 September 2026, the business department launched a consultation on modernising corporate reporting. The same day the Chancellor pledged to cut the burden of business regulation by 25%.

Both sides of the Irish Sea are cutting reporting duties in the name of competitiveness. For a Northern Ireland SME manufacturer the effect is mixed. The EU now caps what large customers can demand from smaller suppliers. The UK has no such cap, and public buyers still ask for carbon plans. Requests will keep arriving in 2027, in fewer and more standard formats. The sensible response is to build one data set that answers all of them. The political cost of the cuts falls mostly on the social pillar and on impact reporting; investor-focused climate data survives best.

*This piece sets out the arguments on each side; the recommendations are the author's. Facts checked to 30 October 2026.*

## Where the pillars compete

- **The social pillar took the deepest cut.** The CSDDD lost EU-wide civil liability and its transition-plan duty, and its scope moved up to 5,000 employees. Human-rights due diligence is now a large-firm matter only.

- **Investor materiality is winning over impact materiality.** UK SRS and the FCA rules follow the ISSB investor lens, and the VSME uses an "if applicable" test instead of double materiality. Harms to people and places get less reporting than risks to profit.

- **In NI procurement the social pillar is compulsory and carbon is optional.** PPN 01/21 makes fair-work measures mandatory; the zero-carbon theme is one option among several.

- **E against E: the easy carbon gets measured.** PPN 006 requires five Scope 3 categories, but not purchased goods (Category 1), which is the largest source of embodied carbon for most manufacturers.

- **Cost against transparency.** The SECR review found ongoing costs of £140m a year against £31m forecast, and 14–23% non-compliance. It also found that 79% of compliers disclosed data they would not otherwise have published.

## What it means for Northern Ireland firms and councils

- **EU customers.** From FY2027, an in-scope EU customer can only insist on Voluntary Standard-level data from a supplier with no more than 1,000 employees. Customers outside CSRD scope can still ask for anything as a commercial condition.

- **UK listed customers.** UK SRS reporting begins with periods from 1 January 2027 and first reports in 2028. Expect supplier emissions requests to rise through 2027 and 2028.

- **Public buyers.** Central government contracts over £5m a year need a published carbon reduction plan. NI departments score social value at 10% or more and may include carbon.

- **NI councils and public bodies** have filed their first emissions baselines. As they start to measure procurement emissions, supplier data requests will follow.

- **The formats are converging:** one EU format (VSME), one UK format (UK SRS and the GHG Protocol) and one public-sector format (the carbon reduction plan).

## Practical examples

- **All 40 NI public bodies reported.** Every specified body met its first duty under the 2024 Reporting Bodies Regulations, and two more reported voluntarily. Minister Andrew Muir said the public sector has "a crucial leadership role" ([DAERA, 12 Jun 2026](https://www.daera-ni.gov.uk/news/public-bodies-celebrated-milestone-achievement-climate-change-reporting)).

- **NILGOSC reports without being made to.** The council pension fund has published climate disclosures against TCFD voluntarily since 2021. Its latest report covers the year to 31 March 2026 ([NILGOSC, page checked 28 Oct 2026](https://nilgosc.org.uk/pension-fund/being-a-responsible-investor/climate-risk/)).

- **SECR cost four times the forecast.** The official review put ongoing compliance costs at £140m a year, against £31m predicted. The average firm spent £7,100 a year, against £2,300 forecast ([DESNZ post-implementation review, 26 May 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a0dbf75faac4bc0b0e906af/2026-streamlined-energy-and-carbon-reporting-post-implementation-review.pdf)).

- **The EU's SME format is now law.** The Voluntary Standard, Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/1560, was published on 21 September 2026. Its Basic Module covers energy, emissions, workforce and governance metrics; it is the minimum for any firm that uses it ([Ashurst, 21 Sep 2026 update](https://www.ashurstperkinscoie.com/en/insights/eu-adopts-simplified-esrs-and-sustainability-reporting-standard-for-voluntary-use/)).

- **NI manufacturers are pooling the work.** Invest NI's Industrial Decarbonisation for Northern Ireland network brings together more than 200 firms, councils and government bodies. It offers a shared roadmap and a sustainability toolkit ([Invest NI, 9 Jan 2025](https://www.investni.com/media-centre/features/invest-northern-ireland-helping-firms-seize-opportunities-in-global-green-economy)).

## Recommendations

1.  **DESNZ: in the promised SECR and ESOS consultation, adopt a template that matches the VSME Basic Module and PPN 006.** One format cuts repeat work.

2.  **Department for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade: consider a UK value-chain cap like the EU's** in the corporate reporting consultation (closes 30 Nov 2026).

3.  **NI Department of Finance: issue guidance accepting a PPN 006-style carbon reduction plan in place of bespoke questionnaires.**

4.  **DAERA: when guiding public bodies on Scope 3, point them to supplier VSME and carbon reduction plan data,** to avoid a new council-by-council survey burden.

5.  **Firms: build one data set in 2026–27:** Scope 1 and 2, the five PPN 006 Scope 3 categories, energy use, and Basic Module workforce and policy data. Answer every request from it.

6.  **Firms: add an estimate for purchased goods (Category 1).** It is the number customers will ask for next.

7.  **Firms with EU customers: use the cap.** From FY2027 you can politely decline requests beyond the Voluntary Standard.

8.  **Councils: publish a short supplier note on which formats you accept,** to cut costs for SMEs bidding locally.
