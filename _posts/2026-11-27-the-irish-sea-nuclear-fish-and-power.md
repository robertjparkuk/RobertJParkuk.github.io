Sellafield's mark on the Irish Sea is real, measured and, today, small. The bigger radiation dose in Northern Ireland comes out of the ground as radon. Our fish and shellfish are good food, and we export most of the best of it. And a small reactor would be low-carbon but, for now, has no published price and no UK disposal route.

## What the evidence says

- **The sea.** Sellafield adds about 7 µSv a year to Northern Ireland seafood eaters, under 1% of the public limit. Doses have fallen about tenfold since the 1980s. The open risks are the old waste stores and an accident, not routine discharges.
- **The ground.** Radon gives the average UK resident about 1,300 µSv a year. As a dose comparison, fixing high-radon homes would cut more dose in NI than any change to Sellafield's routine discharges.
- **The catch.** UK boats landed 17,720 tonnes worth £38.1 million into NI ports in 2025, up from £27.6 million in 2021. Dublin Bay prawns earned £20.1 million of it. Kilkeel landed the most value; Belfast the most weight, almost all herring and mackerel.
- **The plate.** NI adults eat 30–60 g of oily fish a week against the 140 g advised. Herring, at about 61p a kilo at the quay, is the cheapest fish we land.
- **The power.** One 470 MWe Rolls-Royce reactor would make nearly eight times the metered electricity used in Mid and East Antrim, Larne's council area, in 2024. The 25 MWe advanced modular reactor is the size that fits a town with its industry.

## Our view

Northern Ireland and the Republic should plan for, and champion, small and advanced modular reactors as part of one energy policy built on security, interdependence, independence and storage, hand in hand with biomethane and new technologies such as transparent solar PV. Prepare now, with a grid study, siting screen and skills audit; commit when there is a real price, a waste-disposal host and a grid case; and work on an all-island route. The briefing sets out the case against at its strongest, so readers can test that view.

## What to do

- **MLAs:** revisit the Assembly's 2007 position on Sellafield and take evidence on reactors before May 2027.
- **Councils:** use radon data in housing work, and respond to the Wylfa consultations, which are open to anyone wherever they live.
- **Manufacturers:** plan your 2027 imports of iron and steel, aluminium, cement, fertiliser and hydrogen before UK CBAM starts on 1 January 2027. The UK intends to exempt EU-origin goods; that is not yet law.
- **Everyone:** test your home for radon, and eat more herring.
