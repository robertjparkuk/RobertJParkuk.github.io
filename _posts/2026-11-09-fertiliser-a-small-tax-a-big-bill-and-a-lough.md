*Published early, on 9 November 2026. I'll update this piece in December, with a refresh of the [NI Agriculture and Land Carbon dashboard]({{ '/tools/dashboards/ni-agriculture-land-carbon.html' | relative_url }}), once the first UK CBAM rates and the outcome of the 20 November UK–EU summit are known.*

## The argument in brief

On 26 October 2026 Reform UK published a 65-point alternative Budget, two days before the Prime Minister's first Budget. Point 7 pledges to stop the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM), and names "the fertiliser tax" as part of it. The Conservatives made the same promise in April. On 19 October the government confirmed that the UK CBAM starts on 1 January 2027 regardless. The Budget of 28 October 2026 made no fertiliser-specific CBAM change.

The row is loud, but the tax itself is small. HMRC's illustrative 2027 rate for fertiliser is £1.45 per tonne of CO2e. On ammonium nitrate (AN) that comes to about £3.77 a tonne, against a market price of around £480 a tonne in October. What actually sets the bill is gas prices, disrupted shipping through the Strait of Hormuz since February 2026, EU tariffs on Russian supply and the fact that the UK no longer makes its own ammonia.

In Northern Ireland the binding constraint is the nutrient rules, not CBAM. The new Nutrients Action Programme (NAP) and Lough Neagh will shape how much fertiliser NI farms buy and spread far more than any carbon border levy. The CBAM charge is low only while free allowances last, and it will rise as they taper. The political fight is over a cost that has barely started.

*This piece sets out the arguments on each side; the recommendations are the author's. Facts checked to 30 October 2026.*

## The lifecycle at a glance

The UK CBAM touches fertiliser once, at the border (stage 4), at an illustrative £1.45/tCO2e. The bigger losses to air and water happen at stages 6 and 7, which the NAP governs and the border charge does not reach.

- **Scrap or pause.** Conservatives and Reform would remove the whole UK CBAM. The unions ask only for a delay to the fertiliser element.

- **Leakage logic.** Labour argues CBAM matches what domestic producers pay. That is weak for ammonia, because the UK has almost no ammonia production left to protect.

- **NI has two fights.** At Westminster the fight is CBAM. At Stormont it is the NAP and Lough Neagh.

- **Support or rules.** The UFU and Sinn Féin want cost support now. DAERA points to nutrient efficiency.

## Where the pillars compete

- **Food security against water quality (S against E).** NI farms carried a phosphorus surplus of 8,729 tonnes in 2024. Modelling attributes 61% of the phosphorus reaching Lough Neagh to agriculture ([FactCheckNI, 2025](https://factcheckni.org/articles/lough-neagh-crisis-does-most-of-the-phosphorus-in-the-lough-come-from-agriculture/)). Tighter rules protect the lough but squeeze margins in a year of high input costs.

- **Farm incomes against nitrous oxide (S against E).** NI agriculture emitted 5.8 MtCO2e in 2024, 1.2 Mt of it N2O. Emissions from manufactured fertiliser rose 16.9% from 2023 to 2024 ([DAERA bulletin, 9 Jun 2026](https://datavis.nisra.gov.uk/daera/northern-ireland-greenhouse-gas-inventory-1990-2024-statistical-bulletin.html)). Cost support that cuts the price of fertiliser also encourages more spreading.

- **Domestic production against imported low-carbon ammonia (E against E).** Billingham's ammonia plant closed on gas and carbon costs, moving the emissions offshore. CBAM now applies to imports with no UK plant left to shield. Green ammonia from abroad could cut emissions faster than rebuilding at home, at a cost to supply security.

- **Recycled nutrients against contamination (E against E).** Digestate and biosolids replace mineral fertiliser; 87% of UK biosolids go to farmland ([CIWEM, Jul 2025](https://www.ciwem.org/news/should-we-be-putting-sewage-sludge-on-land-anymore)). Even sewers serving only homes carry PFAS, and the sludge rules date from 1989.

- **Ammonia against climate (E against E).** LESSE and protected urea cut ammonia, and 97% of NI Special Areas of Conservation exceed ammonia critical levels (DAERA, 27 Jul 2026). Retaining more nitrogen can raise N2O losses unless the amount spread falls too.

## What it means for Northern Ireland firms and councils

- **Merchants and blenders.** The NAP proposes point-of-sale checks: a soil test and nutrient plan before selling chemical P for grassland, plus training. Plan for this before 2027.

- **Importers.** Anyone bringing in ammonia, nitric acid or nitrogen fertiliser from outside the UK must account for UK CBAM from 1 Jan 2027. The first annual return and payment are due by 31 May 2028.

- **Agri-food processors.** Supplier emissions data matters for EU customers and your own Scope 3. Fertiliser N2O is a large part of dairy, beef and grain footprints.

- **Councils.** Expect more LESSE and slurry-store conditions in planning, and more questions on biosolids and PFAS.

- **ETS linkage.** A UK–EU link is reported as "increasingly likely" at the November summit (Carbon Pulse, 14 Oct 2026), which could ease CBAM friction across the Irish border.

## Practical examples

- **Grants for slurry kit.** DAERA's £40m Sustainable Farming Investment Scheme offered 40% of the cost, up to £25,000 a business. Its first window ran from 21 July to 3 September 2026, with LESSE applications ranked separately ([DAERA](https://www.daera-ni.gov.uk/articles/sustainable-farming-investment-scheme-key-information); [Farmers Journal, 15 Jul 2026](https://www.farmersjournal.ie/more/northern-ireland/grant-funds-driven-by-lesse-demand-923887)). Farms with 200 or more cattle units were excluded because they must already use LESSE.

- **An NI machinery maker in the scheme.** Fleming Agri listed its 7.5m and 9.2m trailing shoes and dribble bars as SFIS-eligible LESSE kit before the scheme opened on 21 July 2026 ([Farming Life](https://www.farminglife.com/business/fleming-agri-products-eligible-under-daera-sfis-2026-scheme-8802708)). Nutrient rules create orders for local manufacturers as well as costs for farms.

- **Billingham stops making ammonia.** On 25 July 2023 CF Fertilisers proposed closing its idle Billingham ammonia plant, citing UK gas and carbon costs, with up to 38 redundancies. It kept making ammonium nitrate from imported ammonia ([CF Industries](https://cfindustries.com/newsroom/2023/billingham-ammonia-plant)).

- **Soil data at scale.** The Soil Nutrient Health Scheme reached 92.5% of NI farm businesses by September 2025. It is a condition of the Farm Sustainability Payment from 2026 ([AFBI, 18 Sep 2025](https://www.afbini.gov.uk/news/soil-nutrient-health-scheme-zone-4-uptake)). Merchants asked to check soil tests under the new NAP will have data to work from.

- **What the charge comes to.** HMRC's [draft default values](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/check-the-default-values-for-the-carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism-cbam/fertilisers-default-values) (16 Oct 2026) give ammonium nitrate (CN 3102 30 90) 2.600 tCO2e per tonne and CAN at up to 28% nitrogen, the 27% product sold here (3102 40 10), 2.120. At the illustrative £1.45 rate that is about £3.77 a tonne of AN and £3.07 a tonne of CAN, against £480–485 delivered ([Farmers Weekly, 19 Oct 2026](https://www.fwi.co.uk/business/markets-and-trends/input-prices/government-presses-on-with-fertiliser-import-tax)). Fertiliser defaults carry no UK mark-up. A 100-tonne merchant order of AN would carry about £377 of CBAM in 2027 (author's arithmetic). The importer pays: usually the merchant or co-op that brings the fertiliser in, not the farm, which sees it in the price. Origin decides, not route: urea made in Egypt and bought through an Irish merchant is non-UK origin either way, and fertiliser made in the EU and delivered into NI counts as an import too (Finance Act 2026, s.144(5)(a)). Each 8-digit code has its own default value, so check the code on the invoice.

## Recommendations

1.  **HM Treasury: publish the free-allocation taper for fertiliser to 2034.** A low starting rate gives false comfort; farms need the full path.

2.  **HM Treasury: keep the fertiliser CBAM but recycle revenue into nutrient-efficiency grants.** This meets the competitiveness complaint without reopening leakage.

3.  **DAERA: publish the NAP economic impact assessment before making the regulations.** The UUP and UFU have asked, and it would steady the consensus.

4.  **DAERA and DfE: collect data on fertiliser stocks.** Without it, supply risk cannot be judged.

5.  **UK and EU negotiators: secure mutual CBAM exemption as part of ETS linkage.** This would remove the UK charge on EU-made fertiliser coming into NI from Ireland, and the EU charge on GB-made goods going the other way.

6.  **DAERA and NI Water: set PFAS testing for biosolids before their use grows.**

7.  **Firms: map CBAM-covered imports by CN code now.** Ammonia (2814), nitrogen fertilisers (3102) and mixed fertilisers (3105) are in scope.

8.  **Farms and advisers: switch to protected urea and LESSE ahead of the 2028 tier.** The first SFIS window (closed 3 Sep 2026) paid 40% of the cost, up to £25,000; more rounds are planned.
