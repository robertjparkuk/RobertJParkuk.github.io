## The argument in brief

Both carbon border charges will apply to UK trade from January 2027. The EU CBAM has applied to imports since 1 January 2026: EU importers owe the EU carbon price on UK steel, aluminium, cement and fertiliser, minus any carbon price effectively paid in the UK, net of free allowances. The UK CBAM starts on 1 January 2027. Goods made in Northern Ireland are outside the EU CBAM and inside the UK one.

It works like a tariff on carbon content; in law it is a tax collected by HMRC. It is charged only on imports and varies with the country and plant of origin. For steel from the US, Brazil, China or India it adds about £19 a tonne on HMRC's 2027 illustration, with little or no relief, and rises as free allocation ends.

There are three broad options, and each has its advocates:

- **Keep** the UK CBAM and UK ETS as they are, with free allowances phased out as the border charge takes over. This is the Government's position.
- **Link** the UK ETS with the EU's, which could lead to mutual exemptions from each other's CBAM. The Government is negotiating this, and a draft linking agreement was before the Council of the EU's working party on the UK on 10 November 2026.
- **Scrap** both, as the Conservatives (April 2026) and Reform UK (26 October 2026) have pledged. They point to industrial electricity prices among Europe's highest, refinery and blast-furnace closures, and a border charge that raises costs for farmers and fabricators.

This piece sets out what each option would mean for Northern Ireland firms, and the case each side makes. Facts checked to 30 October 2026 and updated on 11 November 2026.

## Who decides what

The UK CBAM is a Westminster tax that any UK government could repeal on its own. The UK ETS is jointly owned by four governments, so scrapping it is a harder job than the pledges suggest.

On the EU side, the Commission negotiates the ETS link, and the Council of the EU, under the Irish Presidency until 31 December 2026, chairs member states on the CBAM downstream extension and the EU ETS review ([Irish Presidency](https://irish-presidency.consilium.europa.eu/en/)).

This matters for the abolition pledges. A Conservative or Reform government could end the UK CBAM in a Finance Bill. Ending the UK ETS without Edinburgh, Cardiff and Belfast would most likely need Westminster primary legislation overriding a joint scheme, which would raise and Sewel-style legislative consent questions in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. In Northern Ireland it would change little for electricity, which stays in the EU ETS either way.

## Scrapping the UK CBAM and ETS: the case for and against

Reform UK would abolish the UK ETS and stop the UK CBAM (point 6 and point 7 of its 65-point plan of 26 October 2026, which names "the fertiliser tax"). In February 2026 Richard Tice told POLITICO the party would scrap "all these levies and green taxes", including CBAM. On 1 April 2026 Kemi Badenoch pledged that a Conservative government would scrap the UK ETS, the Carbon Price Support and the UK CBAM; the party restated the pledge in June.

**The best case for scrapping, as its supporters make it**

- **Costs.** Carbon prices feed into energy bills and into the cost of steel, cement and fertiliser. Remove them and input costs fall at once.

- **The fertiliser tax.** UK farms depend on imported fertiliser, so supporters see the CBAM as a direct cost on food production.

- **Closures.** Blast furnaces and refineries have closed as carbon costs rose. Critics say this makes the UK Europe's dearest place to make basic materials.

- **Paperwork.** The CBAM is a new reporting system for small importers, and its live rates were still unpublished two months before launch.

**The case against scrapping**

- **The EU charge stays.** EU CBAM deducts carbon prices paid at origin. With no UK ETS there is nothing to deduct, so UK exporters pay the full EU price into the EU budget.

- **No link, no exemption.** A linked ETS is the only route to exempting UK goods from the EU CBAM. Abolition closes it. Critics of linking reply that it means following ETS rules the UK does not vote on.

- **Trade diversion.** High-carbon goods priced out of the EU look for an uncharged market. Without a UK CBAM, the UK becomes that market. Steel quotas manage volume, not carbon.

- **Investment.** Carbon capture, hydrogen and electric-arc projects are financed against a carbon price. The Carbon Capture and Storage Association and UKSIF warned in June 2026 that abolition would deter investment.

- **The diagnosis.** Industrial power prices are driven chiefly by gas prices and levies. The Government is already ending the Carbon Price Support in 2028 and cutting levies through the British Industrial Competitiveness Scheme (BICS). The Budget of 28 October 2026 also funds the NI Executive to set up its own version.

## How the UK CBAM charges importers from outside Europe

**How the charge is worked out.** Liability = embodied emissions × CBAM rate − carbon price relief. HM Treasury sets the rate quarterly from the UK ETS price. Relief covers carbon price actually paid abroad, with a verifier's form from the producing installation; free allowances count as zero cost, and rebates and refunds reduce relief ([SI 2026/809](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/809/made); [HMRC policy summary](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism-cbam-policy-summary/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism-cbam-policy-summary)).

HMRC's illustrative 2027 rate for iron and steel is £9.69/tCO₂e ([GOV.UK, 16 October 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/illustrative-cbam-rates-and-worked-example-for-carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism-cbam/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism-cbam-illustrative-cbam-rates-and-worked-example)). It starts from the £58.15 average UK ETS auction price for July to September 2026, scaled down because UK producers still get most allowances free. A tonne of blast-furnace steel embodying about 2 tCO₂e would carry roughly £19 before relief. The first live rates are published by 1 January 2027, and the charge grows each year to 2035.

Two design points matter for buyers:

- **UK defaults are not set by country until 2029.** For 2027 and 2028 the UK uses a single default value per commodity code, with a 10% mark-up (none for fertiliser), and moves to country-specific values from 2029 ([HMRC draft notice](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism-force-of-law-notice-and-reference-document/the-carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism-default-values-force-of-law-notice-draft--4)). - **Being on the list is not the same as paying a carbon price.** Most Asian schemes still give steelmakers free allowances, so steel from the US, Brazil, China and India will carry close to the full UK charge.

## AI as a complement to industrial policy

CBAM is a data problem before it is a tax. Every charge, deduction and exemption depends on the emissions embedded in a tonne of product, traced through suppliers. AI tools make that work cheaper.

- **For firms.** Draft and chase supplier questionnaires, map product catalogues to CBAM commodity codes, and run first-pass emissions calculations and 2027–2030 cost scenarios for a verifier to check.

- **For government.** Scan HMRC import data for transhipment and odd use of default values, target recycled revenue at the most exposed plants, and offer a plain-English CBAM assistant on GOV.UK and nibusinessinfo.

The limits are real. AI drafts; it does not verify. Every figure on a declaration needs a named person, an audit trail and, where required, an accredited verifier.

## Where the pillars compete

- **Emissions at home against jobs at home (E against S).** Billingham stopped making ammonia in 2023, citing gas and carbon costs, with up to 38 redundancies. UK emissions fell, and the ammonia is now made abroad ([CF Industries, 25 Jul 2023](https://cfindustries.com/newsroom/2023/billingham-ammonia-plant)).

- **Territorial against global emissions (E against E).** Port Talbot's last blast furnace closed in 2024, cutting UK emissions years before its replacement furnace runs. Without a UK CBAM, the imports that fill the gap carry no UK carbon price.

- **Food costs against a carbon price (S against E).** The NFU asked for the CBAM to be postponed and reviewed after 12 months (17 Apr 2026). A charge on imported fertiliser shields few UK producers, since the UK has almost no ammonia plant left.

- **Cheaper power against a carbon signal (S against E).** Ending the Carbon Price Support in April 2028 cuts industrial power bills. It also removes the extra carbon cost on gas-fired generation in GB.

- **Fair treatment against shipping emissions (S against E).** The maritime ETS has covered GB–NI ferries since 1 July 2026, on 50% of voyage emissions, but exempts Scottish island routes.

- **Sovereignty against a stronger price (G against E).** Linking with the EU ETS would pull the UK carbon price towards the EU's and cut emissions faster. Critics say the cost is following rules the UK does not vote on.

## What it means for Northern Ireland firms and councils

Keeping, linking or scrapping would affect Northern Ireland differently from Great Britain. Goods made in NI are outside the EU CBAM: the European Commission says "Goods produced and/or released for free circulation in Northern Ireland are not subject to CBAM" ([CBAM Q&A, Q 7.14](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/document/download/013fa763-5dce-4726-a204-69fec04d5ce2_en?filename=CBAM_Questions+and+Answers.pdf)). The UK CBAM applies in NI from January 2027, including to steel, aluminium, cement, fertiliser and hydrogen brought in from Ireland or the rest of the EU (Finance Act 2026, s.144(5)(a)); HMRC has not yet published guidance on how those movements, which carry no customs declaration, are captured. NI power stations already sit in the EU ETS because of the all-island electricity market.

- The position to plan around. Under the rules as they stand, goods made in Northern Ireland are outside the EU CBAM and inside the UK CBAM. Whether that helps depends on what you make and where you sell. It applies only to goods genuinely made in NI: GB-made goods moved through NI to the EU are still subject to the EU CBAM (Commission Q&A, Q 7.14).

- Scrapping would change little for NI power and more for NI industry. Abolishing the UK ETS would leave NI generators in the EU ETS, so NI electricity prices would barely move. NI importers would no longer face a UK CBAM charge, and GB-made goods sold into the EU would carry the full EU charge with no UK carbon price to deduct.

- Cheaper power is now partly an NI decision. The Budget of 28 October 2026 funds the Executive to set up its own version of BICS for heavy electricity users in manufacturing. No amount was given, and design and timing are for the Executive.

- Refinery products may join the UK CBAM. The government is consulting until 20 January 2027. NI firms that import fuels or other refinery products from outside the UK should check their exposure and respond.

- The link matters. If the 20 November summit leads to mutual exemptions, the difference between GB and NI firms narrows, and EU-made goods coming into NI would no longer carry the UK charge once both sides have legislated. Firms should plan for both outcomes.

- Councils and public bodies. Capital projects buying steel or cement from outside the UK will carry the UK CBAM from January 2027, on top of steel tariffs. Councils can also create demand for low-carbon producers by setting embodied-carbon limits in tenders.

The [CBAM Exposure Tracker]({{ '/tools/dashboards/cbam-exposure-tracker.html' | relative_url }}) sets out the dates, prices and the common NI situations.

## Practical examples

- **Invest NI's CBAM toolkit.** nibusinessinfo offers six free tools: a scope decision tree, a CN code look-up, an emissions calculator, an evidence pack, a supplier data request and a 2027 cost modeller ([updated 6 Mar 2026](https://www.nibusinessinfo.co.uk/content/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism-cbam-northern-ireland-businesses)).

- **HMRC's cement worked example.** £58.15 × \[1 − (87.1% × 97.5%)\] gives £8.78/tCO₂e for 2027 ([HMRC, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/illustrative-cbam-rates-and-worked-example-for-carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism-cbam/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism-cbam-illustrative-cbam-rates-and-worked-example)). As the 97.5% factor falls each year, the rate climbs towards the full UK ETS price.

- **Steel quotas cut by 60%.** Since 1 July 2026, steel imports above quotas set 60% below the old safeguard pay a 50% tariff ([written statement, 19 Mar 2026](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-statements/1892806)). An NI fabricator buying out-of-quota steel from outside the UK will face both charges from January 2027.

- **Port Talbot's arc furnace.** The £1.25bn furnace, backed by a £500m government grant in 2023, has slipped to late 2028 or early 2029 because of grid delays. Tata is reported to be seeking more support ([Business Today, citing Sky News, 19 Sep 2026](https://businesstoday.in/latest/corporate/story/tata-steel-seeks-fresh-uk-government-funding-as-port-talbot-project-faces-delays-556642-2026-09-19)).

- **Ferries to Northern Ireland.** The UK ETS has covered domestic shipping, including GB–NI ferries, since 1 July 2026, after approval in all five legislatures ([Lords Library](https://lordslibrary.parliament.uk/greenhouse-gas-emissions-trading-scheme-amendment-extension-to-maritime-activities-order-2026/)). Operators may pass the cost on to NI importers.

## Recommendations

These recommendations are the author's. They do not depend on whether the instruments are kept, linked or scrapped.

1.  **HM Treasury: publish the free-allocation taper for CBAM sectors to 2035, and say how CBAM receipts are used.** Firms need the full cost path, not just the 2027 rate.

2.  **UK Government: set out after the 20 November 2026 summit how GB–NI and NI–EU trade will be treated under both CBAMs.** Include what still needs a legal act on each side, and when.

3.  **HMRC: publish NI-specific guidance with Invest NI before the first live rates.** Cover goods arriving in NI from Ireland or the rest of the EU, which carry no customs declaration, and goods moving on from NI to GB.

4.  **Department for the Economy: use the Budget funding (28 Oct 2026) to set up NI's own BICS-style scheme.** Publish its amount, eligibility and start date.

5.  **Councils and public bodies: set embodied-carbon limits for steel and cement in tenders.** This creates demand for low-carbon producers whatever happens to the border charge.

6.  **NI importers: map suppliers by country and route, request verified emissions data and add CBAM clauses to 2027 contracts.** If you buy fuels or other refinery products from outside the UK, respond to the consultation by 20 January 2027.
