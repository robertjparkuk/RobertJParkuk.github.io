## The argument in brief

Scrapping the UK CBAM and the UK ETS would not cut the carbon cost on British exports to Europe. It would move the money. Since 1 January 2026, EU importers have owed the EU carbon price on UK steel, aluminium, cement and fertiliser, minus any carbon price paid in the UK. Abolish the UK price and that deduction falls to zero. UK exporters still pay, and the receipts go to Brussels instead of the Treasury. Abolish the UK CBAM too, and Britain becomes the obvious outlet for high-carbon steel the EU now charges for.

CBAM works as a tariff on carbon. Governments call it a tax to keep it clear of trade law. It is charged only on imports and varies with the country and plant of origin. For steel from the US, Brazil, China or India it adds about £19 a tonne on HMRC's 2027 illustration, with little or no relief, and rises as free allocation ends.

Reform UK's critique still deserves a hearing. Its 65-point plan of 26 October 2026 pledges to abolish the UK ETS (point 6) and to "cease the implementation of the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism, including the fertiliser tax" (point 7). It points at real problems: industrial electricity prices among Europe's highest, refinery and blast-furnace closures, and a border charge that raises costs for farmers and fabricators. Those problems stem mostly from energy prices and policy design. Abolishing the carbon price does little to fix them.

The better course is to keep both instruments and engineer them for industry. Recycle the revenue into domestic production. Pair the border charge with low-carbon procurement and cheaper electricity. Extend it downstream so leakage does not simply move to finished goods. Link with the EU market on terms that protect UK industry.

*This piece sets out the arguments on each side; the recommendations are the author's. Facts checked to 30 October 2026.*

## Who decides what

The UK CBAM is a Westminster tax that any UK government could repeal on its own. The UK ETS is jointly owned by four governments, so scrapping it is a harder job than the pledges suggest.

This matters for the abolition pledges. A Conservative or Reform government could end the UK CBAM in a Finance Bill. Ending the UK ETS without Edinburgh, Cardiff and Belfast would most likely need Westminster primary legislation overriding a joint scheme, which would test the Sewel convention in Scotland and Wales. In Northern Ireland it would change little for electricity, which stays in the EU ETS either way.

## Reform UK's policy: the case for and against

Reform would abolish the UK ETS and stop the UK CBAM as part of a wider retreat from net zero rules (plan of 26 October 2026). In February 2026 Richard Tice told POLITICO the party would scrap "all these levies and green taxes", including CBAM. On 1 April 2026 Kemi Badenoch pledged that a Conservative government would scrap the UK ETS, the Carbon Price Support and the UK CBAM; the party restated the pledge in June.

**The best case for scrapping, as its supporters make it**

- **Costs.** Carbon prices feed into energy bills and into the cost of steel, cement and fertiliser. Remove them and input costs fall at once.

- **The fertiliser tax.** UK farms depend on imported fertiliser, so supporters see the CBAM as a direct cost on food production.

- **Closures.** Blast furnaces and refineries have closed as carbon costs rose. Critics say this makes the UK Europe's dearest place to make basic materials.

- **Paperwork.** The CBAM is a new reporting system for small importers, and its live rates were still unpublished two months before launch.

**The case against scrapping**

- **The EU charge stays.** EU CBAM deducts carbon prices paid at origin. With no UK ETS there is nothing to deduct, so UK exporters pay the full EU price into the EU budget.

- **No link, no exemption.** A linked ETS is the only route to exempting UK goods from the EU CBAM. Abolition closes it.

- **Trade diversion.** High-carbon goods priced out of the EU look for an uncharged market. Without a UK CBAM, Britain becomes that market. Steel quotas manage volume, not carbon.

- **Investment.** Carbon capture, hydrogen and electric-arc projects are financed against a carbon price. The Carbon Capture and Storage Association and UKSIF warned in June 2026 that abolition would deter investment.

- **The diagnosis.** Industrial power prices are driven chiefly by gas prices and levies. The Government is already ending the Carbon Price Support in 2028 and cutting levies through the British Industrial Competitiveness Scheme (BICS). The Budget of 28 October 2026 also funds the NI Executive to set up its own version.

**Author's verdict.** Reform is right that carbon policy was designed for emissions targets more than for industry. Abolition does not fix that. With an EU CBAM in place, abolition hands the carbon rent to Brussels and opens the UK to diverted imports. Redesigning the tools is the stronger answer.

The strongest objections to this verdict deserve a hearing. Linking means regulatory alignment. A redesigned scheme still raises material prices. Recycled revenue depends on Treasury discipline that past green levies have not always shown.

## CBAM is a tariff: what importers from outside Europe will pay

**How the charge is worked out.** Liability = embodied emissions × CBAM rate − carbon price relief. HM Treasury sets the rate quarterly from the UK ETS price. Relief covers carbon price actually paid abroad, with evidence; free allowances, rebates and refunds do not count ([Clifford Chance primer, September 2026](https://www.cliffordchance.com/content/dam/cliffordchance/briefings/2026/09/2026-UK-CBAM-primer.pdf)).

HMRC's illustrative 2027 rate for iron and steel is £9.69/tCO₂e ([GOV.UK, 16 October 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/illustrative-cbam-rates-and-worked-example-for-carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism-cbam/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism-cbam-illustrative-cbam-rates-and-worked-example)). It starts from the £58.15 average UK ETS auction price for July to September 2026, scaled down because UK producers still get most allowances free. A tonne of blast-furnace steel embodying about 2 tCO₂e would carry roughly £19 before relief. The first live rates come before the end of 2026, and the charge grows each year to 2035.

The list of 16 recognised schemes is a draft and not exhaustive ([EUROMETAL, 3 September 2026](https://eurometal.net/uk-recognizes-16-overseas-carbon-pricing-schemes-for-cbam-relief/)). Two design points matter for buyers:

- **Defaults are not set by country.** The UK uses a single default value per commodity code. A cleaner supplier, such as an electric-arc mill, only wins if it supplies verified actual data. The EU, by contrast, sets country-level defaults.

- **Being on the list is not the same as paying a carbon price.** Most Asian schemes still give steelmakers free allowances, so steel from the US, Brazil, China and India will carry close to the full UK charge.

## Engineering CBAM and the ETS for reshoring

A carbon border charge protects the home market; it doesn't build anything. To reindustrialise, government has to pair it with demand, cheaper power and capital. These are the levers, roughly in order of impact.

Taken together, this turns the ETS and CBAM from a cost regime into an industrial strategy: the carbon price funds the plant, procurement buys its output, and the border stops it being undercut.

## AI as a complement to industrial policy

CBAM is a data problem before it is a tax. Every charge, deduction and exemption depends on the emissions embedded in a tonne of product, traced through suppliers. AI tools make that work cheaper.

- **For firms.** Draft and chase supplier questionnaires, map product catalogues to CBAM commodity codes, and run first-pass emissions calculations and 2027–2030 cost scenarios for a verifier to check.

- **For government.** Scan HMRC import data for transhipment and odd use of default values, target recycled revenue at the most exposed plants, and offer a plain-English CBAM assistant on GOV.UK and nibusinessinfo.

The limits are real. AI drafts; it does not verify. Every figure on a declaration needs a named person, an audit trail and, where required, an accredited verifier.

## Where the pillars compete

- **Emissions at home against jobs at home (E against S).** Billingham stopped making ammonia in 2023, citing gas and carbon costs, with up to 38 redundancies. UK emissions fell, and the ammonia is now made abroad ([CF Industries, 25 Jul 2023](https://cfindustries.com/newsroom/2023/billingham-ammonia-plant)).

- **Territorial against global emissions (E against E).** Port Talbot's last blast furnace closed in 2024, cutting UK emissions years before its replacement furnace runs. Without a UK CBAM, the imports that fill the gap carry no UK carbon price.

- **Food costs against a carbon price (S against E).** The NFU asked for the CBAM to be postponed and reviewed after 12 months (17 Apr 2026). A charge on imported fertiliser shields few UK producers, since Britain has almost no ammonia plant left.

- **Cheaper power against a carbon signal (S against E).** Ending the Carbon Price Support in April 2028 cuts industrial power bills. It also removes the extra carbon cost on gas-fired generation in GB.

- **Fair treatment against shipping emissions (S against E).** The maritime ETS has covered GB–NI ferries since 1 July 2026 but exempts Scottish island routes.

- **Sovereignty against a stronger price (G against E).** Linking with the EU ETS would pull the UK carbon price towards the EU's and cut emissions faster. The cost is following rules the UK does not vote on.

## What it means for Northern Ireland firms and councils

Northern Ireland gains most from keeping the instruments and stands to lose most from scrapping them. Goods made in NI are outside the EU CBAM. The UK CBAM applies in NI from January 2027. NI power stations already sit in the EU ETS because of the all-island electricity market.

- A dual-market advantage. An NI plant can sell into the EU without an EU CBAM charge and into GB under UK rules. That is a strong inward-investment pitch for low-carbon steel fabrication, cement products and fertiliser blending.

- Scrapping changes little for NI power and a lot for NI industry. Abolishing the UK ETS would leave NI generators in the EU ETS, so NI electricity prices would barely move. NI importers would lose UK CBAM protection against diverted high-carbon imports arriving through GB.

- Cheaper power is now partly an NI decision. The Budget of 28 October 2026 funds the Executive to set up its own version of BICS for heavy electricity users in manufacturing. No amount was given, and design and timing are for the Executive.

- Refinery products may join the UK CBAM. The government is consulting until 20 January 2027. NI firms that import fuels or other refinery products from outside the UK should check their exposure and respond.

- The link matters. If the 20 November summit delivers mutual exemptions, GB firms move closer to NI's position and NI's edge narrows. Firms should plan for both outcomes.

- Councils and public bodies. Capital projects buying steel or cement from outside the UK will carry the UK CBAM from January 2027, on top of steel tariffs. Councils can also create demand for low-carbon producers by setting embodied-carbon limits in tenders.

The [CBAM Exposure Tracker](https://robertjpark.co.uk/tools/dashboards/cbam-exposure-tracker.html) sets out the dates, prices and the six common NI situations.

## Practical examples

- **Invest NI's CBAM toolkit.** nibusinessinfo offers six free tools: a scope decision tree, a CN code look-up, an emissions calculator, an evidence pack, a supplier data request and a 2027 cost modeller ([updated 6 Mar 2026](https://www.nibusinessinfo.co.uk/content/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism-cbam-northern-ireland-businesses)).

- **HMRC's cement worked example.** £58.15 × \[1 − (87.1% × 97.5%)\] gives £8.78/tCO₂e for 2027 ([HMRC, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/illustrative-cbam-rates-and-worked-example-for-carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism-cbam/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism-cbam-illustrative-cbam-rates-and-worked-example)). As the 97.5% factor falls each year, the rate climbs towards the full UK ETS price.

- **Steel quotas cut by 60%.** Since 1 July 2026, steel imports above quotas set 60% below the old safeguard pay a 50% tariff ([written statement, 19 Mar 2026](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-statements/1892806)). An NI fabricator buying out-of-quota steel from outside the UK will face both charges from January 2027.

- **Port Talbot's arc furnace.** The £1.25bn furnace, backed by a £500m government grant in 2023, has slipped to late 2028 or early 2029 because of grid delays. Tata is reported to be seeking more support ([Business Today, citing Sky News, 19 Sep 2026](https://businesstoday.in/latest/corporate/story/tata-steel-seeks-fresh-uk-government-funding-as-port-talbot-project-faces-delays-556642-2026-09-19)).

- **Ferries to Northern Ireland.** The UK ETS has covered domestic shipping, including GB–NI ferries, since 1 July 2026, after approval in all five legislatures ([Lords Library](https://lordslibrary.parliament.uk/greenhouse-gas-emissions-trading-scheme-amendment-extension-to-maritime-activities-order-2026/)). Operators may pass the cost on to NI importers.

## Recommendations

These recommendations are the author's.

1.  **HM Treasury: keep the UK CBAM and ring-fence its receipts.** Put CBAM revenue and newly auctioned allowances into industrial decarbonisation, carbon contracts for difference and grid connections.

2.  **UK Government: secure the ETS link with mutual CBAM exemptions.** Use the 20 November 2026 summit to set out how GB–NI and NI–EU trade will be treated.

3.  **HMRC: set default values high enough to reward verified data.** Publish NI-specific guidance with Invest NI before the first live rates.

4.  **Department for the Economy: use the Budget funding (28 Oct 2026) to set up NI's own BICS-style scheme.** Publish its amount, eligibility and start date.

5.  **Councils and public bodies: set embodied-carbon limits for steel and cement in tenders.** This turns the border charge into demand for low-carbon producers.

6.  **NI importers: map suppliers by country and route, request verified emissions data and add CBAM clauses to 2027 contracts.** If you buy fuels or other refinery products from outside the UK, respond to the consultation by 20 January 2027.

7.  **Parties pledging abolition: say how they would replace about £2.6bn of ETS receipts and handle the EU CBAM on UK exports.**
