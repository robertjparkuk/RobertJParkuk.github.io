The Government wants to modernise corporate reporting. For Northern Ireland manufacturers, I asked it not to change energy, carbon and sustainability reporting while the UK–EU carbon framework is still settling. In 2026 the rules are known. In 2027 we know what is changing but not the detail. 2028 is unknown for both CBAM and carbon reporting. Changing Companies Act reporting in the same window would make smaller suppliers rebuild their data systems twice.

## Why timing comes first

Four dates fall within 15 months. The UK CBAM starts on 1 January 2027 under Part 5 of the Finance Act 2026. The UK and EU concluded their agreement to link emissions trading on 20 November 2026; the UK intends to exempt EU-origin goods from its CBAM from 1 January 2027, and the EU exemption for UK goods is expected from 1 January 2028. Neither exemption is yet law. And EU member states must write the Omnibus I changes to CSRD into national law by 19 March 2027.

So I asked the Department to publish its conclusions, but to commence the energy, carbon and sustainability provisions no earlier than the first financial year after the linking agreement and both CBAM exemptions are in force, and not before financial years beginning on or after 1 January 2029, with a review in 2028 if either exemption slips. Simplifications that only remove burden can go ahead sooner.

## The other asks

1. **Count Northern Ireland.** Say how many responses came from NI. Our companies report under UK company law, but many sell into the EU single market under the Windsor Framework and get data requests under both sets of rules.
2. **A 'very large' threshold, honestly explained (Q13, Q23).** I support option (b), but narrowing mandatory reporting does not reduce what big customers ask of their suppliers. The Government should say so.
3. **Materiality with guidance (Q22).** Directors should judge financial materiality, with guidance naming carbon-price exposure (UK ETS, UK CBAM, EU CBAM through EU customers) for manufacturers, issued at the same time as any disclosures are removed.
4. **One data set, many uses (Q26).** SECR, ESOS, carbon reduction plans and CBAM should share definitions, boundaries and factors; NI needs guidance on its electricity factor in the all-island market; fuel should be measured as consumed, not bought.
5. **Location (Q27).** In the strategic report where material, with a cross-reference for SECR.
6. **A safe harbour for smaller suppliers (Q28).** The EU's value-chain cap protects firms with up to 1,000 employees from requests beyond a voluntary standard, but only for CSRD reporting. A UK equivalent should reach across regimes, and a Reporting Gateway should test requirements from every department, DESNZ and HMRC included.

## Why it matters to clients

If you sell to larger UK or EU customers, the reporting duty may sit with them, but the data request lands with you. Keep one carbon data set, measured once, that will answer SECR, ESOS, a carbon reduction plan and a CBAM request, and keep an eye on the dates above before you rebuild anything.
