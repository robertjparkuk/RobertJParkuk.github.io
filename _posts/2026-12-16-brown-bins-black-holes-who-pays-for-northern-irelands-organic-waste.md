*Part of the [Policy series]({{ '/policy-series/' | relative_url }}): where the rules stand in Northern Ireland, who decides them, and what to do about it. Updated as the rules change.*

## The argument in brief

England's weekly food waste duty for households started on 31 March 2026. A month earlier, more than 70 English councils told the BBC they would miss that date ([letsrecycle, 25 Feb 2026](https://www.letsrecycle.com/news/more-than-70-councils-not-ready-for-food-waste-collections-finds-bbc/)). On 21 April 2026, Causeway Coast and Glens councillors debated going the other way. They looked at cutting winter brown bin collections to monthly to save about £30,000. One councillor warned that a few dumping incidents could cost more than the saving ([Derry Now, 21 Apr 2026](https://www.derrynow.com/news/local-news/763397/council-contemplate-reducing-winter-bin-collections.html)).

Those two stories set the frame. Food waste is now a statutory service in every UK nation. Garden waste is still discretionary, and councils treat it as a cost to cut or charge for. In Northern Ireland, food and garden waste usually share one brown bin, so the two are hard to separate.

Downstream, anaerobic digestion (AD) plants turn food waste and slurry into energy and produce digestate. That digestate is rich in phosphorus, and it is spread in catchments such as Lough Neagh that already hold too much. Cutting methane is a settled goal. Where the nutrients and plastics go next is the open question.

*This piece sets out the arguments on each side; the recommendations are the author's. Facts checked to 30 October 2026.*

## The lifecycle at a glance

<figure class="page-photo"><img src="{{ '/assets/policy-series/compost-lifecycle.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Diagram: organic waste lifecycle: 7 stages, 4 leaks" loading="lazy"><figcaption>organic waste lifecycle · 7 stages, 4 leaks</figcaption></figure>

Policy has focused on the first four stages, getting food out of landfill and capturing its methane. The open leaks sit further down: plastic in the output, spills, and phosphorus spread where the soil already has too much.

## Where the pillars compete

- **Revenue against fairness (E against S).** Garden waste charges raise money and encourage home composting. They also hit households with gardens but little spare cash, and MPs heard that such charges fuel fly-tipping ([Commons Library](https://researchbriefings.files.parliament.uk/documents/SN05672/SN05672.pdf)).

- **Methane capture against phosphorus (E against E).** AD keeps food and slurry out of landfill and makes biomethane. But NI has a phosphorus surplus of about 6,000 tonnes a year (rePhoKUs 2020, cited by DAERA on 19 Feb 2026). Unless digestate is exported or processed, its phosphorus stays in the catchment. A digestate discharge killed 2,352 fish in the Muff River in November 2023.

- **Peat-free against clean compost (E against E).** Compost from food and garden waste is the obvious peat substitute, but kerbside feedstock carries plastics. England halved its plastic limit in October 2025. The UK PFAS Plan (3 Feb 2026) flags sewage sludge as a route for PFAS into soils, and the same worry could reach compost.

- **Compostable packaging against contamination (E against E).** Most UK food waste services reject compostable packaging, and UK EPR gives it the highest-fee red rating (Resource, 12 Aug 2026).

- **Fewer lorry miles against smells and dumping (E against S).** ABC's winter brown bin pilot (Dec 2024 to Mar 2025) aimed to cut mileage, fuel and emissions. Where food shares the bin, less frequent collection risks smells and misuse.

## What it means for Northern Ireland firms and councils

- **Food manufacturers.** The 5kg threshold and the sewer ban apply now. Expect a wider duty if DAERA follows its 2024 proposal.

- **AD gate fees.** Food waste competes with slurry, silage and processing residues. NIRO closed in 2017, so gate fees will reflect the squeeze on feedstock and digestate outlets. The UK Budget did not lift the biomethane cap, ADBA said (28 Oct 2026).

- **Packaging contamination.** Plastic in food waste lowers compost and digestate quality. Depackaging lines help but can grind plastic into fragments.

- **Councils.** The 70% duty and 10% landfill cap need more organics captured. Winter cuts save little and carry a fly-tipping risk. Landfill tax rises by RPI from 1 Apr 2027, so food left in black bins costs more to dispose of (Budget, 28 Oct 2026).

- **Data gaps.** No central register counts NI AD plants; estimates range from 46 to 84 (RaISe, 16 Mar 2026).

- **Compost buyers.** NI still works to the older protocol and plastics limit, so check certification and plastic content.

## Practical examples

- **Slurry and food waste AD, Dunmurry.** On 19 February 2026 DAERA awarded Natural World Products £4m to build NI's first high-solids AD plant. It will digest slurry solids with food and garden waste, and blend phosphorus-rich solids into PAS 100 compost ([DAERA](https://www.daera-ni.gov.uk/news/daera-awards-third-company-ps4million-advance-sustainable-utilisation-livestock-slurry)).

- **Digestate pollution, Muff River.** In March 2026 the Loughs Agency secured a conviction against an AD operator for a November 2023 discharge that killed 2,352 fish. The penalty was the £5,000 statutory maximum plus £2,160 costs ([Derry Now, 26 Mar 2026](https://www.derrynow.com/news/local-news/1750794/loughs-agency-secures-conviction-following-major-muff-river-fish-kill.html)).

- **Winter collections, ABC.** Armagh, Banbridge and Craigavon moved brown bins to four-weekly collection from mid-December 2024 to the end of March 2025. The council said winter bins hold much less garden waste ([Armagh I, 2 Dec 2024](https://armaghi.com/news/craigavon-news/abc-council-move-to-monthly-brown-bin-collections-over-winter/257245)).

- **Second-bin charge, Mid Ulster.** Mid Ulster collects one brown bin per property and charges £50 a year for a second ([Mid Ulster Council, checked 28 Oct 2026](https://www.midulstercouncil.org/resident/bins-recycling/household-recycling/brown-bin)).

- **England's food waste rollout.** About three-quarters of English councils were ready for weekly food waste on 31 March 2026. Another 31 had transitional agreements, some running to 2043, despite over £340m in capital funding ([letsrecycle, 1 Apr 2026](https://www.letsrecycle.com/news/analysis-86-of-councils-on-track-for-simpler-recycling-in-2026/)).

## Recommendations

1.  **DAERA: publish its response to the 2024 consultation, with a date for business food waste duties.** Firms need lead time, and England and Wales already have one.

2.  **NIEA: say whether NI will update the compost protocol, and restore its end-of-waste service.** Clear rules protect the market for peat substitutes.

3.  **DAERA: match England's tighter plastics limit, with a transition period.** NI compost should not become where GB rejects end up.

4.  **DAERA and DfE: link any biomethane support to phosphorus export or recovery from digestate.** Otherwise climate policy adds to Lough Neagh's nutrient load.

5.  **Councils: keep fortnightly brown bin collections all year where food shares the bin.** The savings are small and the public health risk is real.

6.  **Councils: offer subsidised home composters before considering a garden waste charge.**

7.  **Food manufacturers: audit what goes into food waste and remove packaging at source.** Cleaner feedstock means lower gate fees.

8.  **DAERA: run the retail peat consultation in 2027 alongside a supply plan for quality compost.** A ban without supply pushes buyers to imported peat.
