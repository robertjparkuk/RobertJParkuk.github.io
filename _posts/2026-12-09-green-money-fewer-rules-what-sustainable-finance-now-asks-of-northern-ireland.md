## The argument in brief

On 30 September 2026 the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) dropped its plan to make climate reporting mandatory for listed companies. Under policy statement PS26/19, listed companies will report against the UK Sustainability Reporting Standards on a "comply or explain" basis from January 2027. Three weeks earlier, on 7 September, Chancellor John Healey promised to cut the cost of business regulation by 25% by the end of this Parliament. The same day the business department opened a consultation proposing to replace the Companies Act list of reporting topics, such as environment and employees, with five baseline requirements.

Rules about where money goes are loosening at the top. The demand for carbon data from smaller firms is not going away, though. It is passing from regulators to banks, customers and investors. Banks must now understand climate risk in their loan books. EU customers still need value-chain data. Green loans and grants need evidence. A Northern Ireland manufacturer that builds one solid carbon data set can answer all of these, and can use it to borrow more cheaply.

The ESG pillars do not line up neatly. Nuclear is now "green" in UK gilts. Fossil-fuel firms may qualify as "transition" investments in the EU. Pension trustees are caught between the duty to earn returns and political pressure from all sides.

*This piece sets out the arguments on each side; the recommendations are the author's. Facts checked to 30 October 2026.*

Green money flows from bills to borrowing and back again, but only for firms that can evidence their numbers. It leaks where claims lack evidence and where minimum deal sizes shut out small projects.

- **Reporting.** Labour is trimming disclosure while keeping the frameworks. The Conservatives would remove them.

- **Pension money.** Reform-led councils have dropped climate targets. The Greens want divestment. Labour pushes pooled funds towards UK investment.

- **Public investment banks.** Scotland's and Wales's public investment banks keep net zero in their remits. Northern Ireland has no equivalent bank.

- **Northern Ireland.** The Assembly divide is over energy policy. No party has set out a position on finance rules.

## Who decides what

Most finance rules are set in London and apply UK-wide. EU finance rules reach NI only through customers and lenders in the Republic.

## Where the pillars compete

- **Nuclear as "green" (E against E).** Since November 2025 the UK Green Financing Framework counts nuclear and radioactive waste storage as eligible. That cuts carbon but stretches the label over a long-lived waste legacy.

- **Fossil firms as "transition" investments.** Both EU institutions would let fossil-fuel firms into SFDR 2.0's Transition category if at least 20% of their capital spending is Taxonomy-aligned. Supporters cite energy security and jobs; critics see a loophole.

- **Pension duty against political demands (S against E).** NILGOSC sold about £700,000 of Israeli bonds in 2024 and says it does not divest for political reasons.

- **Anti-greenwashing against access to finance.** Danske's labelled green loans start at £1m. National Wealth Fund minimums are £5m for councils and £25m for private projects. The Shared Island capital grant needed at least £5m of capital spend. Small firms carry the evidence burden without reaching the cheap money.

- **Investor materiality against local impact.** UK SRS and the September consultation focus on what is financially material to the company. Local harms that do not move a balance sheet fall out of scope.

## What it means for Northern Ireland firms and councils

- **Expect bank questions.** SS5/25 requires banks to evidence climate risk in lending. Have energy, fuel, refrigerant and Scope 1 and 2 figures ready. Ulster Bank offers a free Carbon Planner.

- **Cheaper finance needs evidence.** AIB (NI)'s Business Sustainability Loan runs from £2,000 to £100,000 at a rate below its standard business loan, with an invoice, quote or EPC needed. Danske prices sustainability-linked loans against agreed targets.

- **EU customers' requests now have a ceiling.** Under the Omnibus, firms averaging up to 1,000 staff can decline value-chain requests beyond the Voluntary Standard (VSME) from FY2027. Build your data to that level.

- **Grants reward good data.** Invest NI's Energy Efficiency Capital Grant funds energy-efficient equipment. The Shared Island Sustainability Capital Grant closed to applications on 2 May 2025.

- **Councils sit outside the English pension shake-up.** NILGOSC reports voluntarily against TCFD and moved £2.8bn of passive equity into a low-carbon fund in 2022.

- **National Wealth Fund access is the gap.** Its minimum drawdowns of £5m for councils and £25m for private projects exceed the typical NI project.

- **More Executive money, but no new green finance rules.** The Budget of 28 October 2026 gave the NI Executive an extra £270m in Barnett consequentials over the Spending Review period. It also offered a separate £1.5bn over three years to help the Executive agree a budget. The Executive is funded to set up its own version of the British Industrial Competitiveness Scheme (BICS) for heavy electricity users in manufacturing. No amount was stated, and design and timing are for the Executive ([HM Treasury and NIO, 28 Oct 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/breathing-space-for-northern-ireland-families-and-businesses)). The Budget made no change to green finance rules ([OOTLAR, 28 Oct 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/budget-2026-overview-of-tax-legislation-and-rates-ootlar/budget-2026-overview-of-tax-legislation-and-rates-ootlar)).

## Practical examples

- **O&S Doors, Dungannon.** The manufacturer's £9m biomass combined heat and power plant burns its own MDF dust. It won £2.4m from the Shared Island Sustainability Capital Grant and is expected to save about 1,500 tonnes of carbon a year ([4NI, 29 Jan 2026](https://www.4ni.co.uk/northern-ireland-news/326773/ministers-welcome-milestone-9m-green-investment-at-o-s-doors)).

- **The Shared Island grant set a high bar.** The €20m scheme split €10m each side of the border, required at least £5m of capital spend and closed on 2 May 2025 ([NI Business Info, page checked 28 Oct 2026](https://nibusinessinfo.co.uk/business-support/shared-island-sustainability-capital-grant)).

- **A green loan sized for SMEs.** AIB (NI) lends £2,000 to £100,000 unsecured for solar, EV charging and buildings reaching EPC B. The representative variable rate is 8.00%, and a quote, invoice or EPC is needed before drawdown ([AIB (NI), page checked 28 Oct 2026](https://www.aibni.co.uk/business/our-products/finance/business-sustainability-loan)).

- **A free first step.** Ulster Bank's Carbon Planner app measures a firm's footprint and returns a tailored reduction plan ([Ulster Bank, page checked 28 Oct 2026](https://ulsterbank.co.uk/business/green-banking.html)).

- **Nuclear money in a green wrapper.** The Debt Management Office sold £6.25bn of the 4⅝% Green Gilt 2037 on 10 March 2026. Nuclear has been an eligible use of green gilt money since November 2025 ([DMO, 10 Mar 2026](https://dmo.gov.uk/media/4hvmv2w1/pr100326a.pdf); [HM Treasury, 15 Oct 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/uk-government-green-financing-allocation-and-impact-report-2026)).

## Recommendations

1.  **DfE and Invest NI: publish one NI SME carbon data template aligned to VSME and UK SRS S2 metrics.** Banks, grant bodies and customers could accept the same file.

2.  **HM Treasury and the National Wealth Fund: create an aggregation route with Invest NI and the British Business Bank.** The £25m minimum excludes almost all NI manufacturers.

3.  **FCA: require ESG ratings providers to disclose how they score firms that report no data.** Proxy scoring tends to penalise small firms.

4.  **UK Government: decide on transition plans before the corporate reporting changes take effect.** Lenders are pricing in the uncertainty.

5.  **DfC and NILGOSC: publish a single responsible-investment and exclusions policy with dated climate targets.** It would ease council-by-council motions.

6.  **Firms: build a Scope 1 and 2 inventory from your bills and add your top five Scope 3 sources.** One data set answers banks, green lenders and EU customers.

7.  **Firms: ask your bank about green or sustainability-linked pricing before committing capital spending.**

8.  **Councils: raise climate risk through NILGOSC's formal processes, framed as fiduciary risk.**
