## The argument in brief

On 6 July 2026 the UK Government replied to the Environmental Audit Committee's PFAS report and made no firm commitment to any of its recommendations. It said it would wait for the EU's universal PFAS restriction before deciding on a group-wide approach. On 23 October 2026 the first duties under the EU's ban on PFAS in firefighting foam started to apply in Northern Ireland. Great Britain has no equivalent rule yet.

PFAS has become the clearest case of regulatory divergence inside the UK. Under the Windsor Framework, Northern Ireland follows EU REACH. Great Britain follows UK REACH, which has not restricted any new PFAS group since 2021. NI firms will reach EU standards first, whether or not Westminster follows. The harder question is how fast to move. Fluoropolymers sit inside some of the hardware that decarbonisation needs, and clean-up costs land on water bills and councils. Both sides of that argument rest on real evidence.

*This piece sets out the arguments on each side; the recommendations are the author's. Facts checked to 30 October 2026.*

**Why "forever"? A chemist's note.** Fluorine is the most electronegative element. So the carbon–fluorine bond is highly polar and very strong: about 485 kJ/mol, against about 410 kJ/mol for carbon–hydrogen. It is the strongest single bond carbon forms. In a perfluorinated chain, the fluorine atoms also form a tight sheath that shields the carbon backbone from attack. Sunlight, water and microbes rarely supply enough energy, or the right enzyme, to break the bond. These molecules last for decades or longer, and destroying them usually needs high-temperature incineration.

## The lifecycle at a glance

<figure class="page-photo"><img src="{{ '/assets/policy-series/pfas-lifecycle.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Diagram: PFAS lifecycle: 8 stages, 4 leak points" loading="lazy"><figcaption>PFAS lifecycle · 8 stages, 4 leak points</figcaption></figure>

PFAS escape at manufacture, use, disposal and in the environment, and recycling can carry them back into new products. Only high-temperature destruction ends the cycle, so every earlier stage is a point where regulation can cut releases.

- **Speed.** The EAC wants non-essential uses restricted from 2027 (23 Apr 2026). The Government's legislative horizon is December 2028.

- **Who pays.** The EAC wants a remediation fund and a central backstop for councils. The Government points to existing contaminated-land law.

- **Alignment.** The Government follows EU decisions by default, which narrows the practical gap between parties.

- **NI silence.** EU rules apply in NI automatically, so local parties have had little reason to take positions, leaving no local voice on costs or exemptions.

## Who decides what

EU institutions decide most PFAS rules for NI. Westminster, with Scottish and Welsh consent, decides for GB.

## Where the pillars compete

- **E versus E: hydrogen and energy hardware.** PEM electrolysers and fuel cells rely on fluorinated membranes. SEAC did not accept a blanket carve-out for fluoropolymers (March 2026). A fast ban could slow green hydrogen; a slow one locks in more persistent material.

- **E versus E: the circular economy.** RAC saw derogations for PFAS in recycled materials as possibly justified. The European Environmental Bureau argues they keep toxic chemicals cycling (26 Mar 2026).

- **E versus S: safety at sea and in the air.** EU foam derogations for aviation, maritime and offshore run to 2035. Defra flags friction for ferries such as Belfast to Cairnryan if GB and NI rules differ (15 Jun 2026).

- **E versus S: who pays.** Partial clean-up of short-chain PFAS in Europe could cost about €100bn a year (NGI, Jan 2025). Without a polluter-pays fund, bill payers and councils carry it.

- **Health speed versus data gaps.** RAC found no safe level of emissions; SEAC says data on alternatives and costs is still thin. Waiting delays health gains, and acting first risks costly substitution.

## What it means for Northern Ireland firms and councils

- **Two regimes.** NI manufacturers selling into GB and the EU face two PFAS regimes. Building to the EU standard covers both markets for now.

- **Food-contact packaging** placed on the NI market since 12 August 2026 must meet PPWR PFAS limits.

- **Fixed foam systems** at airports, ports and chemical sites must plan for the 23 October 2030 limit, including disposal of legacy foam stock.

- **Legacy waste carries PFAS.** NIEA's Mobuoy note found raw groundwater up to 1.28 µg/L for the sum of 48 PFAS (March 2026), with no impact found at the Carmoney abstraction.

- **Drinking water.** NI Water reports treated water within EU limits, but those limits have no statutory force in NI.

- **Councils** running landfills or depots may face PFAS in leachate and soil, with no national backstop fund promised.

## Practical examples

- **NI Water tests ahead of the law.** NI Water has sampled raw and treated water for PFAS since 2020. It reports all treated results below 0.01 µg/L, the lowest tier in the English guidance it borrows ([NI Water response 2526600](https://www.niwater.com/media/bdxecqz5/2526600-response.pdf), 13 Mar 2026).

- **DAERA holds data but no programme.** Asked for all PFAS sampling since 2024, DAERA released results for private water supplies, marine sediments and Mobuoy. It described no wider monitoring programme ([DAERA/26-482](https://www.daera-ni.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2026-08/DAERA%2026-482%20-%20Response%20letter_Redacted.pdf), 17 Aug 2026).

- **A council-led investigation in Lancashire.** Wyre Council and partner agencies have sampled gardens near the former ICI site at Thornton-Cleveleys for PFOA. Most homes needed no further action, and no land had been designated under Part 2A ([Wyre Council](https://www.wyre.gov.uk/news/article/599/residents-updated-after-latest-multi-agency-investigation-soil-sampling), 2 Mar 2026). The site's operator, AGC Chemicals Europe, proposed closing the plant on 13 July 2026, citing four years of losses ([ITV News](https://www.itv.com/news/granada/2026-07-13/major-chemical-plant-proposes-closure)).

- **Airports tested under permit powers.** The Environment Agency used permit notices to make 24 commercial airports in England test for PFAS; reports indicate 17 found elevated levels ([Henderson Chambers](https://www.hendersonchambers.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/2026-10-16-Alerter-by-Toby-Riley-Smith-KC-Tim-Green-KC-Lucy-McCormick-Douglas-Maxwell-PFAS-aqueous-film-forming-foam-contaminated-land.pdf), 16 Oct 2026).

- **The bill for legacy foam.** In April 2026 Guernsey sought approval for up to £16.54m to dig out about 15,000 tonnes of foam-contaminated soil at its airport ([Henderson Chambers](https://www.hendersonchambers.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/2026-10-16-Alerter-by-Toby-Riley-Smith-KC-Tim-Green-KC-Lucy-McCormick-Douglas-Maxwell-PFAS-aqueous-film-forming-foam-contaminated-land.pdf), 16 Oct 2026).

- **Clothing rules already live in NI.** Since 10 October 2026, consumer clothing, accessories and footwear placed on the NI market must meet EU PFHxA limits of 25 ppb ([Beveridge & Diamond](https://www.bdlaw.com/publications/new-eu-pfhxa-limits-take-effect-amid-expanding-pfas-rules-for-apparel-and-textiles/), Oct 2026). NI textile and workwear firms selling into the EU need supplier test data now.

## Recommendations

1.  **DAERA and DfI: write the EU drinking water PFAS limits into NI regulations.** NI Water already meets them, so the cost is low and confidence improves.

2.  **NIEA: publish an NI PFAS monitoring programme like SEPA's,** starting with airports, landfills, fire-training grounds and Lough Neagh abstractions.

3.  **Defra: bring the GB foam decision forward from 2027.** It would remove friction for ferries and fire-equipment suppliers.

4.  **DAERA: commence Part III of the 1997 Order with PFAS in scope.** Mobuoy shows the need, and NI has no statutory contaminated-land regime.

5.  **NI Executive: set out NI's interests in the EU restriction through UK–EU structures before the REACH Committee vote.** NI has no vote, so it needs to make its case early.

6.  **Firms: map PFAS in products and processes now.** Look at seals, coatings, PTFE parts, foams and packaging, and ask suppliers for total-fluorine data.

7.  **Energy and engineering firms: give trade bodies evidence on essential fluoropolymer uses.** Exemptions go to sectors that supply data.

8.  **Councils: specify fluorine-free foams and PFAS-free packaging in procurement.** Switching now is cheaper than cleaning up later.
