*Part of the [Policy series]({{ '/policy-series/' | relative_url }}): where the rules stand in Northern Ireland, who decides them, and what to do about it. Updated as the rules change.*

## The argument in brief

On 1 October 2026 the Environment Secretary, Dame Angela Eagle, told waste criminals they would have ["nowhere to hide"](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/no-hiding-place-new-tracking-system-exposes-waste-criminals-like-never-before). That day, every permitted waste site in England and Wales had to start recording each load it receives online. Defra's own [press release of 24 April 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/game-changing-digital-tracking-takes-fight-to-rogue-waste-traders) listed Northern Ireland for October too. It was wrong, or it slipped. [DAERA](https://www.daera-ni.gov.uk/articles/digital-waste-tracking) (page updated 25 June 2026) and [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/digital-waste-tracking-service/digital-waste-tracking-service) (updated 1 October 2026) now both say January 2027 for NI receivers.

Three months matters less than what the system can see. Northern Ireland's worst waste crime happened under paper. At Mobuoy, near Derry, an estimated 635,000 tonnes were deposited illegally. The [NI Audit Office](https://www.niauditoffice.gov.uk/files/niauditoffice/documents/2025-07/NI%20Audit%20Office%20Report%20-%20Waste%20Crime%20in%20NI.pdf) found in July 2025 that NIEA had carried out no inspections matching waste movements in the previous two years.

Digital tracking is the right fix. The problem is the order it arrives in. Phase 1 records waste only when it reaches a permitted site. The legs where waste goes missing stay on paper until at least October 2027: carriers, brokers, exempt sites and cross-border loads. Firms will keep paper notes and the digital record side by side. And the data deters no one unless NIEA has people to read it.

The Welsh Government's own assessment makes the point in numbers. Receivers alone return 17p per £1 of cost; the full system returns £1.26 ([explanatory memorandum, March 2026](https://laiddocuments.senedd.wales/sub-ld17907-em-en.pdf)). Phase 1 only pays if Phase 2 lands on time.

*This piece sets out the arguments on each side; the recommendations are the author's. Facts checked to 30 October 2026.*

## Where the pillars compete

Tracking serves the environment by making waste visible. Its costs fall on small operators and on privacy. The phasing also creates an environmental risk of its own.

- **E: waste crime and data quality.** Receivers must log each load within two working days, with the carrier and waste code. The Environment Agency will compare reported collections with receipts to flag suspicious loads. NIEA has never had this. The NIAO found it held about 28,000 consignment notes a year with a review backlog, and never saw transfer notes at all.

- **S: burden on small firms.** The fee is small at £26 a year. Software is the real cost, estimated at £632 a year per business. The Welsh assessment notes extra burdens on micro operators, such as data security, that it did not cost. During Phase 1, firms also keep every paper record they keep today.

- **S: digital exclusion.** Each set of Regulations exempts "digitally excluded" operators, who keep written records instead. The Welsh memorandum expects only natural persons, such as sole traders, to qualify. A small limited company with poor broadband does not.

- **S: privacy and commercial confidence.** In the 2022 consultation, commercial confidentiality was the commonest reason for objecting to proposed data access. Scotland's Cabinet Secretary said in February 2026 that no extra commercially sensitive data would be published. The England and Wales Regulations put civil sanctions on a public register.

- **E versus E: displacement.** Phase 1 lights up permitted sites while carriers, brokers and exempt sites stay on paper. A rogue load has every reason to avoid a permitted gate until October 2027. No evidence of this exists yet, so regulators should watch for it.

The honest balance: the burden on a well-run site is modest, and the crime it targets is large. In NI the NIAO put waste crime at about £34m a year. The weak point is the 12 months when firms pay for two systems and criminals can route around the new one.

## What it means for Northern Ireland firms and councils

**Manufacturers producing waste.** You are not in Phase 1. Your duty of care and transfer notes are unchanged. From January 2027 your contractor's receiving site must log your loads with a waste code. A wrong code on your note will now show up against their record. Check codes and descriptions now.

**Firms that receive waste.** If you hold a permit or licence to take third-party waste, you are a receiver. Register, pay the fee, and choose the API through your software or the Defra spreadsheet. GOV.UK expects the spreadsheet to stay until at least October 2027.

**Hauliers and brokers.** Phase 2 brings you in from October 2027, with a public beta in spring 2027. England is also moving carriers into environmental permitting. The NIAO found NI carrier registration is a single flat fee with no routine inspection.

**Firms shipping waste to Ireland or the EU.** EU shipment law applies in NI under the Windsor Framework. Dublin City Council's guidance treats shipments to NI as within the EU. Green-list paperwork moves onto DIWASS after the 31 December 2026 transition.

**Councils.** Wales left recycling centres out. England does not require household waste from residents to be logged in Phase 1. DAERA has not published how NI recycling centres will be treated. Over time the service could replace much of councils' separate waste data reporting, which matters for recycling targets and packaging EPR.

## Practical examples

- **Mobuoy, Co Londonderry.** About 1.6 million tonnes of waste sit on the site, an estimated 635,000 tonnes deposited illegally. The best clean-up estimate is £107m, mostly falling to the public sector ([NIAO, 3 July 2025](https://www.niauditoffice.gov.uk/files/niauditoffice/documents/2025-07/NI%20Audit%20Office%20Report%20-%20Waste%20Crime%20in%20NI.pdf)).

- **Cross-border dumping.** About 270,000 tonnes of waste from the Republic was dumped at 17 NI sites. The five largest still held about 137,000 tonnes, and NIEA had spent £492,000 on 12 sites (NIAO case study 6, July 2025).

- **Prosecutions in NI.** NIEA secured 29 illegal dumping prosecutions over five years, between four and seven a year from 2021 to 2025. The figures came from Assembly answers ([Irish News, 10 May 2026](https://www.irishnews.com/news/northern-ireland/fewer-than-six-illegal-dumping-prosecutions-a-year-in-northern-ireland-despite-rampant-fly-tipping-PREHFRNXMNG3JKOXTULJAFJEGM/)).

- **England's launch.** From 1 October 2026 more than 12,000 permitted sites report every load in near real time. Defra paired the launch with a Waste Crime Crackdown Group and named site clearances in Kidlington, Bradford and Wigan ([Defra, 1 October 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/no-hiding-place-new-tracking-system-exposes-waste-criminals-like-never-before)).

- **Clearance funding in the Budget.** The UK Budget funded the tax liabilities from planned clearance of six illegal waste sites. It did not say any are in NI, where Mobuoy alone is estimated at £107m ([letsrecycle, 29 October 2026](https://www.letsrecycle.com/news/live-sector-reactions-autumn-budget-2026/)).

- **Councils treated differently.** Wales left household waste recycling centres out of scope ([Welsh Government, 30 June 2026](https://www.gov.wales/mandatory-digital-waste-tracking-system)). England's centres need not log residents' household waste in Phase 1 ([letsrecycle, 1 October 2026](https://www.letsrecycle.com/news/digital-waste-tracking-goes-live-what-happens-now/)).

- **Industry-built design.** Scotland's service was shaped by a user panel of more than 450 operators, the Cabinet Secretary told MSPs on 3 February 2026 ([Scottish Parliament](https://www.parliament.scot/chamber-and-committees/committees/committee-reports/nzet/2026/2/18/nzets062026r07/consideration-by-the-net-zero-energy-and-transport-committee)).

## Recommendations

These are the author's recommendations.

1.  **DAERA should lay the NI Regulations and confirm January 2027 in one public notice.** It should publish the NI fee, the digitally excluded route and the treatment of recycling centres at the same time.

2.  **NIEA needs analysts as well as a database.** Ring-fence staff to match receipts with collections. This answers the NIAO's first recommendation on inspecting transfer and consignment notes. DAERA should also ask HM Treasury whether its new clearance funding (28 October 2026) can reach NI sites.

3.  **Hold the October 2027 date for carriers and brokers.** Every month of slip extends the displacement risk. Pair it with reform of NI carrier registration, as the NIAO recommended.

4.  **Set a date to retire duplicate paper.** Name when transfer notes, consignment notes and quarterly returns end for digital users. Wales already expects returns to go from 2028.

5.  **Confirm how NI shipments reach DIWASS.** NI firms sending waste to Ireland need one clear route before the green-list paper transition ends on 31 December 2026.

6.  **For firms now:** check your contractor's permit, correct your waste codes, and ask your software supplier whether it connects to the Defra API.
