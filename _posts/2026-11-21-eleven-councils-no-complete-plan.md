*Status as at 21 November 2026. General information, not legal or planning advice.*

Councils took over planning in April 2015, each to produce a two-part Local Development Plan: a Plan Strategy, then a Local Policies Plan with site zonings. Six councils now have an adopted Plan Strategy. None has published even a draft Local Policies Plan. The delay is not only local: the [NI Audit Office found](https://www.niauditoffice.gov.uk/node/1334) (May 2026) that examining a Plan Strategy took 22 to 43 months in total, 16 to 28 of them at the Planning Appeals Commission, and that complete plans are unlikely before the mid-2030s.

## Where the system is falling short

- **Time is running out on the plans themselves.** Four plans end in 2030, with about four years left and no site zonings adopted.
- **Site zonings still come from old area plans.** Plans whose periods ended in 1995, 2001 and 2010 remain in force until each council's Local Policies Plan replaces them, read alongside the newer Plan Strategy.
- **Decisions are slower; enforcement is improving.** In 2025/26 major applications took 41.4 weeks on average against a 30-week target, and local applications 19.6 weeks against 15. Enforcement beat its target at 75.1% ([DfI planning statistics](https://www.infrastructure-ni.gov.uk/system/files/2026-08/planning-statistics-2025-26-bulletin.pdf), 26 August 2026).
- **Infrastructure decides what a plan can deliver.** An LDP can zone land for homes, but it cannot make NI Water connect them. The [NI Audit Office](https://www.niauditoffice.gov.uk/publications/funding-water-infrastructure-northern-ireland) (March 2024) found development restricted by wastewater capacity in some 100 areas. That is NI Water's funding gap, not a failure of plan-making.
- **Carbon, land and environment policy is moving under the plans.** Planning policy on renewable and low-carbon energy was revised on 11 December 2025. Northern Ireland still has no statutory contaminated land regime, so the planning system carries most of the burden for brownfield sites.

## Six proposals

1. **A two-way long-stop for Local Policies Plans:** each council, DfI and the examining body report each year on the time each has taken.
2. **Interim zonings** where area plan periods have ended, with full appraisal, consultation and examination.
3. **A duty on central bodies to give reasons** where their programmes do not match an adopted plan.
4. **Use the Independent Inspectors Project** for the examinations still to come. Inspectors examine; councils still adopt.
5. **A carbon and planning policy check** of every adopted Plan Strategy, done once by DfI and shared, without re-opening adopted plans.
6. **A working contaminated land regime**, so that brownfield housing does not rest on planning conditions alone.
