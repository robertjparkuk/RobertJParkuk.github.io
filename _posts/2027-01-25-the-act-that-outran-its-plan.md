*Part of the [Policy series]({{ '/policy-series/' | relative_url }}): where the rules stand in Northern Ireland, who decides them, and what to do about it. Updated as the rules change.*

## The argument in brief

On 14 April 2026 the DUP asked the Assembly to tell DAERA Minister Andrew Muir to withdraw Northern Ireland's draft Climate Action Plan. MLAs defeated the motion by 56 votes to 26. Muir told the chamber he had no power to withdraw it. The UUP said it would have backed a motion to revise the targets instead. Six months on, the plan still has not been laid; on 3 September 2026 DAERA said it was with Executive colleagues for approval.

The Climate Change Act (Northern Ireland) 2022 is law, but most of the machinery it requires is late. The first Climate Action Plan was due by June 2024. The fourth carbon budget was due by 31 December 2025 and has not been set. Emissions in 2024 were 29.4% below the base year, against a first-budget average cut of 33%. That budget runs to 2027, so the gap is a warning, not yet a breach.

The real political argument is whether the targets are revised after the May 2027 election, not whether they are met. For firms and councils, the duties that already bite are reporting, procurement and planning. Those will survive any revision of the targets.

*This piece sets out the arguments on each side; the recommendations are the author's. Facts checked to 30 October 2026.*

The Act and the CCC's advice are in place. Delay starts at the budgets and builds through the plan and sectoral plans, so delivery and oversight run without the framework the Act expected.

- The Act began as a DAERA bill brought by a DUP minister, Edwin Poots, and was heavily amended before it passed in 2022.

- The disagreement is about pace and who carries the cost, especially farmers. Few parties openly reject the 2050 goal.

- Sinn Féin is split between holding the targets and delivering the A5. The Court of Appeal ruling takes some heat out of that.

- The UUP's "revise" position is the likely ground for any compromise. Section 54 allows target changes only on CCC advice or "significant developments".

## Who decides what

DAERA drafts the plan and budgets. Under the Executive's cross-cutting rules, any party in the Executive can delay them.

| Instrument                    | Who decides                                                                           | Whose approval is needed                                            | Position in Northern Ireland                                   |
|-------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------|
| Carbon budgets and targets    | DAERA                                                                                 | Executive, then Assembly (draft affirmative); CCC advice considered | Budgets 1–3 set; budget 4 overdue                              |
| Climate Action Plan           | DAERA with all departments                                                            | Executive, then laid before the Assembly                            | With the Executive                                             |
| Sectoral plans (ss.13–22)     | Each department (DfE energy and industry, DfI transport, DAERA agriculture and waste) | Executive                                                           | Not yet produced (Assembly research, Feb 2026)                 |
| Climate Commissioner          | Executive Office (FM and dFM jointly)                                                 | Assembly approved regulations                                       | Not appointed                                                  |
| Electricity emissions trading | EU for NI generators                                                                  | Windsor Framework                                                   | NI generators in the EU ETS; other installations in the UK ETS |
| UK net zero and UK ETS        | UK Government                                                                         | Parliament                                                          | UK-wide alongside the NI Act                                   |

## Where the pillars compete

- **Livestock against the methane cut (E against S).** Agriculture produced 31.4% of NI emissions in 2024, the largest sector share. The TUV says the 2030 target implies about 20% fewer livestock (11 Nov 2025). Muir says the plan has no herd-reduction proposals.

- **Plan cost against public services (E against S).** The draft plan sets out £718m of capital over 2023/24 to 2026/27, with £341m for transport, £70m for agriculture and £16m for business. The DUP claims the cost to 2027 is above £1bn (14 Apr 2026). The Budget of 28 Oct 2026 gave the Executive an extra £270m and offered £1.5bn over three years, none of it earmarked for climate.

- **Cheaper heating oil against heat decarbonisation (S against E).** The Budget of 28 Oct 2026 removed the 4% tariff on kerosene, and 61% of NI households rely on heating oil. It eases bills now but makes oil boilers cheaper to keep.

- **Road safety against emissions (S against E).** The A5 has a serious crash record. It was blocked under s.52 until the Court of Appeal reversed the ruling, showing how a missing plan can stall social infrastructure.

- **Forestry against peat and biodiversity (E against E).** Belfast City Council challenged the plan's treatment of conifer planting as nature-based and asked for a review of forests on peat.

- **Bioenergy against water and air (E against E).** Bioenergy supplied 18% of NI's renewable generation in the year to June 2026. AD feedstock and digestate need managing near Lough Neagh.

## What it means for Northern Ireland firms and councils

- **Public procurement.** Section 52 binds departments now. Expect carbon questions and whole-life carbon requests in tenders whatever happens to the plan.

- **Grants.** Invest NI consultancy support is limited to firms spending over £30k a year on energy and resources, which leaves many small firms out.

- **Supply-chain asks.** NI dairy and meat processors have their own net zero commitments, and supplier asks will continue even if Stormont softens targets.

- **Future regulation.** The draft plan includes mandatory commercial recycling, tighter building standards and F-gas compliance.

- **Councils.** All 40 specified public bodies, including councils, filed emissions and adaptation reports by June 2026. Each sets out emissions, actions, climate risks and adaptation.

- **Energy.** Renewables met 50% of NI electricity consumption in the year to June 2026, against 80% required by 2030. The PAC warned on 22 Oct 2026 that uncertainty is pushing investment elsewhere.

- **Industrial electricity costs.** The Budget of 28 Oct 2026 funds the Executive to set up its own version of the British Industrial Competitiveness Scheme (BICS). It is for heavy electricity users in manufacturing. No amount was given, and design and timing are for the Executive ([HM Treasury and NIO, 28 Oct 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/breathing-space-for-northern-ireland-families-and-businesses)).

## Practical examples

- **O&S Doors, Dungannon.** The door maker is installing a £9m biomass CHP plant that burns over 10,000 tonnes a year of its own MDF dust. It expects to save about 1,500 tonnes of carbon a year, helped by a £2.4m Shared Island grant awarded by Invest NI ([Executive Office, 28 Jan 2026](https://www.executiveoffice-ni.gov.uk/news/os-doors-turn-dust-power-ps9million-first-its-kind-investment)).

- **The A5 and section 52.** The Court of Appeal held on 6 Oct 2026 that s.52 does not require each project's emissions to be mapped onto a quantified pathway. Consistency with the Act's objectives, as far as possible, is enough ([Irish Legal News, 7 Oct 2026](https://www.irishlegal.com/articles/court-of-appeal-overrules-high-court-decision-on-a5)). For public clients, the test is evidenced reasoning.

- **First public body reports.** All 40 specified bodies, including councils and the Housing Executive, met their first statutory climate reporting duty. Each report covers emissions, actions, risks and adaptation ([DAERA, 12 Jun 2026](https://www.daera-ni.gov.uk/news/public-bodies-celebrated-milestone-achievement-climate-change-reporting)).

- **Energy strategy audit.** The NI Audit Office found progress on the key 2030 energy targets "lagging considerably", after about £107m of spending since 2020 ([NIAO, 21 Oct 2025](https://www.niauditoffice.gov.uk/node/1229)). The Assembly's Public Accounts Committee followed with its own report on 22 Oct 2026 ([PAC inquiry](https://www.niassembly.gov.uk/assembly-business/committees/2022-2027/public-accounts/inquiries/inquiry-into-northern-ireland-energy-strategy/)).

- **Which renewables figure to use.** Renewables met 50% of NI electricity consumption in the year to June 2026, 4,431 of 8,865 GWh. The 53% figure in some reports measures NI generation and ignores imports. The 80% target uses the 50% measure ([DfE, 3 Sep 2026](https://www.economy-ni.gov.uk/news/electricity-consumption-and-renewable-generation-northern-ireland-year-ending-june-2026)).

## Recommendations

1.  **Executive: agree and lay the Climate Action Plan before the election.** The statutory date passed in June 2024, and delay raises legal and investment risk.

2.  **DAERA: lay draft regulations for the fourth carbon budget with a statement on the missed deadline.** Openness protects credibility.

3.  **Executive Office: set a date to appoint the Climate Commissioner.** A vacancy invites challenge.

4.  **Invest NI and DfE: open technical consultancy to firms spending under £30k a year on energy and resources.** Many small manufacturers fall below it.

5.  **Parties: publish costed climate positions in 2027 manifestos, including any s.54 target changes.**

6.  **Firms: build an audit-ready carbon inventory now.** Public buyers and processors will ask whatever happens at Stormont.

7.  **Councils: use your 2026 reports to set local adaptation priorities and bid for funding together.**
