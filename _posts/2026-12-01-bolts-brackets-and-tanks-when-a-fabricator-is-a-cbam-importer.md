Most fabricators think of CBAM as a steelworks problem. It is not. From **1 January 2027** the UK carbon border adjustment mechanism (CBAM) charges the importer of steel and aluminium goods, and the list goes well past billet and coil. Bolts, nuts, screws and washers (7318), steel structures and parts (7308), tanks and containers (7309 and 7310), gas cylinders (7311), other steel articles (7326), aluminium structures (7610) and other aluminium articles (7616) are all CBAM goods, in both the UK and the EU regimes.

**The question for a fabricator is not "do I make CBAM goods?" but "do I import them?"**

## Three Northern Ireland cases

1. **You buy fixings from a distributor in Great Britain.** Goods already in GB are not a UK import, so you have no UK CBAM liability. Check where the distributor sourced them only if your customer asks.
2. **You buy plate and sections from a stockholder in Ireland.** On my reading of the Finance Act 2026 (section 144(5)(a)), goods arriving in Northern Ireland from Ireland or the rest of the EU count as UK CBAM imports; HMRC has not yet confirmed this. On 20 November 2026 the UK Government said it intends to exempt goods originating in the EU, the EEA and Switzerland from 1 January 2027 ([UK-EU ETS linking agreement: explainer](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/uk-eu-linking-agreement-explainer/uk-eu-linking-agreement-explainer), DESNZ). The power to do that is in section 156 of the Finance Act 2026, by Treasury regulations, and those regulations have not yet been made. Steel rolled in GB and bought back through a Dublin stockholder is UK-origin and out either way. Steel that originated outside the EU, such as Turkish or Indian plate held in a Dublin yard, stays in scope either way. If it has already borne the EU CBAM, the UK rules allow relief for a carbon price paid under another country's CBAM, with evidence.
3. **You import welded mesh direct from outside Europe.** You are the importer. The £50,000 threshold is two tests: a forward test (do you expect to import £50,000 or more of CBAM goods in the next 30 days?) and a backward test (have you passed £50,000 in the last 12 months, checked on the first of each month?), counted together across steel, aluminium, cement, fertiliser and hydrogen. Goods of UK origin are excluded. Pass either test and you must register and report: see HMRC's guidance, [Check if you'll need to register for CBAM](https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/check-if-youll-need-to-register-for-carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism-cbam).

## Worked example (fictional)

Firm A, a fictional Co. Antrim fabricator, imports in 2027: £38,000 of structural sections from a Dublin stockholder (origin: Turkey), £14,000 of stainless fixings from China, and £60,000 of aluminium extrusions from a GB supplier.

- GB extrusions: not an import. Out.
- Fixings from China: in scope.
- Turkish-origin sections via Dublin: in scope on my reading of the Act, and still in scope if the EU-origin exemption is made, because their origin is Turkey.
- Total in scope: **£52,000.** That is over £50,000 on the 12-month test, so Firm A is a UK CBAM importer for 2027.

## What it costs

The charge itself is modest in 2027. HMRC's default value for hot-rolled coil under CN 7208 10 00 is 2.722 tCO2e a tonne, including the 10% mark-up. At HMRC's illustrative iron and steel rate of £9.69 (published 16 October 2026), that is about **£26 a tonne**, with little or no carbon price relief.

Fabricated goods carry higher defaults than coil, because more processing sits behind them. On HMRC's tables (16 October 2026, in tCO2e a tonne):

| Goods | CN heading | HMRC default range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Steel structures and parts | 7308 | 4.989 to 6.523 |
| Tanks and large containers | 7309 | 6.288 to 7.050 |
| Cans and small containers | 7310 | 3.502 to 4.662 |
| Gas cylinders | 7311 | 5.654 to 6.949 |
| Screws, bolts, nuts and washers | 7318 | 2.552 to 6.804 |
| Other steel articles | 7326 | 3.039 to 4.124 |
| Aluminium structures | 7610 | 3.987 to 4.847 |
| Other aluminium articles | 7616 | 4.547 to 5.768 |

So a tonne of imported bolts or brackets could carry roughly £25 to £68 of CBAM at the illustrative rate. The illustrative rate is not the 2027 rate: HMRC publishes the actual rate before each quarter starts. Check your own codes on GOV.UK's default values pages. For most small importers, the registration, records and annual return are the larger burden.

## What to do this month

- List every steel and aluminium purchase for the last 12 months by supplier, CN code, origin and value.
- Run your codes through the free [Is it a CBAM good?]({{ '/cbam-desk/is-it-a-cbam-good/' | relative_url }}) lookup.
- If you are near £50,000, ask suppliers outside the UK for their installation's emissions now. Default values apply for 2027 and 2028 if they cannot supply them.
