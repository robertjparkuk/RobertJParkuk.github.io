*Northern Ireland's breeding flock has fallen 13% in three years while lamb is scarce across Europe. The money is there. Northern Ireland is not set up to catch it.*

*General information as at 21 November 2026, not legal, veterinary or financial advice.* The full briefing covers sheep and lamb, the cost of shearing and the end of the overseas shearer visa route, and Northern Ireland's goats, with every chart, table and source: [download the full briefing (PDF, 18 pages)]({{ '/assets/sheep/2026-11/sheep-lamb-shearing-goats-ni-briefing.pdf' | relative_url }}).

In June 2025 there were 864,522 breeding ewes in Northern Ireland. That is 7% fewer than a year earlier, and about 13% fewer than in 2022, according to [DAERA's census](https://www.daera-ni.gov.uk/news/results-june-agricultural-census-2025). In Scotland and Wales, total sheep numbers rose slightly over the same year. Ours fell.

At the same time, lamb across Europe is scarce and dear. UK sheepmeat exports rose 15% in the first nine months of 2025, with France the biggest buyer ([AHDB](https://ahdb.org.uk/news/growing-uk-lamb-exports-continue-to-lend-support-to-domestic-prices-sheep-meat-q3-2025-trade-update)). A shrinking flock in a rising market is a sign that the value is going somewhere other than the farm.

## The lambs are heading east

For years a large share of Northern Ireland's lambs went live to plants in the Republic. In 2026 that changed. In the first 30 weeks of the year, 78,338 lambs went live to Great Britain for slaughter, against 21,209 a year earlier. Live exports south fell from 123,908 to 77,850 ([LMC bulletin, 1 August 2026](https://www.lmcni.com/multisite/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Bulletin-0108026-1.pdf)).

The Livestock and Meat Commission has not said why. Higher GB prices are one likely reason. Bluetongue is another: it was confirmed in Northern Ireland for the first time on 5 December 2025, suspended our disease-free status, and was found again in Co Down this month.

In late July, Northern Ireland plants quoted 700 to 720p/kg for lambs. The average paid in Great Britain was 809p/kg. A quote is not the same as a price paid, because bonuses come on top, so the real gap is smaller. But the direction is clear.

## Hill farms earn least

DAERA's Farm Business Survey puts average income on cattle and sheep farms in the Less Favoured Area at £24,298 in 2024/25. Dairy farms averaged £118,784 ([DAERA](https://www.daera-ni.gov.uk/news/northern-ireland-agricultural-incomes-2025)). DAERA forecasts a strong rise for 2025/26, but from a low base.

These are the farms that graze the uplands and the peat. Northern Ireland's [Peatland Strategy](https://www.daera-ni.gov.uk/news/daera-publishes-first-peatland-strategy-delivery-plan-northern-ireland) needs grazing set at the right level: too many sheep damage the bog, too few let scrub and wildfire in. Nobody yet pays for getting it right.

## The carbon question, honestly

Lamb carries about 22 to 24 kg of CO2e per kilo of carcase, similar to beef and several times pork or chicken. Most of it is methane from the sheep's gut. Hill lamb carries more per kilo than lowland lamb, because hill ewes rear fewer, slower lambs ([Jones and others, 2014](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:agisys:v:123:y:2014:i:c:p:97-107)).

Agriculture produced 31.4% of Northern Ireland's emissions in 2024 ([NISRA](https://datavis.nisra.gov.uk/daera/northern-ireland-greenhouse-gas-inventory-1990-2024-statistical-bulletin.html)). The easy answer is fewer sheep. But if demand holds, the lamb comes from somewhere else, and the hill still needs grazing. The better answer is more value and better management per sheep.

## Wool is worth something again

For years wool barely paid for the shearing. That has turned. Ulster Wool paid an average of 68p/kg for the 2025 clip, a ten-year high, and many core grades are expected to pass £1/kg for 2026. Shearing costs about £1.93 a ewe. For the first time in a long while, the fleece can pay its way.

## Who shears them now

The UK concession that let up to about 75 overseas shearers a year, mostly New Zealanders, work the British season closed for the last time on 30 June 2026. Only 16 used it in its final year, according to Home Office figures reported by Farmers Weekly. From 2027 the only route is the Youth Mobility Scheme, open to New Zealanders aged 18 to 35. Experienced shearers over 35 have no route at all. Northern Ireland has its own contractors and many farmers shear their own, but a wet year with too few hands leaves hill flocks unshorn into the flystrike season.

## The goats nobody counts

Northern Ireland had 7,335 goats in 1990 and 2,140 in June 2025 ([DAERA census](https://www.daera-ni.gov.uk/news/results-june-agricultural-census-2025)). Goats' milk rests on a handful of herds and one main buyer. Goats' cheese has relied on one or two makers at a time, and I found no goats' butter made here. Most male kids born on dairy farms have no market. Broughgammon Farm near Ballycastle shows what one can look like.

## Northern Ireland sells lamb without a name

Welsh Lamb, Scotch Lamb, Shetland Lamb, Orkney Lamb, Lakeland Herdwick, Cambrian Mountains Lamb, Isle of Man Manx Loaghtan Lamb and Connemara Hill Lamb are all protected names ([GOV.UK register](https://www.gov.uk/protected-food-drink-names/welsh-lamb)). Northern Ireland has none. Nor does it pay farmers to keep rare sheep breeds, as the Republic does.

## Six ways to get more from fewer sheep

1. **Named mutton and hogget.** Cull ewes mostly leave Northern Ireland as a commodity. Our dry-ageing specialists could build a named line that keeps more of the value here.
2. **A protected hill lamb name.** Mourne, Sperrin or Antrim Glens lamb, registered through the EU scheme that applies here.
3. **Wool kept at home.** With prices near £1/kg, test local scouring, yarn or insulation.
4. **Paid peat grazing.** Pay hill farmers for managed grazing under the Peatland Strategy.
5. **Native-breed support.** A small payment for rare sheep and conservation grazing.
6. **A Northern Ireland cure.** A salted and smoked lamb or mutton, made to the EU nitrite limits that have applied here since October 2025.

## What the UK–EU deal changes

The UK and EU concluded talks on a food and farm-goods (SPS) agreement on 20 November 2026. It is meant to apply from 1 March 2028 and still needs signing, ratifying and legislating ([Cabinet Office](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ukeu-summit-2026-key-documents)). Northern Ireland lamb already moves freely into the EU. The deal mainly helps GB lamb into France on easier terms. That narrows our advantage, which is one more reason to give Northern Ireland lamb a name of its own.
