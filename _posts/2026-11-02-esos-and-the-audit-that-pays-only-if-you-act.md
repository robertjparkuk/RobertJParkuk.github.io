*Part of the [Policy series]({{ '/policy-series/' | relative_url }}): where the rules stand in Northern Ireland, who decides them, and what to do about it. Updated as the rules change.*

## The argument in brief

On 7 September 2026 the government published its Modernising Corporate Reporting consultation, which closes on 30 November 2026. It said DESNZ would consult separately later in 2026 on reforming Streamlined Energy and Carbon Reporting (SECR) and the Energy Savings Opportunity Scheme (ESOS). Five days earlier GOV.UK updated its ESOS Phase 4 guidance. On 1 October the British Industrial Competitiveness Scheme (BICS) opened to cut electricity levies for manufacturers in Great Britain only. The Budget of 28 October 2026 then funded the NI Executive to set up its own version, with no amount stated.

That has been a lopsided deal for Northern Ireland. NI manufacturers carry the full UK-wide ESOS duty but are shut out of the GB relief. The NI version funded on 28 October 2026 is still to be designed, so the Executive can link relief to the audit. ESOS requires firms to audit and to report. It does not require them to act on what the audit finds, and the evidence shows audits alone change little.

Firms that tie the audit to their carbon data and their energy contracts can make it pay. Firms that treat it as paperwork will pay consultants and save little.

*This piece sets out the arguments on each side; the recommendations are the author's. Facts checked to 30 October 2026.*

## The lifecycle at a glance

The law requires the audit, the plan and the progress updates, but not the investment at stage 6. Savings reach the bill only when the board funds the measures; each new phase then reviews what was done.

- **Scrap or reform.** The Conservatives would remove carbon reporting; Labour would streamline it. Neither plans to abolish ESOS.

- **Relief or duty.** NI parties focus on prices and relief. None has engaged with the audit duty their firms already carry. With an NI version of BICS now funded (28 Oct 2026), the Executive can decide whether relief comes with audit conditions.

- **GB or NI.** BICS and the earlier Supercharger exclude NI because of the all-island electricity market. ESOS covers NI in full. The Budget (28 Oct 2026) funds the Executive to build its own BICS instead.

- **Homes or firms.** Devolved efficiency agendas lean towards homes.

## Where the pillars compete

- **Compliance cost against savings (E against S).** The 2014 impact assessment expected an average saving of only about 0.7% per firm, and the SECR review found costs about three times the estimate. For a mid-size firm, fees can take most of the first year's savings.

- **Audits without a duty to act.** Only 6% of Phase 1 measures were directly attributed to ESOS. Phase 3 audits found 47 TWh of potential savings, but DESNZ had no data on delivery (PIR, 2025).

- **Relief against fuel poverty (E against S).** GB funds BICS from the Exchequer and savings elsewhere in the energy system ([written statement, 16 Apr 2026](https://hansard.parliament.uk/html/Commons/2026-04-16/WrittenStatements)). NI's senior energy official said an NI version was hard to fund without harming fuel poverty work (reported early 2026). Exchequer funding for an NI version, announced on 28 Oct 2026, eases this if it stays off household bills.

- **Efficiency against electrification (E against E).** With the net zero element postponed, Phase 4 audits can favour cheap fixes to gas plant and lock in fossil equipment for another decade.

- **Rebound (E against E).** Lower unit energy costs can expand output and claw back savings. ESOS counts kWh saved per measure, not absolute use.

## What it means for Northern Ireland firms and councils

- **Check status on 31 December 2026.** You qualify with 250 or more staff, or turnover over £44m and a balance sheet over £38m. Group membership counts.

- **Plan to the deadlines.** Use a 12-month reference period that overlaps 31 Dec 2026 and ends before 5 Dec 2027. Get lead assessor and board sign-off. Notify on MESOS by 5 Dec 2027, then the action plan by 5 Dec 2028.

- **Phase 3 firms: second progress update due 5 Dec 2026.** The Phase 4 report must explain dropped measures.

- **Penalties are real.** Failing to carry out an audit can cost up to £50,000. Failing to notify or keep records can cost £5,000 plus £500 a day for up to 80 days ([Linklaters, 4 Aug 2026](https://sustainablefutures.linklaters.com/post/102mo34/uk-preparing-for-esos-phase-4)).

- **Councils are usually outside ESOS.** GOV.UK says public sector bodies do not usually need to comply. Council-owned companies should check their own status.

- **NI support is narrower.** No IETF, and the funded NI version of BICS is not yet designed (28 Oct 2026). Invest NI's Energy Efficiency Capital Grant (up to £150,000) and Technical Consultancy can fund follow-through. The €13m NI share of the Shared Island Sustainability Challenge Fund opened 12 Oct 2026.

- **Cross-border groups face two regimes.** Sites in the Republic face EU audit duties above 10 TJ and energy management systems above 85 TJ by 11 Oct 2027.

## Practical examples

- **Savings found, delivery unknown.** Phase 3 audits identified 47 TWh a year of potential savings, about 5% of participants' energy use. DESNZ said it had no data on savings actually achieved ([ESOS review, April 2025](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/689c6b3cd2a1b0d5d1bb1260/esos-pir-2025.pdf)).

- **An NI firm that acted.** Simplyfruit in Craigavon used an Invest NI energy efficiency grant. In a year it saved over £16,000 and 200,000 kWh, and cut over 50 tonnes of CO2 ([DfE, 30 Aug 2022](https://www.economy-ni.gov.uk/news/economy-minister-welcomes-energy-efficiency-northern-ireland-businesses-following-covid-scheme)).

- **Cash is the barrier for smaller firms.** A Utility Regulator study of 42 NI small businesses found they valued efficiency but could not meet upfront costs. Few knew what support existed ([Utility Regulator, 14 Oct 2026](https://www.uregni.gov.uk/news-centre/small-business-consumer-experience-energy-stress-lived-experience-report-published)).

- **NI manufacturers outside GB relief.** Jim Shannon (DUP) said Magellan Aerospace, T.G. Eakin and Mash Direct may not qualify for BICS. Peter Kyle replied that the Government was working with the Assembly on NI help ([Commons, 16 Apr 2026](https://parallelparliament.co.uk/debate/2026-04-16/commons/commons-chamber/british-industrial-competitiveness-scheme)).

- **Cross-border money for follow-through.** The €26m Shared Island Sustainability Challenge Fund, with a €13m NI share, backs projects in flexible electrification and hard-to-abate industry. Invest NI, Enterprise Ireland and InterTradeIreland deliver it ([News Letter, 12 Oct 2026](https://www.newsletter.co.uk/business/major-opportunity-northern-ireland-businesses-invited-to-bid-for-share-of-eu26m-sustainability-fund-9282644)).

## Recommendations

1.  **DESNZ: publish the SECR and ESOS consultation before Phase 4 audits are bought.** Firms need the rules before paying for 2027 work.

2.  **DESNZ: consult on a single energy and carbon data return feeding SECR, ESOS and UK SRS.** It would remove double counting and cut fees.

3.  **DESNZ and the Environment Agency: publish Phase 3 implementation data first.** Any duty to act should rest on evidence.

4.  **DfE: link Invest NI capital grants to ESOS action plan measures.** The audit then becomes a route into funding.

5.  **NI Executive and DfE: make the funded NI version of BICS conditional on cost-effective audit measures.** The Budget (28 Oct 2026) gave funding but no design, so conditions can be built in from the start. Relief should buy lower demand as well as lower bills.

6.  **NI Executive: say whether and when it intends a non-domestic MEES.** Landlords and tenants need a timetable or a clear no.

7.  **Firms: book a lead assessor early in 2027 and combine ESOS with a carbon inventory and an energy contract review.** One data set then serves SECR, customers and CBAM.

8.  **Boards: set a payback threshold now and fund measures that meet it.** ESOS pays back only when the action plan is carried out.
