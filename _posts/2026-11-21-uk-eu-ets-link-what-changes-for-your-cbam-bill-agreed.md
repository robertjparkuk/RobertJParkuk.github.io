At the summit in Brussels on 20 November 2026 the UK and the EU agreed in principle to link their emissions trading systems. That is the first step towards each side exempting the other's goods from its carbon border charge. It is not the last, and nothing changes for your 2027 bill yet.

## What still has to happen

- **The agreement itself.** It has to be signed and brought into force by both sides.
- **The UK side.** HM Treasury can exclude goods from a linked country from the UK CBAM only by regulations under section 156 of the [Finance Act 2026](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2026/11/part/5), approved by both Houses of Parliament.
- **The EU side.** The UK has to be added to Annex III of the [CBAM Regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32023R0956) by a Commission delegated act, which Parliament and Council can object to.

Until both are in force, both CBAMs apply as they stand. HMRC's [policy summary](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism-cbam-policy-summary/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism-cbam-policy-summary) still says no jurisdiction is exempt from the UK CBAM.

## What it means for you

- **You import steel, aluminium, cement, fertiliser or hydrogen into NI from Ireland or the EU.** The UK CBAM still starts on 1 January 2027. Keep records from that date. Once an exemption is in force, EU-made goods would come out of the charge. Goods made elsewhere and sold through an EU merchant would not.
- **You import from outside Europe.** Nothing changes. The UK CBAM applies in full.
- **You make goods in Northern Ireland.** They are already outside the EU CBAM: the European Commission says "Goods produced and/or released for free circulation in Northern Ireland are not subject to CBAM" ([CBAM Q&A, Q 7.14](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/document/download/013fa763-5dce-4726-a204-69fec04d5ce2_en?filename=CBAM_Questions+and+Answers.pdf)).
- **You supply EU customers from Great Britain.** Your customers still owe the EU CBAM on your goods until the EU exemption is in force. They will still want your emissions data.

## What to do in December

Check the origin on your 2026 invoices, set up CBAM records for 1 January, and plan your 2027 budget on both charges applying. Treat an exemption as upside when it arrives.
