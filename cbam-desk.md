The UK CBAM starts on 1 January 2027. The EU CBAM has applied to imports since 1 January 2026; EU importers pay for 2026 goods when certificates go on sale on 1 February 2027, at the price fixed for the quarter the goods arrived. If you bring steel, aluminium, cement, fertiliser or hydrogen into the UK, including into Northern Ireland from Ireland or the EU, or your EU customers are asking for the emissions behind what you sell them, you need answers that will stand up, and you need to know the cost before you start.

## Two ways this reaches you

**1. You import CBAM goods into the UK.** From 1 January 2027 you are liable once the CBAM goods you import pass £50,000 in value: on any day, if you expect £50,000 or more in the next 30 days, or on the first of the month, if you brought in £50,000 or more in the last 12 months. Register by 31 January 2028; the first return and payment for 2027 are due by 31 May 2028. No country is exempt from the UK CBAM today, and that includes the EU.

**2. You sell CBAM goods from Great Britain to EU customers.** Your EU customer is the importer. Its CBAM declaration needs your verified emissions, or it must use default values plus a mark-up. Certificates go on sale on 1 February 2027; the first declaration, for 2026 imports, is due by 30 September 2027.

**Buy from a UK stockholder?** You are probably not the importer, so you are not liable, but expect a CBAM surcharge in 2027 prices. Check your contracts and Incoterms.

**Make goods in Northern Ireland and sell them into the EU?** The European Commission says "Goods produced and/or released for free circulation in Northern Ireland are not subject to CBAM" ([CBAM Q&A](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/document/download/013fa763-5dce-4726-a204-69fec04d5ce2_en?filename=CBAM_Questions+and+Answers.pdf), Q 7.14, updated 15 October 2026). Your EU customer has no CBAM declaration to make on them. It may still ask for figures for its own carbon reporting, and the [NI CBAM status letter](#ni-cbam-status-letter) answers that.

**Bring CBAM goods into NI from Ireland or the EU?** Finance Act 2026, section 144(5)(a), treats "a Union good that is imported into the United Kingdom as a result of its entry into Northern Ireland" as an import, and no regulations exclude it. With no customs declaration, the importer is "the person on whose behalf the good is imported" (section 146(4)), which is usually you. HMRC's guidance does not yet cover this case, and some Invest NI material describes it differently. Plan on the basis that the UK CBAM applies, count these purchases from your invoices towards the £50,000, and take customs advice before January. I am asking HMRC and will publish the answer here.

## Is your product in scope?

**Already in scope, in both regimes:** steel structures and parts (7308); tanks, drums, casks and gas cylinders (7309–7311); bolts, nuts, screws and washers (7318); other steel articles (7326); iron and steel in chapter 72, including wire; aluminium foil, including blister foil (7607); aluminium structures (7610); aluminium cans, tubes, aerosol and other containers (7612, 7613); other aluminium articles (7616); cement; nitrogen fertilisers, nitric acid and ammonia; hydrogen. All of these count together towards the UK's single £50,000 test.

**Not in scope today:** your finished food, drink or medicines; machinery; vehicle parts; plastics; lids, can ends and crown corks (8309).

**Ask a customs adviser:** warehouse racking (7308 or 9403), foil trays (7607 or 7615), and kegs that are hired or returnable.

> **Food, drink or farm business?** What you sell is almost certainly outside CBAM. What you buy may not be: nitrogen fertiliser, empty cans, steel kegs and drums, foil, plain storage tanks, structural steel, bolts and fixings. If you, or a buying group, bring in more than £50,000 of these from outside the UK in any 12 months, you are a UK CBAM importer from 1 January 2027.

Check your exact code on the [Is it a CBAM good?]({{ '/cbam-desk/is-it-a-cbam-good/' | relative_url }}) lookup, or on GOV.UK's sector lists for [iron and steel](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/check-which-goods-are-in-scope-of-carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism-cbam/goods-within-the-iron-and-steel-sector), [aluminium](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/check-which-goods-are-in-scope-of-carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism-cbam/goods-within-the-aluminium-sector) and [fertilisers](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/check-which-goods-are-in-scope-of-carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism-cbam/goods-within-the-fertiliser-sector).

**Proposed, not law:** the EU plans to add steel- and aluminium-heavy downstream goods, such as machinery parts, fabrications and vehicle components, from 1 January 2028 ([COM(2025) 989](https://oeil.europarl.europa.eu/oeil/en/procedure-file?reference=2025%2F0419%28COD%29)). The Council and Parliament are negotiating and the list is not final. There is no UK equivalent, and goods made in NI remain outside the EU CBAM either way.

## Two worked examples

These figures use HMRC's illustrative 2027 rates and published default values; they are not a quote.

**Fertiliser, UK CBAM, 2027.** Calcium ammonium nitrate at 27% nitrogen (CN 3102 40 10) has an HMRC default of 2.120 tCO2e a tonne. At the illustrative rate of £1.45 that is about **£3.07 a tonne**, or about £3,070 for 1,000 tonnes. Ammonium nitrate (CN 3102 30 90) has a default of 2.600, about **£3.77 a tonne**. Fertiliser defaults carry no UK mark-up. The importer pays, which is often the merchant or co-op, not the farm. Check the code on your invoice, because the higher-nitrogen CAN code has a different default. HMRC publishes each quarter's rates by the start of that quarter, so the real rates for January to March 2027 are due by 1 January 2027.

**Default or actual? 500 tonnes of hot-rolled steel coil.**

- **UK, 2027:** 500 t × 2.722 (HMRC default, including the 10% mark-up) × £9.69 = **£13,188**. A mill 20% better than the default would save about £2,638, which is probably less than one verification.
- **EU, goods imported in Q3 2026:** on default values the EU importer owes about **€54,588** after the free-allocation adjustment. Verified data from a mill 20% below the default saves about €29,900, and about €39,800 for 2027 imports.

So verified actual data usually pays for EU customers, because EU defaults carry a 10–30% mark-up, but often does not for UK imports at today's illustrative rates. If a plant is dirtier than the default, it should not verify at all. I run that sum for you.

## What's changing

- **UK–EU emissions trading link.** The UK–EU summit in Brussels on 20 November 2026 may announce a link between the two emissions trading systems. That would start, not finish, the process: each side then needs its own legal act before any CBAM exemption applies, a UK regulation approved by Parliament and an EU delegated act. Plan for both charges in 2027. A link would exempt EU-made goods, not goods made elsewhere and sold through an EU merchant, so check the origin on the invoice.
- **EU phase-in.** The Commission's July 2026 proposal would slow the end of free allocation for CBAM sectors to 2038, so any cost figure for 2028 onwards is a range.
- **UK scope.** HM Treasury is consulting until 20 January 2027 on adding refinery products to the UK CBAM. HMRC's technical feedback window on its monitoring, reporting and verification guidance closes on 13 November 2026.
