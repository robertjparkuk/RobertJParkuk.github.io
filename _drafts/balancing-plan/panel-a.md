The best practicable means defence for light is available only for industrial, trade or business premises and outdoor sports facilities (s.65(13)). The 1978 Order survives only for noise codes and some ancillary articles. | [legislation.gov.uk, CNEA (NI) 2011 Part 7](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/nia/2011/23/part/7) |

| W6 | Omnibus I, the 1,000-employee value-chain cap (items 6, 7) | **Broadly right.** Published in the OJ on 26 Feb 2026 and in force 18 Mar 2026. Scope is over 1,000 employees and over €450m turnover. The third-country test is over €450m EU turnover plus an EU subsidiary or branch over €200m. The live site already gives Directive (EU) 2026/470 and Delegated Reg (EU) 2026/1560 for VSME. | [Latham & Watkins summary](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/EU-Sustainability-Omnibus-Published-in-the-Official-Journal) (law-firm secondary source; confirm on EUR-Lex) |
| W7 | EUDR applies in NI from 30 Dec 2026; soy (item 14) | **Right**, with a nuance. It applies in NI, not GB. Medium and large operators from 30 Dec 2026, micro and small from 30 Jun 2027. Soya is in scope. The duty sits on the operator placing the soy or feed on the market (the importer or compounder), not on the processor. | [GOV.UK, deforestation-free products in NI (updated 11 Nov 2026)](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/moving-exporting-or-placing-deforestation-free-products-on-the-market-in-northern-ireland) |
| W8 | Red diesel for a fabricator's yard telehandler (item 9) | **Wrong in the case study.** From 1 Apr 2022 manufacturing lost its red diesel entitlement UK-wide; the only NI-specific change concerns pleasure craft. A fabricator's telehandler is almost certainly not entitled. | [GOV.UK, Reform of red diesel entitlement](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/reform-of-red-diesel-entitlements/reform-of-red-diesel-and-other-rebated-fuels-entitlement) |
| W9 | Legal basis of trade effluent consent (item 18) | **Right.** It is the Water and Sewerage Services (NI) Order 2006 (SI 2006/3336), Part VI, Chapter III ("Consent for discharge of trade effluent into public sewer"). | [legislation.gov.uk, Part VI Ch III](https://legislation.gov.uk/nisi/2006/3336/part/VI/chapter/III) |

A tenth search was not needed. On drinking water (item 20), the DAERA page found while checking says the NI 2017 Regulations implement the **old** Directive 98/83/EC ([DAERA, drinking water quality](https://www.daera-ni.gov.uk/node/370823)). A5's view is that the recast Directive (EU) 2020/2184 and its PFAS limits are not in the Windsor Framework annexes. The site's own post *Forever chemicals, two rulebooks* argues *for* EU limits in NI law, so they are not law there yet.

## 4. Cross-cutting issues

**Accuracy and internal consistency**
- **CBAM numbers and certainty do not match across the site.** Item 1 says about £19/t of steel, while the CBAM Desk works an example at about £26/t (2.722 × £9.69). Items 1 and 2 state that EU goods entering NI are UK CBAM imports "under the Finance Act 2026" as settled law, while the CBAM Desk calls this "my reading… unconfirmed" and says HMRC has been asked. Pick one wording and one set of figures. A single `_data/cbam.yml` facts file, used by every page, would stop this drifting.
- **`[verify]` flags the site has already answered.** Examples are the UK CBAM return dates (item 2) and the ESOS regulator in NI (item 27). Clear them from the live pages and primary sources.
- **Circular sourcing.** Many drafts list "Site: …" as a source for regulatory facts (£52.2m pEPR payments, 3.4% price limit, 3 Mar 2026 discharge standards, 23 Oct 2026 PFAS date, 6 Jul 2026 EAC reply). Every regulatory claim in a published page needs a primary source and a date, as the project rules require.

**Legal risk (A5's list, most serious first)**
1. Item 1 and item 2: EU→NI CBAM liability stated as settled law when the site's own position is "unconfirmed". The risk is a client acting on it.
2. Item 9: red diesel in a manufacturer's telehandler, which has been unlawful since April 2022 (W8). A fictional case study should not model non-compliance without saying so. The fictional company names also need checking against Companies House.
3. Item 15 and the item 16 tool: pEPR duties understated (no PRNs or certificate of compliance for large producers), a threshold boundary bug in the tool, and PPWR described as applying in full in NI (W2, W3).
4. Item 21: statutory nuisance attributed to the 1978 Order as amended. The operative provisions are CNEA (NI) 2011 Part 7 (W4).
5. Item 20: "EU drinking-water limits applied in NI" is not NI law.
6. Item 6: an NI company described as possibly "directly" in CSRD scope. It is reached only through EU subsidiaries or the third-country rule.
7. Item 35: the UK ETS attributed to DESNZ alone, when it is run jointly by the UK ETS Authority, which includes DAERA.
8. Item 27: board sign-off presented as new for Phase 4 when it came in during Phase 3.
9. Item 11: green claims framed only through the consumer regime. Business-to-business claims fall under the Business Protection from Misleading Marketing Regulations 2008.
