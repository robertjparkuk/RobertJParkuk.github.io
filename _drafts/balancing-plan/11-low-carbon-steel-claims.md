## The short answer
You can say what is **true, specific and evidenced**. "Low-carbon steel" on its own is none of those.

## What makes steel and aluminium lower-carbon
- **Route**: electric-arc steel made from scrap averaged about 0.7 tonnes of CO2 per tonne in 2024 against about 2.3 for blast-furnace steel (worldsteel, Sustainability Indicators 2025, route averages).
- **Recycled content**: recycled aluminium needs roughly 5% of the energy of primary aluminium *[industry figure (IAI/WEF), not a primary regulator source: cite the IAI directly or soften]*.
- **Power**: hydro- or low-carbon-grid smelting.

## The evidence that stands up
- An **Environmental Product Declaration (EPD)** to EN 15804, third-party verified, for the actual product you buy.
- Mill certificates that state route and recycled content.
- For your own fabrication: your Scope 1 and 2 per tonne, from an inventory.

## Claims to avoid
"Green steel", "carbon neutral frames", "net zero gates". Each needs evidence you probably do not have, and in business-to-business marketing the Business Protection from Misleading Marketing Regulations 2008 apply; the CMA's Green Claims Code, written for consumer claims, sets the standard most buyers expect: claims to be truthful, clear, substantiated and to consider the full life cycle.

## A claim that works
"Our 2027 frames use electric-arc steel with an EPD showing [x] kg CO2e per tonne, about [y]% below the sector average *[source]*."
