Under the UK CBAM you can report either your producer's **actual, verified emissions** or HMRC's **default value** for the product. Defaults are global for 2027 and 2028 and become country-specific from 2029. Which is cheaper depends on three numbers: how far the producer's real emissions sit below the default, how much you import, and what verification costs.

## The rule of thumb

Chasing actual data pays when:

> (default value − actual emissions) × tonnes imported × CBAM rate > cost of getting verified data

For most small Northern Ireland importers in 2027, **it does not pay**. The CBAM rate starts low (about £26 a tonne of hot-rolled coil under CN 7208 10 00: 2.722 tCO2e × HMRC's illustrative £9.69, as in the CBAM Desk worked example; about £3 to £4 a tonne of ammonium nitrate fertiliser on HMRC's draft defaults) *[Robert decided 22 Nov 2026: £26; checked 23 Nov 2026 against GOV.UK iron and steel default values and illustrative rates]*, so the saving per tonne is small, and a verifier's fee is not.

## When it does pay

- **High volumes.** A few thousand tonnes of steel a year from one mill.
- **A clean producer.** Electric-arc steel or hydro-powered aluminium can sit far below a global default.
- **From 2029.** Country-specific defaults will penalise high-carbon origins; a producer with good data becomes worth more.
- **Your EU customers.** If you also sell into the EU, the same supplier data may be needed anyway.

## What to do now

Ask every supplier outside the UK one question in writing: *"Can you provide verified installation-level emissions for the goods you sell us, and from when?"* Keep the answers. You do not have to use the data in 2027 to benefit from having asked.
