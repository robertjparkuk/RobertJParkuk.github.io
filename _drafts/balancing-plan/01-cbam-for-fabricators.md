Most fabricators think of CBAM as a steelworks problem. It is not. From **1 January 2027** the UK CBAM charges the importer of steel and aluminium goods, and the list goes well past billet and coil. Bolts, nuts, screws and washers (7318), steel structures and parts (7308), tanks, drums and gas cylinders (7309 to 7311), other steel articles (7326), aluminium structures (7610) and other aluminium articles (7616) are all CBAM goods in both the UK and EU regimes.

**The question for a fabricator is not "do I make CBAM goods?" but "do I import them?"**

## Three Northern Ireland cases

1. **You buy fixings from a distributor in Great Britain.** Goods already in GB are not a UK import. No UK CBAM liability for you; check where the distributor sourced them only if your customer asks.
2. **You buy plate and sections from a stockholder in Ireland.** Goods arriving in Northern Ireland from Ireland or the rest of the EU count as UK CBAM imports under the Finance Act 2026. The UK Government said on 20 November 2026 that it intends to exempt EU-origin goods from 1 January 2027. Until regulations are made, the charge is the law, and steel that originated outside the EU (for example Turkish or Indian plate held in a Dublin yard) stays in scope either way.
3. **You import welded mesh direct from outside Europe.** You are the importer. If your CBAM goods from outside the UK pass **£50,000 in any 12 months**, counted together across steel, aluminium, cement, fertiliser and hydrogen, you must register and report.

## Worked example (fictional)

*Antrim Fab Ltd* imports, in 2027: £38,000 of structural sections from a Dublin stockholder (origin: Turkey), £14,000 of stainless fixings from China, and £60,000 of aluminium extrusions from a GB supplier.

- GB extrusions: not an import. Out.
- Fixings from China: in scope.
- Turkish-origin sections via Dublin: in scope on the law at 22 November 2026, and still in scope if the EU-origin exemption is made, because their origin is Turkey.
- Total in scope: **£52,000.** Over the £50,000 test. Antrim Fab is a UK CBAM importer for 2027.

The charge itself is modest for steel in 2027: about **£19 a tonne** of steel from the US, Brazil, China or India on HMRC's 2027 illustration, with little or no carbon price relief. The registration, records and annual return are the larger burden.

## What to do this month

- List every steel and aluminium purchase for the last 12 months by supplier, CN code, origin and value.
- Run your codes through the free [Is it a CBAM good?](/cbam-desk/is-it-a-cbam-good/) lookup.
- If you are near £50,000, ask suppliers outside the UK for their installation's emissions now. Default values apply for 2027 and 2028 if they cannot supply them.

*Not legal or tax advice. Who the importer is, and origin, are customs questions: where they are unclear, take advice from a customs specialist.*
