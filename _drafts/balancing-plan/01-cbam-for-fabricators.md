Most fabricators think of CBAM as a steelworks problem. It is not. From **1 January 2027** the UK CBAM charges the importer of steel and aluminium goods, and the list goes well past billet and coil. Bolts, nuts, screws and washers (7318), steel structures and parts (7308), tanks, drums and gas cylinders (7309 to 7311), other steel articles (7326), aluminium structures (7610) and other aluminium articles (7616) are all CBAM goods in both the UK and EU regimes.

**The question for a fabricator is not "do I make CBAM goods?" but "do I import them?"**

## Three Northern Ireland cases

1. **You buy fixings from a distributor in Great Britain.** Goods already in GB are not a UK import. No UK CBAM liability for you; check where the distributor sourced them only if your customer asks.
2. **You buy plate and sections from a stockholder in Ireland.** On my reading of the Finance Act 2026 (section 144(5)(a)), goods arriving in Northern Ireland from Ireland or the rest of the EU count as UK CBAM imports; HMRC has not yet confirmed this. The UK Government said on 20 November 2026 that it intends to exempt goods originating in the EU, the EEA and Switzerland from 1 January 2027 ([UK-EU ETS linking agreement: explainer](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/uk-eu-linking-agreement-explainer/uk-eu-linking-agreement-explainer) (DESNZ, 20 November 2026)). The power to do this is in section 156 of the Finance Act 2026: Treasury regulations, by the affirmative procedure. Steel rolled in GB and bought back through a Dublin stockholder is UK-origin and out either way. Third-country steel brought into Ireland and then into NI could, in principle, meet the EU CBAM and then the UK CBAM The UK relief rules (SI 2026/809) extend carbon price relief to a price paid under another country's CBAM, so a double charge should be relievable, with HMRC's carbon pricing verification form *[checked 23 Nov 2026 against the GOV.UK CBAM policy update; confirm the form name at publication]*. Until regulations are made, the charge is the law, and steel that originated outside the EU (for example Turkish or Indian plate held in a Dublin yard) stays in scope either way.
3. **You import welded mesh direct from outside Europe.** You are the importer. The £50,000 threshold is two tests on GOV.UK: a forward test (do you expect to pass £50,000 of CBAM goods in the next 30 days?) and a backward test (have you passed it in the last 12 months, checked on the first of each month?), counted together across steel, aluminium, cement, fertiliser and hydrogen. Goods of UK origin are excluded. Pass either test and you must register and report *[link the GOV.UK threshold guidance]*.

## Worked example (fictional)

*[Fictional firm; name to be checked against Companies House]* imports, in 2027: £38,000 of structural sections from a Dublin stockholder (origin: Turkey), £14,000 of stainless fixings from China, and £60,000 of aluminium extrusions from a GB supplier.

- GB extrusions: not an import. Out.
- Fixings from China: in scope.
- Turkish-origin sections via Dublin: in scope on my reading of the Act at 22 November 2026, and still in scope if the EU-origin exemption is made, because their origin is Turkey.
- Total in scope: **£52,000.** Over £50,000 on the 12-month test, so the firm is a UK CBAM importer for 2027.

The charge itself is modest in 2027: on HMRC's default value for hot-rolled coil under CN 7208 10 00 (2.722 tCO2e a tonne, including the 10% mark-up) and HMRC's illustrative iron and steel rate of £9.69 (published 16 October 2026 from a Q3 2026 UK ETS price of £58.15), about **£26 a tonne**, with little or no carbon price relief. Defaults are set per commodity code, so other steel goods differ: check your own code on GOV.UK's iron and steel default values page. The illustrative rate is not the 2027 rate; HMRC publishes the real quarterly rate before each quarter starts. The registration, records and annual return are the larger burden.

## What to do this month

- List every steel and aluminium purchase for the last 12 months by supplier, CN code, origin and value.
- Run your codes through the free [Is it a CBAM good?](/cbam-desk/is-it-a-cbam-good/) lookup.
- If you are near £50,000, ask suppliers outside the UK for their installation's emissions now. Default values apply for 2027 and 2028 if they cannot supply them.

*Not legal or tax advice. Who the importer is, and origin, are customs questions: where they are unclear, take advice from a customs specialist.*
