Most noise and light complaints never reach court. They are settled, or not, in the first fortnight, by how the business responds.

## What the council can do *[verify sections]*
- **Investigate** a complaint and decide whether it amounts to a **statutory nuisance**: something that unreasonably interferes with the use of someone's property, or harms health. In Northern Ireland that covers noise and, since 2011, artificial light.
- **Serve an abatement notice.** Breaching it is an offence. For trade premises, the defence of "best practicable means" may apply.
- **Use planning conditions** on hours, lighting and plant, and enforce them.

## What a business should do first
1. **Listen and log.** Note the complaint, time, source and conditions.
2. **Measure.** A basic sound-level reading and a photo of light spill at night.
3. **Fix the cheap things first**: hood or re-aim floodlights, timers and motion sensors, reversing alarms that use broadband sound, doors shut during noisy work.
4. **Write back** to the neighbour and the council with what you have done.
5. **Record "best practicable means"**: the steps you took and why further steps are not practicable.

## Light is a carbon and nature issue too
Lighting left on wastes electricity; artificial light is one of the causes the Natural History Museum gives for the fall in UK moth numbers. A lighting review can cut cost, complaints and harm at once.
