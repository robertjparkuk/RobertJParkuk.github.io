Most consultation responses change nothing. The ones that do share five habits.

1. **Respond early in the process, not on the last day.** Ask to meet officials while the policy is still forming; the consultation is the end of a conversation, not the start.
2. **Answer the questions asked**, in their order, and say plainly which you are not answering.
3. **Give evidence officials can use**: your numbers, costs, examples from your site. One firm's real figure beats a page of opinion.
4. **Propose wording.** If you want a change, draft it.
5. **Follow up.** Ask when the outcome will be published and what happened to your points. Publish your response if you can.

## Northern Ireland specifics
- Check for the **equality screening** and **rural needs** documents: they often reveal what officials think the impacts are.
- Many NI consultations run in parallel with a UK or EU process: say which regime your point is about.
- Council consultations on Local Development Plans have their own stages and tests.

## Trade bodies
Coordinate members' evidence; avoid identical campaign responses, which are counted but rarely weighed.
