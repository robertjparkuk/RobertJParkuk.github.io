## CBAM and CSRD

<figure class="page-photo"><img src="{{ '/assets/photos/shipping-containers.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="Stacked shipping containers at a port" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Two carbon regimes, one supply chain.</figcaption></figure>

Northern Ireland manufacturers often trade into both the UK and EU markets, which means navigating two sets of carbon rules at once: the UK CBAM applies across the UK, including Northern Ireland, from 1 January 2027, while goods made in Northern Ireland are outside the EU CBAM. I help you work out:

- whether the UK and EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanisms (CBAM) and the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) affect you, directly or through your customers
- whether you are the importer at all: goods arriving in Northern Ireland from Ireland or the rest of the EU count as UK CBAM imports under the Finance Act 2026, and bolts, brackets, tanks, drums, cans, kegs and foil are CBAM goods, not just raw steel and aluminium
- what emissions and supply-chain data you'll need, and where to get it
- how to prepare without building a reporting burden you don't need

## Carbon inventories

<figure class="page-photo"><img src="{{ '/assets/photos/food-manufacturing.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="Food production workers at a stainless-steel line" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Every product has a footprint behind it.</figcaption></figure>

A carbon inventory measures your organisation's greenhouse gas emissions across its operations and supply chain. Done well, it gives you:

- a credible baseline for customers, lenders and tender requirements
- better conversations with suppliers and customers about where emissions really sit
- non-financial management information your board can use

I lead the work, with carbon accountants where the volume needs them. If you need the figures verified, that is done independently by a UKAS-accredited verifier, who reaches their own opinion.

## Water, waste and packaging

<figure class="page-photo"><img src="{{ '/assets/photos/packaging-conveyor.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="Packaged goods moving along a factory conveyor" loading="lazy"><figcaption>What goes in the drain, the skip and the box is money leaving the site.</figcaption></figure>

For most manufacturers, water, waste and packaging are where environmental rules meet the monthly costs most directly. Packaging producers now carry extended producer responsibility across the UK, including Northern Ireland; waste is moving from paper records to digital tracking; and what a site sends to sewer or to a watercourse needs the right consent. I work on this collaboratively with specialist partners, and help you with:

- **Packaging:** whether you are an obligated producer under packaging extended producer responsibility (pEPR), what data you report, and how packaging choices change what you pay
- **Waste:** your duty of care, waste data and the move to digital waste tracking; cutting what you pay to landfill by separating streams at source
- **Water:** mapping where water enters and leaves your process, trade effluent and discharge consents, and the carbon in water supply and treatment
- **Councils:** organic waste, recycling centre access and the cost of litter and fly-tipping on the public realm

The water, waste and packaging rules sit under both UK and, for goods, EU regimes in Northern Ireland; I check which applies to you before advising.

## Energy costs and ESOS

<figure class="page-photo"><img src="{{ '/assets/photos/plant-room.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="Pipework and plant in an industrial plant room" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Most energy savings are found in the plant room, not the report.</figcaption></figure>

Energy is the cost most Northern Ireland manufacturers can act on fastest. Northern Ireland sits on the all-island electricity market, not the Great Britain grid, so prices, network charges and the carbon figure for your electricity all work differently. I help you with:

- **Energy costs:** a review of your contracts, bills and metering, so you know what you pay for, when your contract ends and what your options are
- **Where the savings are:** a plant-room view of the biggest users, so changes are costed and ranked rather than guessed
- **ESOS:** for large undertakings, delivered with ESOS assessors and signed off by a Lead Assessor

ESOS applies to large undertakings: 250 or more staff, or turnover above £44 million with a balance sheet above £38 million. Phase 4 qualification is judged on 31 December 2026, with notification by 5 December 2027. If that is you, I lead ESOS with experienced Lead Assessors, and recommend running it alongside a carbon inventory and a review of your energy contracts so the work produces savings rather than just compliance. If you are smaller, ESOS does not apply to you: a carbon inventory and an energy-contract review give you the same savings without the compliance overhead.

## Pollution and environmental health

<figure class="page-photo"><img src="{{ '/assets/photos/young-tree.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="A young tree growing on rough ground" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Air, water, land and the street outside the gate.</figcaption></figure>

Pollution is rarely one thing. A site can have an air permit, a discharge consent and a neighbour complaining about noise, all at once. I lead this work with a multidisciplinary team, with monitoring and testing subcontracted, across four areas:

- **Air:** emissions, dust and odour from your processes; what your permit or registration requires; fuel and boiler choices that cut both pollution and carbon
- **Water:** run-off, spills and discharges to sewer or watercourses; pollution prevention on yards, tanks and drains; what to do when something goes wrong
- **Land:** contamination from past and present uses; see [Contaminated land](#contaminated-land) below
- **The public realm:** noise, artificial light, litter, fly-tipping and other nuisance that reaches neighbours, roads and open spaces; for businesses facing complaints, and for councils managing them

## PFAS (forever chemicals)

PFAS are a large family of very persistent chemicals found in firefighting foams, coatings, lubricants, textiles and some process aids. In Northern Ireland, EU chemicals rules continue to apply to goods under the Windsor Framework, alongside UK rules, so a manufacturer can face two sets of restrictions on the same product. I lead this work with technical support on operations, and help you:

- find where PFAS may be in your products, processes, foams and supply chain
- work out which UK and EU restrictions reach you, and when
- plan substitution and what to tell customers, without over-claiming
