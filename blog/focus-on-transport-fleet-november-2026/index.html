Focus on Transport & Fleet · 9 November 2026

Transport emissions are 9% above 1990. NI has the fewest public chargers in the UK.

Moving people and goods on an island economy: what fleet operators, hauliers and employers can do while the infrastructure catches up.

Domestic transport produced 3.9 million tonnes of CO2e in Northern Ireland in 2024: 21% of the total and 9% more than in 1990. With agriculture and fuel supply, it is one of only three sectors whose emissions have risen since 1990, and road vehicles are the main reason. NI has the lowest density of public chargers in the UK, interest in buying an electric car has fallen again, and public transport has only just passed its pre-COVID level. For businesses, fleet and freight are where action pays fastest.

Where emissions sitRoad traffic drives the total

Cars, vans and lorries are the main source of NI transport emissions and the main reason for the rise since 1990, although emissions are still a little below their pre-COVID level. The inventory also counts leaks from vehicle air conditioning and refrigerated transport here. When a vehicle goes electric its emissions move to the electricity grid, and half of NI's electricity now comes from renewables, so each switch gets cleaner as the grid does.

ChargingLowest in the UK on both measures

At 1 July 2026, NI had 60 public EV chargers per 100,000 people, against 174.9 across the UK and 231.6 in Scotland. For rapid chargers (50 kW and above), NI had 19.3 per 100,000 against a UK average of 41.7.

The gap shows in attitudes. In NISRA's 2024/25 survey, only 19% of drivers said they would definitely or strongly consider an EV as their next car, down from 25% a year earlier and 38% in 2021/22. Purchase price (75%), the need to recharge (54%) and the availability of public chargers (44%) were the biggest barriers.

For fleets, depot and workplace charging matter more than the public network. Cars and vans that return to base each night are the easiest to switch.

CostsWhat has changed for business vehicles

  • Electric Car Grant. The UK Government's grant is open in NI, to private buyers and fleets: up to £3,750 off eligible new zero emission cars priced up to £37,000. Funding was raised to £1.3bn in the 2025 Budget and runs to 2030.
  • Company car tax. Benefit-in-kind on zero emission company cars is low but rising: 4% in 2026/27, 5% in 2027/28, 7% in 2028/29 and 9% in 2029/30. Plug-in hybrids rise faster, to 18% and then 19%.
  • Car sales rules. The GB zero emission vehicle mandate, which sets EV sales targets for manufacturers, is made under an Order covering Great Britain only. NI's position depends on separate legislation.
  • Heavier goods. Since 1 August 2026, NI drivers who have held a car (category B) licence for two years can drive zero emission goods vehicles up to 4,250 kg, or up to 5,000 kg where the extra weight is specialist equipment for disabled users.

Freight and the Irish SeaFerries now carry a carbon price

Since 1 July 2026, the UK Emissions Trading Scheme has covered cargo and passenger ships of 5,000 gross tonnes and above on domestic voyages. Sailings between Great Britain and Northern Ireland are included, with a 50% deduction on the allowances operators must surrender, and port stays in NI count in full. Expect the cost to show up in freight and ferry rates, and ask carriers for emissions data: it is part of your Scope 3.

Public transportRail has recovered, buses nearly

Translink carried 84.0 million passengers in 2025-26, up 3.6% on the year and above the 83.4 million of 2019-20. Rail journeys (15.9 million) are now 4.9% above their pre-COVID level; bus journeys (68.1 million) are 0.2% below. For employers, travel plans, cycle-to-work and public transport schemes, and flexible start times are cheap ways to cut commuting emissions, which are part of Scope 3.

This monthThree things a fleet operator can do

  1. List every vehicle with its annual mileage, fuel use and typical daily route. Vehicles that do under 150 miles a day and return to base are your first EV candidates.
  2. Get a quote for depot or workplace charging, and check your site's grid connection capacity before you order vehicles.
  3. Record grey-fleet mileage (staff using their own cars for work). It is often larger than expected and belongs in your Scope 3.

Reading these figures with care

  • Inventory. Emissions are counted where fuel is burned. EV charging is counted under electricity supply.
  • Chargers. DfT replaced "charging devices" with an "EV chargers" measure from January 2026, so older figures are not directly comparable. The 1 July 2026 figures are the latest quarterly release at 9 November 2026; the next, for 1 October, is due on 25 November.
  • Attitudes survey. Covers 2024/25 and only respondents with a driving licence.
  • Company car rates apply to UK tax years starting 6 April.
