DAERA's balance counts all nitrogen and phosphorus inputs together and does not split out manufactured fertiliser, so it cannot by itself size Scope 3 fertiliser manufacture. Output per hectare is up two-thirds while the surplus is flat: farms are using nitrogen better, but the pressure on soils and water has not fallen. The jump in inputs in 2024 is worth watching.

Animals then and now

Head of livestock, 1995 (nearest available to 1990) and June 2025.

Livestock19952025Change
Cattle numbers are roughly flat since the mid-1990s, but the herd has shifted towards dairy, and dairy cows emit more methane per head. Sheep are down by a third, while pigs and poultry are up. The rise in pigs and poultry matters more for ammonia and manure than for methane.

Where F-gases are used

ktCO2e by inventory sector. Farm refrigeration (milk cooling, cold stores) sits inside these lines; it is not reported separately.

Sector19952024Change
The two direct high-GWP gases from farming are methane and nitrous oxide, shown above. Fluorinated gases are a small share of NI emissions and are now falling. The EU F-gas Regulation (EU) 2024/573 applies in NI under the Windsor Framework, so farm refrigeration and heat-pump choices should move to low-GWP refrigerants now.
