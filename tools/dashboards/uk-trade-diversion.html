Trade diversion dashboard

Trade diversion means trade shifting from one partner to another because barriers changed, not because price or quality did. The EU took 42.4% of UK goods exports in 2025, against 43.7% in 2015 and 56.1% in 2000. It supplied 45.3% of UK goods imports in 2025, against 53.4% in 2015. Source: HMRC. A falling share on its own does not prove diversion: see the notes.

What this can and cannot show

The EU's share of UK exports was already falling before 2016, as faster-growing markets took more. So the change since 2015 mixes a long-run trend with the effect of new barriers from 2021, the pandemic and the 2022 energy price spike. These totals include gold and other erratic items, which swing the non-EU figures (trade with Switzerland is mostly gold). The EU is counted with its membership at the time. Two changes of method break the series: from January 2021 HMRC counts Great Britain's exports to the EU from customs declarations instead of the Intrastat survey, and from January 2022 it does the same for Great Britain's imports from the EU; Northern Ireland's EU trade is still collected by Intrastat. HMRC found a break of about 6% by value in EU imports between 2021 and 2022. Goods only: services are not included. Values are in cash terms, not adjusted for inflation.
