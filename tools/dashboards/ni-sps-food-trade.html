NI food, farming and the SPS deal: the numbers

Northern Ireland (NI) food and drink processors sell 49% of their output to Great Britain (GB) and 26% to the European Union (EU). The UK–EU sanitary and phytosanitary (SPS) deal on food, animal and plant health, due to apply from 1 March 2028, removes most food checks on GB processors selling into the EU, though, unlike NI firms, they still face customs. What were "EU intervention stores"?

Under the old Common Agricultural Policy (CAP) of the European Community, when market prices fell below a set floor, each member state's intervention agency had to buy beef, butter, skimmed milk powder and cereals at a guaranteed price and keep them in cold store until prices recovered. The agencies were national and the Community paid; the EU did not exist until 1993. In the UK the buyer was the Intervention Board for Agricultural Produce, with the Department of Agriculture for Northern Ireland, and from August 1990 the Livestock Marketing Commission for Northern Ireland, supervising work here.

In 1990 NI processors sold £128m to intervention, 7% of their sales and more than they sold to Ireland (£122m) or the rest of the EU (£100m). Beef was the main product. By June 1991 the Community held about 760,000 tonnes of beef, 235,000 tonnes of it in Ireland. The 1992 MacSharry reforms cut support prices and limited buying; by 2015 DAERA records no NI processing sales to intervention. It survives in EU law as a safety net, mainly for butter and skimmed milk powder.

Where the trend points

Up to £8.7bn by 2035, in 2015 prices

If each subsector keeps its 2015–22 real growth rate, food processing turnover rises from £5,442m in 2022 to £7,198m by 2030 and £8,741m by 2035. Growing the total at its own 3.0% a year gives about £6.9bn and £8.0bn instead. A trend, not a forecast, and probably an overstatement: 2022 was a milk price spike and the gross domestic product (GDP) deflator lagged food prices; GB competition in the EU from 2028 bears hardest on beef and dairy; NI carbon budgets may cap cattle numbers.
