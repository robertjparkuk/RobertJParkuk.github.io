CBAM Exposure Tracker

Who in Northern Ireland pays a carbon border charge, from when, and at what price. The EU has charged for the carbon in imported steel, aluminium, cement, fertiliser, hydrogen and electricity since 1 January 2026; the UK starts on 1 January 2027. Figures as at 22 October 2026.

The short version for NI. Goods made in Northern Ireland, or released for free circulation there, are outside the EU CBAM. The UK CBAM applies across the whole UK, including NI, so the charge NI firms face is on what they import from outside the UK, from 1 January 2027.

EU customers may still ask for your product emissions for their own reporting, and NI groups with an EU company importing CBAM goods are caught on that side.

Where an NI business stands

Common situations under each regime. A summary of official guidance, not advice: check it against your own goods, commodity codes and routes.

Where an NI business stands under each CBAM
Your situationEU CBAMUK CBAMWhat to do
You make goods in NI and sell them to Ireland or elsewhere in the EUNot subject. Goods produced in NI are outside the EU CBAM.Not relevant: these are exports.No CBAM declaration is needed for these goods. Customers may still ask for product emissions for their own reporting, so keep the figures to hand.
You import steel, aluminium, cement, fertiliser or hydrogen into NI from outside the UKNot subject. Goods released for free circulation in NI are outside the EU CBAM.Liable from 1 January 2027 once CBAM goods pass £50,000 (30-day forward or 12-month backward test). Register by 31 January 2028; first return and payment by 31 May 2028.Check commodity codes and import values now. Ask suppliers for verified emissions per tonne, or plan to use default values. Keep records for six years.
You send goods from NI to the EU for processing and bring them backNot the issue here.Outward processing rules apply. A UK carbon price already paid on the UK-origin goods can reduce the UK CBAM due.Keep the evidence of the UK carbon price on the original goods.
You export goods from NI to the EU and reimport them unchanged within three yearsNot the issue here.Returned goods relief: no UK CBAM, if the goods come back in the same state within three years. The importer need not be the original exporter.Keep export and reimport records that link the two movements.
Your group has an EU company that imports CBAM goods into the EUThat EU importer is caught: authorised declarant status above 50 tonnes a year, certificates from 1 February 2027, first declaration by 30 September 2027 for 2026 imports.Not relevant to the EU import.Make sure the EU company has applied for authorisation and is collecting supplier emissions data for 2026 imports.
Goods move from Great Britain to NI, or on through NI to the EUGB to NI movements carry no EU CBAM formalities at present. Movements onward to the EU are covered in the Commission's Q&A (question 7.14).GB to NI is an internal UK movement, not an import.Check the Commission's current answer before routing GB-made CBAM goods to EU customers through NI.

The UK and EU are negotiating a link between their emissions trading systems that could exempt each other's goods from their CBAMs; until it is in force, both charges apply as set out here.
