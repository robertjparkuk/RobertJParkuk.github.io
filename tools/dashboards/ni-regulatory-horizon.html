20 Nov 2026in 9 daysTrade, customs and CBAM · DecisionUK–EU summit in Brussels

The second UK–EU summit. Draft texts reported by the press would conclude talks on linking the UK and EU emissions trading systems, with each side's goods exempt from the other's carbon border levy, and on an agri-food (SPS) agreement. None of this is confirmed, and nothing changes for either CBAM until both sides legislate.

The summit date is confirmed by GOV.UK. The ETS link and SPS outcomes are reported from draft texts (Euronews, 6 November 2026), not confirmed. On 10 November 2026 the Council’s Working Party on the United Kingdom discussed a draft agreement linking the two emissions trading systems (Council document CM 4531/26). Any CBAM exemption then needs UK regulations approved by both Houses (Finance Act 2026 s.156) and an EU delegated act adding the UK to Annex III of Regulation (EU) 2023/956.

1 Jan 2027in 51 daysTrade, customs and CBAM · Rule changeUK CBAM starts

The UK carbon border levy applies across the UK, NI included, to imported aluminium, cement, fertiliser, hydrogen, iron and steel of any non-UK origin, EU included: HMRC says no jurisdiction is currently exempt. Liability starts once covered imports pass £50,000: looking back over the last 12 months (tested on the first of each month) or forward over the next 30 days (tested on any day). Union goods entering NI from Ireland or the EU count as imports under Finance Act 2026 s.144(5)(a); HMRC guidance does not yet cover this case. Charged on direct emissions, using global default values in 2027 and 2028 where supplier data is missing.

Default values from Budget 2026 (HC 726, para 6.217): global in 2027 and 2028, country-specific from 2029.

1 Jan 2027in 51 daysCarbon · Rule changeEU F-gas bans on new cooling and heat pump kit; HFC supply halves

Under the EU F-gas Regulation (EU) 2024/573, which applies in NI, these can no longer be placed on the market: plug-in and other self-contained air conditioning and heat pumps up to 50 kW with refrigerant GWP 150 or more; split air-to-water heat pumps up to 12 kW with GWP 150 or more; chillers up to 12 kW with GWP 150 or more and above 12 kW with GWP 750 or more (safety exceptions apply). The EU's maximum HFC quantity falls from 42.9 to 21.7 million tonnes CO2e a year for 2027–2029. Great Britain keeps its own rules.

Kit placed on the market before 1 January 2027 can still be installed and serviced.

How to read this page

Status

In force
The law behind it already applies; the date is a step within it.
Confirmed
The date is set in law, in an adopted EU act or by an official announcement.
Expected
An official target or timetable, not yet fixed in law or liable to move.
Proposed
In consultation or draft; it may change or not happen.

Where it applies

NI
Applies in Northern Ireland: Stormont rules, and EU rules that reach NI under the Windsor Framework.
UK
Applies across the UK, Northern Ireland included (some UK taxes apply in England and NI only; the row says so).
GB
Applies in Great Britain only. Listed where it affects NI firms with GB sites, or opens a gap with NI.
EU
An EU rule that does not apply in NI as such, but reaches NI firms through EU customers, subsidiaries or later adoption.
UK–EU
A decision taken jointly by the UK and the EU.

Northern Ireland sits under two rulebooks. EU rules on goods, chemicals, packaging design, F-gases, batteries and deforestation apply in NI under the Windsor Framework, while UK rules on carbon pricing, CBAM, ESOS and most taxes apply too. Rows marked EU are there because they reach NI firms through EU customers, not because they apply in NI.
