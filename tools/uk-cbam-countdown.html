UK carbon border charge

UK CBAM countdown

The UK Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism starts on 1 January 2027. From then, importers of aluminium, cement, fertiliser, hydrogen, iron and steel pay for the carbon in those goods once their imports pass £50,000. It applies across the whole UK, including Northern Ireland.

The short version for NI. Goods made in Northern Ireland and sold into Ireland or the EU are outside the EU CBAM. The UK CBAM bites on what NI firms import from outside the UK. EU customers will still ask for your product emissions, so have the figures ready.

The dates that follow

  1. 1 January 2027UK CBAM starts. Quarterly sector rates are published by the government from this date.
  2. 1 February 2027EU CBAM certificates go on sale (affects NI groups with an EU importing company).
  3. 30 September 2027First EU CBAM declaration, covering 2026 imports.
  4. 31 January 2028UK CBAM registration deadline for the first year (registration opens 1 January 2028).
  5. 31 May 2028First UK CBAM return and payment, covering 2027 imports.

Five things to do before the clock runs out

  1. List your CBAM goods. Check the commodity codes of everything you import from outside the UK against the CBAM list: aluminium, cement, fertiliser, hydrogen, iron and steel.
  2. Test the £50,000 threshold. Look forward 30 days and back 12 months, on the first of each month. Fixings, brackets and steel sections add up quickly.
  3. Ask suppliers for verified emissions. Actual emissions per tonne usually cost less than default values. Ask now; good data takes months.
  4. Check your contracts and Incoterms. Decide who carries the carbon cost. The Incoterms Chooser helps.
  5. Keep records for six years. Imports, values, emissions data and any carbon price paid abroad, which can reduce what you owe.
