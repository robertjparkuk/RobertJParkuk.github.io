    How it's calculated

    Each figure is multiplied by the matching factor from the UK Government greenhouse gas conversion factors 2026 (DESNZ flat file version 1.2, revised 31 July 2026), looked up by its DESNZ ID. Results are tonnes of CO₂ equivalent.

    • Fuel used, not bought. For stored fuels: opening stock + deliveries − closing stock. An emission happens when fuel is burnt, not when it is delivered.
    • Northern Ireland electricity. NI is part of the all-island Single Electricity Market, not the GB grid, and DESNZ does not publish a separate NI location-based factor. The location-based figure uses the DESNZ UK factor as a disclosed proxy (DESNZ treats Ireland as an interconnector import). The market-based figure uses your supplier's fuel-mix CO2 figure from the Utility Regulator's Fuel Mix Disclosure 2024, or the SEM residual mix of 341 g/kWh (SEM Committee, SEM-25-060) if you're not sure. Those figures are CO2 only and are for 2024.
    • Upstream emissions (well-to-tank) are included for every fuel and for electricity, with grid losses, in Scope 3.
    • Biogenic CO2 from the bio share of pump diesel and petrol, and from wood pellets, is shown separately and not added to the scopes.
    • Flights include the effect of emissions at altitude (radiative forcing). Refrigerants use the Kyoto-gas factors.
    • Not covered: gas or biogas escaping from pipework, digesters or slurry stores (a Scope 1 fugitive emission; count the methane lost, not burnt); more than one refrigerant (add the others to the total by hand); purchased goods and services, freight, capital goods, commuting and the use of products you sell. For most manufacturers those are the largest part of the footprint: screen all 15 Scope 3 categories before quoting a total.
    • The 2026 UK electricity factor is 26% lower than 2025, partly from a change in DESNZ's method (DESNZ major changes report 2026). Recalculate earlier years on the same factors before comparing.
