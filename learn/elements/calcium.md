## Climate and carbon

Cement and lime making release CO₂ from limestone itself as well as from fuel; cement is covered by EU and UK CBAM. Lime spread on soil also releases some CO₂.

## Land, soil and water

Lime raises soil pH; AFBI's soil testing has found most Northern Ireland fields below the target pH. Calcium carbonate shells and chalk lock up carbon for millions of years.

## Health and safety

Essential for bones and teeth. Quicklime burns skin and eyes. Calcium metal reacts with water (UN1401); quicklime is Skin Irrit. and Eye Dam. 1.

## Industry and supply

Cement, lime, plaster, steelmaking flux and flue-gas cleaning.

## Engineering

Lime mortar lets old buildings breathe; cement mortar can trap damp in them.

## Politics and trade

Cement is covered by EU CBAM and, from 2027, by UK CBAM.

## Commerce

Cement is heavy and cheap, so it rarely travels far by road.

## In Northern Ireland

The white chalk of the Antrim coast sits under black basalt: the two are the backbone of the Causeway Coast.

## Quirks

The White Cliffs of Dover and the Antrim chalk are the skeletons of tiny sea creatures.

## Remarkable compounds

| Compound | Why it is remarkable |
|---|---|
| Calcium carbonate | chalk, limestone and eggshell |
| Gypsum | plaster and plasterboard |
| Calcium carbide | reacts with water to make acetylene, once burned in miners' lamps |
| Hydroxyapatite | the mineral of bone |

## Discovery

Isolated by Humphry Davy in 1808; from Latin 'calx', lime. Stable.
