## Climate and carbon

Magnesium-rich rocks absorb CO₂ as they weather; spreading crushed basalt on farmland (enhanced rock weathering) is being trialled as carbon removal. Making magnesium metal, mostly in China, is carbon intensive.

## Land, soil and water

Magnesium sits at the centre of chlorophyll. Dolomitic lime supplies it; heavy potash and nitrogen dressings make it harder for grass and livestock to take up.

## Health and safety

Grass tetany (staggers) kills cattle and sheep short of magnesium on lush spring grass. In people, magnesium supports nerves and muscles. Burning magnesium cannot be put out with water. See the magnesium briefing safety tab: powder and swarf are fire risks.

## Industry and supply

Light alloys for cars and laptops, refractories, animal feed, fertiliser and de-icing salts. Magnesium is on the UK critical minerals list.

## In Northern Ireland

The Antrim Plateau is basalt rich in magnesium minerals, a ready source of rock dust for weathering trials.

## Remarkable compounds

| Compound | Why it is remarkable |
|---|---|
| Chlorophyll | the green pigment built around one magnesium atom |
| Epsom salt (magnesium sulfate) | named after the spring at Epsom in Surrey |
| Magnesium hydroxide | milk of magnesia and a flame retardant |
| Struvite | magnesium ammonium phosphate, recovered from wastewater as fertiliser |

## Discovery

Isolated by Humphry Davy in 1808; named from Magnesia, a district in Greece. Stable.
