## Climate and carbon

Electrification runs on copper: cables, motors, transformers and wind turbines. Mining and smelting are energy intensive and demand is rising.

## Land, soil and water

Copper is toxic to aquatic life. Pig slurry, copper fungicides and antifouling paint raise it in soils and sediments.

## Health and safety

An essential trace nutrient, but sheep are poisoned by small excesses. Copper surfaces kill many bacteria. Copper powder: Aquatic hazards. Massive metal not classified.

## Industry and supply

Wiring, plumbing, motors, heat exchangers, brass and bronze.

## Remarkable compounds

| Compound | Why it is remarkable |
|---|---|
| Copper sulfate | blue crystals used in Bordeaux mixture fungicide |
| Verdigris | the green patina on old roofs and statues |
| Haemocyanin | the copper protein that makes octopus and crab blood blue |
| Copper(I) oxide | red antifouling paint for boats |

## Discovery

Used since about 9000 BC; symbol Cu from Latin 'cuprum', from Cyprus. Stable.
