## Climate and carbon

Cobalt stabilises many lithium-ion cathodes and goes into turbine superalloys. Battery makers are cutting it out where they can.

## Land, soil and water

Sheep on cobalt-poor ground suffer 'pine', a wasting disease, because rumen microbes need cobalt to make vitamin B12.

## Health and safety

Part of vitamin B12. Some cobalt compounds are carcinogens; hard-metal dust containing cobalt causes lung disease. Cobalt metal: Carc. 1B, Repr. 1B, sensitiser (harmonised).

## Industry and supply

Batteries, superalloys, magnets, catalysts and blue glass. On the UK critical minerals list.

## Engineering

Cobalt superalloys hold their strength at high temperatures in turbines.

## Politics and trade

Most cobalt comes from the Democratic Republic of Congo, where artisanal mining raises child-labour concerns.

## Commerce

Battery makers are moving to cobalt-free chemistries to cut cost and risk.

## Quirks

Named after 'kobolds', goblins miners blamed for ores that gave off toxic fumes.

## Remarkable compounds

| Compound | Why it is remarkable |
|---|---|
| Vitamin B12 | the only vitamin built around a metal atom |
| Cobalt blue | the pigment of Chinese porcelain and Dutch Delftware |
| Cobalt chloride paper | turns from blue to pink when wet |
| Cobalt-60 | a radioactive isotope used to sterilise medical equipment |

## Discovery

Isolated by Georg Brandt in about 1735; from German 'Kobold', a goblin of the mines. Stable.
