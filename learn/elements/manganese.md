## Climate and carbon

Manganese makes steel tougher and is used in battery cathodes. Its oxides on the ocean floor hold metals of interest to deep-sea miners.

## Land, soil and water

Manganese dissolves in waterlogged, acid and peaty ground and stains water supplies black. In heavily limed soils it can run short, especially for cereals.

## Health and safety

An essential trace nutrient; long exposure to welding or mining fume affects the nervous system. The UK workplace limit is 0.2 mg/m³ inhalable and 0.05 mg/m³ respirable. Manganese and inorganic compounds WEL 0.2 mg/m³ inhalable, 0.05 mg/m³ respirable (EH40).

## Industry and supply

Steel, aluminium drink cans, batteries and fertiliser trace elements.

## Engineering

Manganese steel gets harder as it is hammered, so it is used in rock crushers and rail crossings.

## Politics and trade

Deep-sea mining for manganese nodules is a live international dispute.

## Commerce

South Africa supplies much of the world's manganese ore.

## Quirks

Cave paintings at Lascaux used manganese oxide for black.

## Remarkable compounds

| Compound | Why it is remarkable |
|---|---|
| Manganese dioxide | the black paste in alkaline batteries |
| Potassium permanganate | a purple disinfectant |
| Manganese nodules | potato-sized lumps covering parts of the deep sea floor |
| Manganese violet | an artists' pigment |

## Discovery

Isolated by Johan Gottlieb Gahn in 1774; name from magnesia nigra. Stable.
