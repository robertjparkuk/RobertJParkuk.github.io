## Climate and carbon

Lines the walls of fusion reactors; tungsten tools cut materials for the energy transition.

## Land, soil and water

Tungsten from ammunition can move in some soils.

## Health and safety

Low toxicity; hard-metal dust with cobalt causes lung disease. Powder is flammable.

## Industry and supply

Tungsten carbide cutting tools, filaments, armour.

## Engineering

Highest melting point of any metal, 3,422 °C.

## Politics and trade

China dominates supply; a UK mine at Hemerdon in Devon has operated on and off.

## Commerce

On many critical lists, including the UK's 2022 list.

## Quirks

Its symbol W comes from 'wolfram', from the German for 'wolf's froth'. Tungsten is as dense as gold, so it is used to fake gold bars.

## Remarkable compounds

| Compound | Why it is remarkable |
|---|---|
| Tungsten carbide | drill bits and ballpoint balls |
| Scheelite | glows blue under UV |

## Discovery

Isolated by the Elhuyar brothers in 1783; symbol W from 'wolfram'. Stable.
