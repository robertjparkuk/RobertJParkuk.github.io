## Climate and carbon

Lithium-ion batteries are the core of electric vehicles and grid storage. Lithium from hard rock is refined with a lot of energy; from brine it uses scarce water in deserts.

## Land, soil and water

Brine extraction lowers water tables in South American salt flats. Lithium batteries thrown in household bins start fires in bin lorries and recycling plants.

## Health and safety

Lithium salts treat bipolar disorder, with a narrow safe range. Lithium metal reacts with water and burns fiercely. The metal reacts with water to release hydrogen (H260) and burns fiercely. Lithium batteries are dangerous goods (UN3480, UN3481, UN3090, UN3091).

## Industry and supply

Batteries, ceramics, glass, greases and aluminium alloys. On the UK critical minerals list.

## Remarkable compounds

| Compound | Why it is remarkable |
|---|---|
| Lithium carbonate | battery feedstock and a mood-stabilising medicine |
| Lithium iron phosphate | a cobalt-free battery chemistry |
| Lithium hydroxide | removes CO₂ from the air in spacecraft and submarines |
| Lithium stearate | the thickener in most car grease |

## Discovery

Discovered in 1817 by Johan Arfwedson; named from Greek 'lithos', stone. Stable.
