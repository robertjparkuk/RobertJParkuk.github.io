## Climate and carbon

Lead-acid batteries still start most vehicles and back up power supplies; they are the most recycled consumer product.

## Land, soil and water

Lead from petrol, paint, pipes and shot remains in urban soils, orchards and shooting grounds. It binds strongly to soil but is taken up by root crops and children's hands.

## Health and safety

There is no safe level of lead for children; it lowers IQ. Lead pipes survive in some older homes. The Control of Lead at Work Regulations apply. Lead: Repr. 1A; lead compounds Repr. 1A, STOT RE 2. Control of Lead at Work Regulations apply.

## Industry and supply

Batteries, radiation shielding, roofing and cable sheathing.

## Engineering

Lead blocks X-rays and gamma rays, so it lines X-ray rooms.

## Politics and trade

Leaded petrol was finally phased out worldwide in 2021.

## Commerce

Lead-acid batteries are recycled at very high rates.

## In Northern Ireland

Older homes in Northern Ireland can still have lead supply pipes; NI Water gives advice on replacing them.

## Quirks

The Romans sweetened wine with lead acetate, 'sugar of lead'.

## Remarkable compounds

| Compound | Why it is remarkable |
|---|---|
| Tetraethyl lead | the petrol additive phased out worldwide by 2021 |
| Lead white | the paint pigment of Old Masters, and a poison |
| Lead crystal | glass with lead oxide for sparkle |
| Galena | lead sulfide, the ore, and the first crystal radio detector |

## Discovery

Used since about 7000 BC; symbol Pb from Latin 'plumbum' (hence plumber). Stable.
