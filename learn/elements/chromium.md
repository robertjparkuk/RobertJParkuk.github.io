## Climate and carbon

Chromium makes the stainless steel used in energy plant, food processing and chemical works.

## Land, soil and water

Hexavalent chromium from plating works, tanneries and chromite residues is mobile in water and a priority contaminant.

## Health and safety

Chromium(III) is a trace nutrient; chromium(VI) causes lung cancer and skin ulcers, and has strict workplace controls. Chromium(VI) compounds: Carc. 1B, skin sensitiser; strict WELs.

## Industry and supply

Stainless steel, chrome plating, leather tanning and pigments.

## Engineering

Stainless steel needs at least about 10.5% chromium to resist rust.

## Politics and trade

Hexavalent chromium is tightly restricted under REACH.

## Commerce

South Africa and Kazakhstan supply most chromite.

## Quirks

Chromium gives rubies their red and emeralds their green.

## Remarkable compounds

| Compound | Why it is remarkable |
|---|---|
| Chromium(VI) | the form at the heart of the Hinkley groundwater case behind the film Erin Brockovich |
| Chrome yellow | lead chromate pigment of school buses |
| Ruby | alumina coloured red by a trace of chromium |
| Chromium(III) oxide | the green of banknotes and paint |

## Discovery

Discovered by Louis-Nicolas Vauquelin in 1797; from Greek 'chroma', colour. Stable.
