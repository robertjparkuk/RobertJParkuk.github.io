## Climate and carbon

No effect.

## Land, soil and water

A trace gas in the air.

## Health and safety

Non-toxic; asphyxiant only in confined spaces. Gas under pressure. UN1065 (compressed), UN1913 (refrigerated liquid).

## Industry and supply

Signs, lasers and cryogenics; a by-product of separating air for oxygen and nitrogen.

## Engineering

Excimer lasers that print microchips use a neon gas mix.

## Politics and trade

Much of the world's chip-grade neon came from Ukraine, and the 2022 invasion disrupted supply.

## Commerce

Supply depends on large steel plants with air-separation units.

## Quirks

'Neon' signs in colours other than red use other gases or coloured glass.

## Remarkable compounds

| Compound | Why it is remarkable |
|---|---|
| None stable | neon forms no known neutral compounds |

## Discovery

Discovered by Ramsay and Travers in 1898; from Greek 'neos', new. Stable.
