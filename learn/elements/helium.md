## Climate and carbon

No climate effect, but once released helium escapes to space for good, so it is a finite resource.

## Land, soil and water

Found only where natural gas fields trap helium from radioactive decay deep underground.

## Health and safety

Non-toxic; breathing it from balloons can asphyxiate and has caused deaths. Gas under pressure; liquid helium causes cold burns and expands violently. UN1046 (compressed), UN1963 (refrigerated liquid).

## Industry and supply

Cools MRI magnets and research magnets, purges rocket fuel lines, and finds leaks.

## Engineering

Liquid helium at minus 269 °C is the only practical coolant for superconducting magnets in MRI scanners.

## Politics and trade

Helium shortages have come in waves as a few producing countries changed policy; some governments treat it as strategic.

## Commerce

Supply depends on a handful of gas fields, so hospitals and labs have faced rationing in shortages.

## Quirks

Found in the Sun's spectrum in 1868, 27 years before it was found on Earth. Liquid helium creeps up and over the walls of its container.

## Remarkable compounds

| Compound | Why it is remarkable |
|---|---|
| Helium hydride ion | the first molecule to form after the Big Bang |
| Heliox | a helium–oxygen mix for deep divers |

## Discovery

Seen in the Sun's spectrum in 1868 (Janssen, Lockyer) before it was found on Earth in 1895 (Ramsay). Named from Greek 'helios', the Sun. Stable.
