## Climate and carbon

Cadmium telluride thin-film solar panels are among the lowest-carbon panels to make; they must be recycled at end of life.

## Land, soil and water

Cadmium comes onto farmland as an impurity in phosphate fertiliser and in sewage sludge, and builds up slowly. EU rules limit cadmium in phosphate fertilisers.

## Health and safety

A carcinogen that damages kidneys and bones. Smoking is a major personal source. Cadmium: Carc. 1B, Muta. 2, Repr. 2, Acute Tox. 2 (inhalation).

## Industry and supply

Now restricted in batteries and pigments; still in some solar panels and alloys.

## Engineering

Nickel-cadmium batteries once powered most cordless tools.

## Politics and trade

EU rules restrict cadmium in batteries, electronics and fertilisers.

## Commerce

Mostly a by-product of zinc refining.

## Quirks

Named from 'cadmia', an old word for zinc ore.

## Remarkable compounds

| Compound | Why it is remarkable |
|---|---|
| Cadmium yellow | the bright pigment Monet used |
| Cadmium telluride | thin-film solar panels |
| Cadmium sulfide | light sensors in old street lights |
| Itai-itai disease | the Japanese name for cadmium poisoning from polluted rice fields |

## Discovery

Discovered by Friedrich Stromeyer in 1817; from Latin 'cadmia', zinc ore. Stable.
