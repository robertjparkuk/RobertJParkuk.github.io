## Climate and carbon

Light, durable titanium lasts in desalination and offshore plant; making titanium dioxide pigment uses a lot of energy.

## Land, soil and water

Ilmenite and rutile come from beach sands and igneous rocks.

## Health and safety

Metal is biocompatible and used in implants; titanium dioxide was banned as a food additive in the EU in 2022. Powder is flammable (UN2546, UN1352).

## Industry and supply

Aircraft, implants, chemical plant; TiO₂ whitens paint, paper and plastics.

## Engineering

Titanium bonds directly to bone, which is why it is used for hip joints and dental implants.

## Politics and trade

Russia was a major supplier of aerospace titanium before 2022.

## Commerce

TiO₂ pigment is a large commodity market; titanium metal is a smaller, specialist one.

## Quirks

Titanium burns in pure nitrogen. Self-cleaning glass uses a titanium dioxide film that breaks down dirt in sunlight.

## Remarkable compounds

| Compound | Why it is remarkable |
|---|---|
| Titanium dioxide | the white of paint, toothpaste and sunscreen |
| Titanium tetrachloride | used for smoke screens and to make the metal |

## Discovery

Found by William Gregor in Cornwall in 1791; named by Martin Klaproth after the Titans. Stable.
