## Climate and carbon

Fluorinated gases (HFCs, SF₆, NF₃) are powerful, long-lived greenhouse gases. HFC-134a warms about 1,300 times as much as CO₂ over a century; SF₆ about 23,500 times. Northern Ireland follows the EU F-gas Regulation (EU) 2024/573; Great Britain its own regime.

## Land, soil and water

Phosphate fertiliser carries fluoride onto land. Firefighting foams have left PFAS at airports and training grounds; PFAS barely break down.

## Health and safety

Fluoride protects teeth in small amounts and damages teeth and bone in excess. Hydrofluoric acid burns can be fatal without specific first aid. Some PFAS are linked to health effects and restrictions are tightening. See the fluorine briefing safety tab.

## Industry and supply

Fluorspar becomes hydrogen fluoride, the start of refrigerants, PTFE, battery salts and the uranium hexafluoride used in enrichment.

## In Northern Ireland

NI Water has never fluoridated the public supply since it was set up in 2007; Ireland has fluoridated since 1964.

## Remarkable compounds

| Compound | Why it is remarkable |
|---|---|
| PTFE | the non-stick coating discovered by accident in 1938 |
| Uranium hexafluoride | the gas used to enrich uranium |
| Sulfur hexafluoride | a gas so dense it makes your voice deep |
| Fluoroantimonic acid | among the strongest acids known, made from fluorine and antimony |

## Discovery

Isolated by Henri Moissan in 1886, after attempts that injured or killed several chemists; name from fluorspar. Stable.
