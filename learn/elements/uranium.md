## Climate and carbon

Nuclear power from uranium is low-carbon electricity. Northern Ireland has no nuclear plant but imports electricity across the single electricity market.

## Land, soil and water

Uranium in granite and shale is the source of radon. Uranium mining leaves large volumes of tailings.

## Health and safety

Weakly radioactive and chemically toxic to kidneys. Depleted uranium is used in armour and counterweights. Radioactive; Acute Tox. 2 (inhalation, ingestion); Class 7 in transport.

## Industry and supply

Nuclear fuel after enrichment; ceramics glazes in the past.

## Engineering

A uranium fuel pellet the size of a fingertip holds as much energy as about a tonne of coal.

## Politics and trade

Uranium enrichment is at the heart of nuclear non-proliferation diplomacy.

## Commerce

Kazakhstan, Canada and Australia are leading producers.

## Quirks

Uranium glass glows green under UV light, and old Fiestaware plates are radioactive.

## Remarkable compounds

| Compound | Why it is remarkable |
|---|---|
| Uranium glass | glows green under ultraviolet light |
| Yellowcake | the uranium oxide concentrate sold by mines |
| Uranium hexafluoride | the gas spun in centrifuges to enrich uranium |
| Oklo | a natural nuclear reactor that ran in Gabon two billion years ago |

## Discovery

Identified by Martin Klaproth in 1789; named after Uranus. Radioactivity found by Becquerel in 1896. Uranium-238 half-life 4.5 billion years.
