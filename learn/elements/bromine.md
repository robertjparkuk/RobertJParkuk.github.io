## Climate and carbon

Bromine from halons and methyl bromide destroys ozone more efficiently than chlorine; both are banned under the Montreal Protocol.

## Land, soil and water

Some brominated flame retardants are persistent pollutants found in sediments and wildlife. Methyl bromide soil fumigation is banned in the UK and EU.

## Health and safety

Liquid bromine and its vapour burn skin and lungs. Bromide was once a sedative. Bromine: Acute Tox. 2 H330, Skin Corr. 1A. UN1744.

## Industry and supply

Flame retardants, drilling fluids, water treatment and medicines.

## Remarkable compounds

| Compound | Why it is remarkable |
|---|---|
| Cyanogen bromide | a highly toxic laboratory reagent that cuts proteins at methionine, a tool behind early protein sequencing |
| Tyrian purple | the royal dye from sea snails, a bromine compound |
| Halon 1301 | the fire suppressant once used in aircraft and computer rooms |
| Bromoform | made by seaweed; a red seaweed is fed to cattle to cut methane |

## Discovery

Discovered independently by Antoine Balard (1826) and Carl Löwig (1825); from Greek 'bromos', stench. Stable.
