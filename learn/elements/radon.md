## Climate and carbon

Not a greenhouse gas. Making homes airtight to save energy can trap radon unless ventilation is designed in. Scientists use radon as a tracer of air that has passed over land.

## Land, soil and water

Radon comes from uranium in rocks and soil; levels follow geology. Northern Ireland's radon potential map shows the affected areas. It also dissolves in well water.

## Health and safety

Long-term exposure causes lung cancer, especially in smokers. UKHSA's action level for homes is 200 Bq/m³; above 300 Bq/m³ at work the law requires action. See the radon briefing safety tab.

## Industry and supply

No industrial use of note; radon test detectors are simple and cheap.

## Engineering

A radon sump with a small fan can cut indoor levels dramatically.

## Politics and trade

Radon policy depends on building regulations and voluntary home testing.

## Commerce

Radon test kits are cheap; remediation costs are modest for most homes.

## In Northern Ireland

New buildings in radon affected areas must include protection under the Building Regulations (Northern Ireland).

## Quirks

Radon spas in Europe still advertise radon baths as therapy.

## Remarkable compounds

| Compound | Why it is remarkable |
|---|---|
| Polonium-210 | a radon decay product that delivers much of the lung dose |
| Lead-210 | a decay product used to date lake sediments |
| Thoron | a short-lived radon isotope from thorium |
| Radon in spa water | once sold as a health tonic |

## Discovery

Discovered by Friedrich Ernst Dorn in 1900. No stable isotope; radon-222 half-life 3.8 days.
