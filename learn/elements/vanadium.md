## Climate and carbon

Vanadium redox flow batteries store renewable power for hours and last decades.

## Land, soil and water

Released by burning heavy fuel oil; present in some shales and iron ores.

## Health and safety

Vanadium pentoxide is a suspected carcinogen. Vanadium pentoxide: Muta. 2, Carc. 1B, Repr. 2 (harmonised). UN2862.

## Industry and supply

Strengthening steel, and catalysts for sulfuric acid.

## Engineering

A small addition of vanadium makes steel rebar stronger, so less steel is needed.

## Politics and trade

Supply concentrated in China, Russia and South Africa.

## Commerce

Most vanadium goes into steel, so its market follows construction.

## Quirks

Named after a Norse goddess for its many colourful compounds. Sea squirts concentrate vanadium in their blood.

## Remarkable compounds

| Compound | Why it is remarkable |
|---|---|
| Vanadium pentoxide | the catalyst that makes sulfuric acid |
| Vanadinite | a bright red-orange crystal |

## Discovery

Found in 1801 by Andrés Manuel del Río, rediscovered in 1830 by Nils Sefström; named after Vanadís (Freyja). Stable.
