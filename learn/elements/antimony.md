## Climate and carbon

Used in solar-panel glass, lead-acid batteries and flame retardants for cables and insulation.

## Land, soil and water

Turns up at shooting ranges, in roadside brake dust and around smelters.

## Health and safety

Dust irritates lungs and skin; antimony trioxide is a suspected carcinogen. The UK workplace limit is 0.5 mg/m³. See the antimony briefing safety tab.

## Industry and supply

Flame retardants, battery grids, PET catalyst and glass.

## Engineering

Antimony hardens lead so battery grids and bullets keep their shape.

## Politics and trade

On the UK critical minerals list. China has controlled exports since September 2024; its US ban was suspended to 27 November 2026.

## Commerce

Supply concentrated in China, Russia and Tajikistan.

## Quirks

Ancient Egyptians used antimony sulfide as kohl eyeliner. 'Everlasting pills' of antimony were swallowed as a laxative, recovered and reused.

## Discovery

Known in antiquity (kohl eye make-up); symbol Sb from Latin 'stibium'. Stable.
