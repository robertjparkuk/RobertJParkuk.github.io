## Climate and carbon

Catalytic converters on petrol cars; palladium absorbs hydrogen and is studied for hydrogen purification.

## Land, soil and water

Converter metals build up in roadside dust and soil.

## Health and safety

Can cause allergy. Powder is flammable.

## Industry and supply

Petrol catalysts, electronics, dental alloys.

## Engineering

Palladium soaks up hundreds of times its own volume of hydrogen.

## Politics and trade

Russia is a leading producer, so sanctions debates affect supply.

## Commerce

Demand is tied to petrol cars and will fall as they are replaced.

## Quirks

Named after the asteroid Pallas, discovered the year before.

## Remarkable compounds

| Compound | Why it is remarkable |
|---|---|
| Palladium hydride | a hydrogen sponge |
| Palladium catalysts | behind the 2010 Nobel Prize in chemistry for coupling reactions |

## Discovery

Discovered by Wollaston in 1802; named after the asteroid Pallas. Stable.
