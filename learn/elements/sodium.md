## Climate and carbon

Soda ash for glass releases CO₂. Sodium-ion batteries avoid lithium and cobalt and suit grid storage.

## Land, soil and water

Road salt raises sodium and chloride in streams and kills verge plants and hedges. Sodium-heavy soils lose their crumb structure and drain badly.

## Health and safety

Essential for nerves, but too much salt raises blood pressure. Sodium metal reacts violently with water. The metal reacts violently with water (H260). UN1428 (sodium).

## Industry and supply

Salt is the base of chlorine and caustic soda, glass, soap, food preservation and road gritting.

## Engineering

Molten sodium has cooled some nuclear reactors because it carries heat so well.

## Politics and trade

Salt taxes helped spark revolutions, including Gandhi's Salt March in 1930.

## Commerce

Road salt demand swings with winter weather.

## In Northern Ireland

Rock salt is mined at Kilroot near Carrickfergus and used for gritting roads across the UK and Ireland.

## Quirks

Sodium metal is soft enough to cut with a knife and must be kept under oil.

## Remarkable compounds

| Compound | Why it is remarkable |
|---|---|
| Sodium chloride | table salt and road salt |
| Sodium azide | the chemical that inflated car airbags, now largely replaced because it is toxic |
| Sodium bicarbonate | baking soda |
| Sodium hydroxide | caustic soda, used to make soap since antiquity |

## Discovery

Isolated by Humphry Davy in 1807 by electrolysis of caustic soda; symbol Na from Latin 'natrium'. Stable.
