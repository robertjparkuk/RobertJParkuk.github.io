## Climate and carbon

Electronics contacts; large mines move vast volumes of rock with heavy fuel use.

## Land, soil and water

Small-scale miners use mercury; big mines use cyanide and leave large waste dumps.

## Health and safety

Low toxicity; gold compounds once treated arthritis. Not classified as metal.

## Industry and supply

Electronics, dentistry, jewellery and central bank reserves.

## Engineering

Gold does not corrode, so it plates connectors and coats spacecraft visors.

## Politics and trade

Gold mining proposals in the Sperrins, Co. Tyrone, have been a long-running planning dispute.

## Commerce

Central banks and investors hold most of it; recycling supplies a large share.

## In Northern Ireland

Gold has been found in the Sperrins; mining proposals there have drawn strong local opposition and support.

## Quirks

All the gold ever mined would fit in a cube about 22 metres a side. Gold leaf can be beaten thin enough to let green light through.

## Remarkable compounds

| Compound | Why it is remarkable |
|---|---|
| Gold chloride | used in photography and medicine |
| Purple of Cassius | gold nanoparticles that made ruby glass |

## Discovery

Known since prehistory; symbol Au from Latin 'aurum'. Stable.
