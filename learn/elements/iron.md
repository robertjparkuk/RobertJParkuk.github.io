## Climate and carbon

Blast-furnace steel is one of the largest industrial CO₂ sources; scrap and hydrogen-based routes cut it. Iron and steel are CBAM goods. Iron in the ocean limits plankton growth in some seas.

## Land, soil and water

Iron oxides colour soils red and yellow and bind phosphate. Iron-rich drainage from old mines coats stream beds orange with ochre.

## Health and safety

Essential for blood. Iron tablets are a common cause of poisoning in young children. Iron powder can be flammable; metal is not classified.

## Industry and supply

Steel for buildings, vehicles, machinery and rebar.

## Engineering

Steel is iron with a little carbon; stainless steel adds chromium.

## Politics and trade

Steel is covered by CBAM and is at the centre of trade disputes and tariffs.

## Commerce

Scrap steel is recycled more than any other material.

## In Northern Ireland

Iron ore and bauxite were once mined from the laterite between the Antrim lava flows.

## Quirks

The Earth's core is mostly iron, which creates the magnetic field that protects us from solar wind.

## Remarkable compounds

| Compound | Why it is remarkable |
|---|---|
| Haemoglobin | the iron protein that carries oxygen in blood |
| Magnetite | naturally magnetic; birds may use it to navigate |
| Prussian blue | the first synthetic pigment and an antidote to radioactive caesium |
| Pyrite | fool's gold |

## Discovery

Used since about 1200 BC (the Iron Age); symbol Fe from Latin 'ferrum'. Stable.
