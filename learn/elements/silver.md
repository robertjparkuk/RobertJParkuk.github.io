## Climate and carbon

Silver paste makes the electrical contacts on most solar cells; solar is now a major user of silver.

## Land, soil and water

Silver ions and nanosilver are toxic to aquatic life and soil microbes. Silver from photography and electronics reaches sewage sludge.

## Health and safety

Low toxicity; long-term swallowing of silver can turn skin permanently blue-grey (argyria). Silver dressings kill bacteria in wounds. Silver nitrate: oxidiser, corrosive (UN1493).

## Industry and supply

Solar cells, electronics, mirrors, brazing and jewellery.

## Remarkable compounds

| Compound | Why it is remarkable |
|---|---|
| Silver halides | the light-sensitive crystals of film photography |
| Silver azide | a primary explosive so sensitive a touch or flash sets it off; once used in detonators |
| Silver nitrate | lunar caustic, used to cauterise warts |
| Silver iodide | seeded into clouds to try to make rain |

## Discovery

Used since prehistory; symbol Ag from Latin 'argentum'. Stable.
