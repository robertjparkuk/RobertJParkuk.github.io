## Climate and carbon

Ozone (O₃) is a greenhouse gas and pollutant at ground level, formed from traffic and industrial emissions in sunlight. Oxygen-free conditions in wetlands and slurry are where methane is made.

## Land, soil and water

Waterlogged soils run short of oxygen, which turns nitrate into nitrous oxide and mobilises iron and manganese. Rivers starved of oxygen by algal blooms and slurry spills kill fish.

## Health and safety

Essential for life. Oxygen-enriched air makes clothing and hair burn fiercely, which is why oxygen cylinders need care at home and at work. Oxidiser: oxygen-enriched air makes fires burn fiercely. UN1072 (compressed), UN1073 (refrigerated liquid).

## Industry and supply

Steelmaking, hospitals, water treatment and welding use oxygen separated from air. Oxy-fuel combustion makes CO₂ capture easier.

## Engineering

Oxygen lances cut steel; oxygen-blown converters make most of the world's steel.

## Politics and trade

Medical oxygen shortages became a political issue during the pandemic.

## Commerce

Made in bulk by air-separation plants, often beside steelworks and hospitals.

## Quirks

Liquid oxygen is pale blue and sticks to a magnet.

## Remarkable compounds

| Compound | Why it is remarkable |
|---|---|
| Ozone (O₃) | shield in the stratosphere, pollutant at street level |
| Silica (SiO₂) | sand and quartz: most of the Earth's crust is oxygen bound to silicon |
| Iron oxides | rust, and the red of Antrim's interbasaltic laterite |
| Liquid oxygen | pale blue and strongly attracted to a magnet |

## Discovery

Discovered by Carl Scheele (about 1772) and Joseph Priestley (1774); named by Lavoisier. Stable.
