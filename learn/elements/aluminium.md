## Climate and carbon

Primary aluminium is one of the most electricity-hungry materials and its smelting releases perfluorocarbons. Recycled aluminium uses a small fraction of the energy. Aluminium is a CBAM good in the EU and from 2027 in the UK.

## Land, soil and water

Aluminium is the most common metal in soil. In acid soils it dissolves and poisons roots; liming locks it up. Acid rain once released it into upland lakes and killed fish.

## Health and safety

Low toxicity at normal exposure. Fine aluminium powder is a dust-explosion and fire hazard. Powder is flammable and reacts with water (UN1396 uncoated, UN1309 coated). Massive metal is not classified.

## Industry and supply

Cans, window frames, cables and vehicle bodies. Bauxite residue ('red mud') is one of the world's largest industrial waste streams.

## Engineering

Aluminium forms a thin oxide skin that protects it from corrosion.

## Politics and trade

Aluminium is covered by EU CBAM and, from 2027, by UK CBAM.

## Commerce

Recycled aluminium is worth far more than most scrap.

## Quirks

Aluminium was once more valuable than gold; Napoleon III served honoured guests on aluminium plates.

## Remarkable compounds

| Compound | Why it is remarkable |
|---|---|
| Alumina (Al₂O₃) | corundum; ruby and sapphire are coloured alumina |
| Aluminium sulfate | used to clear drinking water |
| Thermite | aluminium powder and iron oxide, burning hot enough to weld rail track |
| Zeolites | aluminium silicate cages used in detergents and to trap ammonia |

## Discovery

Isolated by Hans Christian Ørsted in 1825 and Friedrich Wöhler in 1827; 'aluminium' is the IUPAC spelling. Stable.
