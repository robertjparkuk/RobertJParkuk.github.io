## Climate and carbon

No direct role.

## Land, soil and water

Caesium-137 from Chernobyl lingered in upland soils and sheep across the UK; restrictions were lifted in 2012.

## Health and safety

Stable caesium is low toxicity; caesium-137 is a radiation hazard. Metal ignites in air, reacts explosively with water (UN1407).

## Industry and supply

Atomic clocks and oil-drilling fluids.

## Engineering

The second is defined by a caesium-133 atom vibrating 9,192,631,770 times.

## Politics and trade

Lost caesium-137 medical sources have caused radiation accidents, notably Goiânia in Brazil in 1987.

## Commerce

A small, specialised market.

## In Northern Ireland

Upland sheep in Northern Ireland were monitored for Chernobyl caesium in the years after 1986.

## Quirks

The most reactive metal you can hold in a sealed glass ampoule; it melts at 28 °C.

## Remarkable compounds

| Compound | Why it is remarkable |
|---|---|
| Caesium formate | dense drilling fluid |
| Prussian blue | binds radioactive caesium in the gut as an antidote |

## Discovery

Discovered by Bunsen and Kirchhoff in 1860 by spectroscopy; from Latin 'caesius', sky blue. Stable (caesium-133).
