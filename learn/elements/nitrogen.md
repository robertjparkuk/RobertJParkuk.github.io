## Climate and carbon

Nitrous oxide from fertilised soils and manure is a greenhouse gas about 265 times as strong as CO₂ over a century and lasts over a hundred years. Fertiliser making burns gas, which is why fertilisers fall under EU CBAM and UK CBAM from 2027.

## Land, soil and water

Reactive nitrogen leaks three ways: nitrate washes into water, ammonia drifts onto bogs and woodland, and nitrous oxide escapes to the sky. Clover fixes nitrogen from the air for free.

## Health and safety

Nitrate in drinking water is limited to protect babies. Nitrogen dioxide from traffic harms lungs. Nitrogen gas asphyxiates in tanks and cold rooms without warning. See the nitrogen briefing safety tab.

## Industry and supply

The Haber–Bosch process makes ammonia from air and gas and feeds about half the world. Nitrogen also makes explosives, nylon and the inert gas in crisp packets.

## Engineering

Liquid nitrogen freezes food fast and shrink-fits engine parts.

## Politics and trade

Ammonia from farms is one of Northern Ireland's most contested environmental issues.

## Commerce

Fertiliser prices follow natural gas prices closely.

## In Northern Ireland

97% of Northern Ireland's ammonia comes from agriculture, with cattle around 62% of that, according to DAERA.

## Quirks

Fritz Haber won a Nobel Prize for fixing nitrogen and also led Germany's poison gas programme.

## Remarkable compounds

| Compound | Why it is remarkable |
|---|---|
| Ammonia (NH₃) | the root of nearly all fertiliser |
| Ammonium nitrate | fertiliser and, carelessly stored, a cause of disastrous explosions |
| Nitroglycerine | both an explosive and a heart medicine for angina |
| Nitrous oxide | laughing gas: an anaesthetic, a greenhouse gas and an ozone-depleting substance |

## Discovery

Isolated in 1772 by Daniel Rutherford; named from Greek for 'nitre-former'. Stable.
