## Climate and carbon

Minor; light, stiff beryllium parts appear in aerospace and fusion research.

## Land, soil and water

Rare in soils; dust from machining is the concern.

## Health and safety

Chronic beryllium disease is an incurable lung condition caused by tiny amounts of dust; beryllium is a carcinogen. Carc. 1B; acutely toxic by inhalation. UN1567 (beryllium powder).

## Industry and supply

Copper-beryllium springs and non-sparking tools for gas and oil plant.

## Engineering

Beryllium mirrors on the James Webb Space Telescope keep their shape at deep-space cold.

## Politics and trade

Treated as a strategic defence material by the US and EU.

## Commerce

A very small market dominated by a few processors.

## Quirks

Beryllium salts taste sweet, which is how chemists once tested them, a dangerous habit. Emerald and aquamarine are beryllium minerals.

## Remarkable compounds

| Compound | Why it is remarkable |
|---|---|
| Beryl | emerald and aquamarine |
| Beryllium oxide | conducts heat like a metal but insulates electricity |

## Discovery

Discovered in beryl by Louis-Nicolas Vauquelin in 1798; isolated in 1828. Stable.
