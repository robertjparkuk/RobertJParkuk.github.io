## Climate and carbon

Lighter, stronger steel for cars and pipelines; superconducting magnets.

## Land, soil and water

Mostly mined from one deposit in Brazil.

## Health and safety

Low toxicity. Powder is flammable.

## Industry and supply

High-strength steel, superconducting wire, jet engines.

## Engineering

Niobium-titanium wire is the superconductor in MRI scanners and the Large Hadron Collider.

## Politics and trade

One country, Brazil, supplies most of it; top of the UK's 2024 criticality assessment.

## Commerce

Almost all is sold as ferroniobium to steelmakers.

## Quirks

Named after Niobe, daughter of Tantalus, because it is so like tantalum.

## Remarkable compounds

| Compound | Why it is remarkable |
|---|---|
| Ferroniobium | the alloy added to steel |

## Discovery

Discovered by Charles Hatchett in 1801 as 'columbium'; named niobium after Niobe. Stable.
