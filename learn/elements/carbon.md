## Climate and carbon

Carbon is the element climate policy counts. Carbon dioxide and methane are the two largest causes of human-made warming. In Northern Ireland, CO₂ was 61.5% of greenhouse gas emissions in 2023 and methane 30.2%, much of it from cattle and sheep.

## Land, soil and water

Soils hold more carbon than the atmosphere and all plants combined. Peat is Northern Ireland's biggest land carbon store; wet peat locks carbon away and drained peat gives it off for decades. Grassland soils gain or lose carbon slowly and the change is hard to measure.

## Health and safety

Carbon monoxide is the silent killer from boilers and engines. Fine soot (black carbon) damages hearts and lungs. Carbon dioxide collects in pits and slurry stores and can suffocate. See the carbon briefing safety tab: CO₂, CO, methane, carbon dusts and self-heating charcoal.

## Industry and supply

Carbon is the backbone of fuels, plastics and medicines, the reducing agent in blast-furnace steel, and the source of CO₂ in cement. Graphite makes battery anodes; carbon fibre makes wind-turbine blades.

## Engineering

Diamond is the hardest natural material and the best natural conductor of heat.

## Politics and trade

Carbon pricing (UK ETS, EU ETS) and border charges (CBAM) now put a price on carbon in trade.

## Commerce

Carbon credits are traded, but quality varies widely; offsets are not the same as cutting emissions.

## In Northern Ireland

Agriculture was about 31% of Northern Ireland's emissions in 2024. Carbon budgets require average cuts of 33% (2023–27), 48% and 62% against the baseline.

## Quirks

Carbon-14 lets archaeologists date anything that once lived. Graphite and diamond are the same element.

## Remarkable compounds

| Compound | Why it is remarkable |
|---|---|
| Calcium carbonate | limestone, chalk and seashells; the largest carbon store on Earth |
| Methane (CH₄) | natural gas, and the gas cattle burp |
| Graphene | a single sheet of carbon atoms, 200 times stronger than steel by weight |
| Buckminsterfullerene (C₆₀) | a football-shaped molecule found in candle soot and in space |

## Discovery

Known since prehistory as charcoal and soot; recognised as an element by Lavoisier in the 1780s; name from Latin 'carbo', charcoal. Stable (carbon-14 is radioactive, half-life 5,730 years).
