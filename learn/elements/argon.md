## Climate and carbon

No effect.

## Land, soil and water

Nearly 1% of the air, made by decay of potassium in rocks over billions of years.

## Health and safety

Non-toxic but heavier than air; it collects in welding pits and tanks and suffocates. Heavier than air; collects in low spaces. UN1006 (compressed), UN1951 (refrigerated liquid).

## Industry and supply

Shield gas for welding, filling for double glazing, and in steelmaking.

## Engineering

Argon-filled glazing cuts heat loss compared with air-filled units.

## Politics and trade

None of note.

## Commerce

Made in bulk as a by-product of oxygen production.

## Quirks

Its name means 'lazy' in Greek. Potassium–argon dating measures the age of volcanic rocks.

## Remarkable compounds

| Compound | Why it is remarkable |
|---|---|
| Argon fluorohydride | made only in frozen argon below about −246 °C |

## Discovery

Discovered by Lord Rayleigh and William Ramsay in 1894; from Greek 'argos', idle. Stable.
