## Climate and carbon

Used in glass-fibre insulation and in the neodymium magnets of wind turbines and EV motors.

## Land, soil and water

A plant micronutrient: brassicas, beet and swedes suffer brown heart and hollow stems without it, but the line to toxicity is narrow.

## Health and safety

Boric acid and borax are classed as toxic to reproduction, so 'slime' made with borax is now discouraged for children. Boric acid and borates: Repr. 1B. Boron powder is flammable.

## Industry and supply

Borosilicate glass, detergents, ceramics and nuclear control rods.

## Engineering

Boron carbide is one of the hardest materials, used in body armour; boron absorbs neutrons and shuts down reactors.

## Politics and trade

EU rules restrict boric acid in consumer products because of its reproductive toxicity.

## Commerce

Turkey holds most of the world's borate reserves.

## Quirks

Boron gives a green flame. Pyrex owes its heat resistance to boron oxide.

## Remarkable compounds

| Compound | Why it is remarkable |
|---|---|
| Borax | a cleaning mineral from dry lake beds |
| Boron nitride | slippery like graphite or nearly as hard as diamond, depending on form |

## Discovery

Isolated in 1808 by Gay-Lussac and Thénard, and by Humphry Davy; name from borax. Stable.
