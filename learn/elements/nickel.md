## Climate and carbon

Nickel-rich battery cathodes give electric vehicles more range. New nickel mining in South-East Asia has cleared forest and burned coal.

## Land, soil and water

Ultramafic soils are naturally high in nickel; a few plants, called hyperaccumulators, can concentrate it enough to be 'farmed' for the metal.

## Health and safety

The commonest cause of contact allergy, from jewellery and buttons. Some nickel compounds cause cancer. Nickel metal: Carc. 2, skin sensitiser (harmonised).

## Industry and supply

Stainless steel, batteries, plating and coins.

## Engineering

Nickel superalloys keep jet-engine turbine blades strong when glowing hot.

## Politics and trade

Indonesia banned nickel ore exports to build its own processing industry.

## Commerce

Battery demand has made nickel a strategic metal.

## Quirks

Named from 'Kupfernickel', devil's copper, because the ore looked like copper but yielded none.

## Remarkable compounds

| Compound | Why it is remarkable |
|---|---|
| Nickel carbonyl | a gas so toxic it is used only in closed refining |
| Nitinol | nickel-titanium 'memory metal' that springs back to shape when warmed |
| Raney nickel | a catalyst for making margarine and medicines |
| Nickel hydroxide | in rechargeable NiMH batteries |

## Discovery

Isolated by Axel Cronstedt in 1751; from German 'Kupfernickel', devil's copper. Stable.
