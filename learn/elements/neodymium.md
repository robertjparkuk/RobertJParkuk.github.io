## Climate and carbon

Neodymium-iron-boron magnets drive most wind turbines and EV motors.

## Land, soil and water

Mining and separation leave acidic and radioactive wastes, mostly in China and Myanmar.

## Health and safety

Low toxicity; strong magnets can injure children if more than one is swallowed. Powder is flammable.

## Industry and supply

Permanent magnets, lasers and glass.

## Engineering

NdFeB magnets are the strongest permanent magnets made.

## Politics and trade

China dominates mining, separation and magnet making, and has used export controls on rare earths.

## Commerce

Western governments are funding new mines and recycling to reduce dependence.

## Quirks

Neodymium glass is used in welding goggles and makes things look different under different light.

## Remarkable compounds

| Compound | Why it is remarkable |
|---|---|
| Nd₂Fe₁₄B | the magnet alloy |
| Neodymium-doped YAG | a common laser crystal |

## Discovery

Separated by Carl Auer von Welsbach in 1885; 'new twin'. Stable.
