*Snapshot taken 22 October 2026. Covers the 11 district councils, the nine Executive departments, and UK Government consultations that extend to or directly affect Northern Ireland, open now or closed since 22 April 2025.*

## What I found

- **385 consultations** in 18 months: 131 from councils, 204 from Executive departments and 50 from UK Government.
- **63 open** on 22 October 2026, 13 of them closing within a fortnight.
- **Only about one in five closed consultations has a published outcome** that I could find: 22% for councils (24 of 111), 18% for Executive departments (30 of 165) and 32% for UK Government (14 of 44).
- Many outcomes that do exist are buried in committee papers rather than published beside the consultation, so someone who responded has little chance of finding out what happened.

A consultation that never reports back is not really a consultation. People give their time and expertise; they are owed an account of what was heard and what changed.

## How councils compare

I scored each council's consultations page on three things a member of the public needs: does it list past consultations as well as open ones, does it show opening and closing dates, and does it work? Two points for yes, one for partly, none for no.

## What the law and councils' own schemes expect

Several duties bear on how public bodies consult and report back. The scan does not show that any particular duty has been broken, but it does show how hard it is for the public to check.

- **Equality schemes.** Under section 75 of, and Schedule 9 to, the Northern Ireland Act 1998, every council's equality scheme must set out its arrangements for consulting, and for publishing the results of equality impact assessments (Schedule 9, paragraph 4(2)). Councils' approved schemes commit them to feedback: Mid and East Antrim's, for example, says consultees will get feedback "in a timely manner" and that a feedback report is prepared (paragraph 3.2.11).
- **Performance improvement.** The Local Government Act (Northern Ireland) 2014 requires councils to consult before setting improvement objectives (section 87) and to publish an improvement plan and an annual assessment of performance (section 92).
- **Planning.** Under section 4 of the Planning Act (Northern Ireland) 2011, each council's statement of community involvement sets out how it will involve the public in planning, including its local development plan.
- **Publication schemes.** Section 19 of the Freedom of Information Act 2000 requires every public authority to adopt, maintain and publish information under a publication scheme, including information about its decisions.
- **Accessible websites.** The Public Sector Bodies (Websites and Mobile Applications) (No. 2) Accessibility Regulations 2018 require an accessibility statement with a way to report problems (regulation 8). A broken or undated consultation page is worth reporting through it.

Sources: [Northern Ireland Act 1998, Schedule 9](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1998/47/schedule/9) · [Local Government Act (NI) 2014, Part 12](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/nia/2014/8/part/12) · [Planning Act (NI) 2011, section 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/nia/2011/25/section/4) · [Freedom of Information Act 2000, section 19](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2000/36/section/19) · [Accessibility Regulations 2018, regulation 8](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2018/952/regulation/8) · [Mid and East Antrim Equality Scheme, chapter 3](https://www.midandeastantrim.gov.uk/council/equality-good-relations/equality-scheme-with-equality-action-plan-2023-2027/equality-scheme-with-equality-action-plan-2023-2027-chapter-3)

## What good looks like

Every council could do these at little or no cost:

1. **One page listing every consultation**, open and closed, with full opening and closing dates.
2. **An outcome report linked from the consultation page**, published within twelve weeks of closing, saying what was heard and what changed.
3. **No dead links.** When a consultation platform changes, old addresses should redirect.
4. **Policy consultations kept apart from surveys and event forms**, so the ones that shape decisions are easy to find.

Better still would be **a single Northern Ireland consultation register** covering all councils and departments, as GOV.UK does for Whitehall. Executive departments already use the NI Direct Citizen Space platform; councils could join it.

## Method

I searched each body's own consultation pages, the NI Direct Citizen Space portals and GOV.UK, and checked each consultation against its own page where it would load. Of 385 records, 296 have dates confirmed on the body's own page; the rest rest on listings, council papers or press reports and are marked as such in the dashboard. Department for Infrastructure traffic orders are included only when open or when they remove pedestrian space. Planning applications are not included; they are published on the [NI Planning Portal](https://planningregister.planningsystemni.gov.uk/), and Mid Ulster has [its own](https://www.midulstercouncil.org/planning). The full data can be downloaded from the dashboard.
