> **This is a fictional sample.** "Example Agri-Engineering Ltd" does not exist. The figures use HMRC's illustrative 2027 rates and published default values as at 11 November 2026, and they will change when HMRC publishes live rates. A real review is based on your own trade data. This is not legal or tax advice.

## The firm

Example Agri-Engineering Ltd is a fictional County Antrim firm with 45 staff. It makes galvanised steel gates and livestock equipment, and runs a farm-supplies branch.

- It buys about **500 tonnes of hot-rolled steel coil** a year from a mill in Turkey, shipped into Belfast.
- Its farm-supplies branch buys about **1,000 tonnes of calcium ammonium nitrate (27% N)** a year from a merchant in the Republic of Ireland. The fertiliser is made in the Netherlands and delivered by lorry.
- It sells gates to farm merchants in Northern Ireland, Great Britain and the Republic of Ireland.

## 1. Who is the importer?

- **Steel coil:** the firm is named on the customs declaration, so it is the UK CBAM importer.
- **Fertiliser:** no customs declaration is made when it crosses from Ireland. Finance Act 2026 section 144(5)(a) still treats it as an import into the UK, and the importer is the person on whose behalf it comes in, which here is the firm. HMRC has not yet published guidance on this case, so this point goes to a customs adviser to confirm.

## 2. What applies, and from when

| | UK CBAM | EU CBAM |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Steel coil from Turkey | Applies from 1 Jan 2027 | Not relevant |
| Fertiliser from the Netherlands, via Ireland | Applies from 1 Jan 2027 (see point 1) | Not relevant |
| Gates sold to Irish merchants | Not relevant | Not subject to CBAM: made in NI (Commission Q&A, Q 7.14) |

The two import lines together are well over £50,000 a year, so the firm must register by 31 January 2028. The first return and payment for 2027 are due by 31 May 2028. Records must be kept from 1 January 2027.

## 3. Roughly what it could cost in 2027

| Line | Calculation | Estimate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Steel coil (CN 7208) | 500 t × 2.722 tCO2e/t (default, including 10% mark-up) × £9.69 | £13,188 |
| CAN fertiliser (CN 3102 40 10) | 1,000 t × 2.120 tCO2e/t (default, no mark-up) × £1.45 | £3,074 |
| **Total** | | **about £16,260** |

## 4. Default or actual?

- **Steel.** If the Turkish mill's verified emissions were 20% below the default, the saving would be about £2,638. That is probably less than the cost of verification, so the recommendation is to use default values for 2027 unless the mill already holds verified data for its EU customers that can be reused.
- **Fertiliser.** The Dutch producer pays the EU ETS price, so carbon price relief is possible in principle. It needs a verifier-completed form from the producing plant, and free allowances count as zero price. On a liability of about £3,000, chasing that form is likely to cost more than it saves. The recommendation is to ask the merchant once, and not to pursue it further.

## 5. The EU side

The firm's gates are made in Northern Ireland, so its Irish customers make no CBAM declaration on them. If a customer sends a CBAM questionnaire, reply with an NI CBAM status letter. Give a carbon figure only if the customer wants one for its own reporting.

## 6. 12-month plan (extract)

| When | Action |
| --- | --- |
| November 2026 | Confirm the commodity codes on recent invoices; ask a customs adviser to confirm the importer for the fertiliser line |
| December 2026 | Update the estimate when HMRC publishes the Q1 2027 rates; ask the steel mill whether it has verified emissions data |
| January 2027 | Start CBAM records for every consignment: code, value, origin, net mass |
| After 20 November 2026 | Check whether any UK–EU agreement changes the fertiliser line. EU-made goods could be exempted later, but only once both sides have passed their own legal acts. |
| April–May 2028 | Register (by 31 January 2028), then prepare and submit the first return by 31 May 2028 |
